Deep-stack tournament preflop: why small suited connectors beat middling ones

Upswing's coach walks through a 200bb solver output. Early in a tournament he argues the best approach sits between wild and ultra-tight. He thinks chip accumulation before the bubble has more upside now, because the field tightens early with premature ICM adjustments. Offsuit hands like ATo fold from the low jack while K5s opens, because deep stacks reward hands that make big flushes and cover many boards. Facing a raise, the cutoff 3-bets to about 9bb, and kings mix flats, which they never do at 100bb.

  • Small suited connectors (54s, 65s) outperform 98s when defending against a 3-bet. Middling connectors are dominated and often make the dummy end of a straight.
  • Big blinds over-call too wide with offsuit hands like A6o and K6o. The solver folds many of them, and pot odds are not a reason to call.
  • Big blind 3-bet bluffs: suited 9x and 8x sit one pip below the opener's weakest offsuit opens. 3-bet large (about 15bb) and polar to force indifference.
  • Aces should always 3-bet deep. Slow-playing aces only starts to be reasonable below about 25bb.

Claim: tongue fat from insulin resistance helps drive sleep apnea, even in lean people

DeLauer argues that ectopic fat, stored outside the safe subcutaneous layer, can end up in the tongue and throat, and a study in the journal Sleep found tongue fat is elevated in people with apnea. He cites a trial in Obesity where fasting with protein pacing cut visceral fat 33% in 8 weeks versus 14% for ordinary calorie restriction at equal calories. For sleep, he suggests 200 to 400 mg of theanine and avoiding alcohol and sedatives, which relax the airway. The video opens with a supplement ad. The 'sauna before bed' and theanine advice come with little cited evidence.

  • Short HIIT plus resistance training is recommended to improve insulin sensitivity.
  • Hot baths or sauna a couple of hours before bed to aid deep sleep.
  • Avoid alcohol and NyQuil-type sedatives if you have apnea.

Exercise scientist on a huge young bodybuilder: lean body fat is what keeps steroid users healthy

RP's coach reviews a viral bodybuilder who wears a CPAP mask to sleep. He says the mask makes sense because heavier people develop sleep apnea, but wearing it in the gym is for show. He lists three ways to be healthy on large steroid doses: good genetics, intelligent drug and nutrition management, and, probably the biggest, staying lean. He criticises starting steroids at 19. On training, he says a deeper stretch on presses grows more muscle, and he recommends lengthened partials after failure.

  • Muscles trained under tension at a stretch grow more than those trained under tension without a stretch, so lower the bar to the chest where possible.
  • Add 10 to 15 partials after a short pause once you reach failure.

Dietary fat matters more for hypertrophy than carbs, but total mix is flexible

RP's coaches say dietary fat is more essential than carbohydrate, and that too little fat has a downside that too few carbs do not. Natural lifters often need more fat than they assume for testosterone. Overall evidence suggests that with enough protein and a slight calorie surplus, hypertrophy is roughly maximised whether the diet is high-carb or high-fat.

Phil Galfond: pick projects by energy and congruence, and watch the dopamine

In the final episode of Seeking Balance, Galfond says he now coaches and runs side projects built to keep running without his full attention. He had previously started businesses assuming 20 hours a week, then lost interest within six months. His filter is 'intentional congruence': work that feeds the skills he wants to grow, so he charges double for poker coaching. Small-group and one-on-one coaching energizes him, and he cut back AI building after noticing how much dopamine it was giving him.

  • Design side projects so they survive without your full attention.
  • Cut first the activities that are least congruent with your main skill.
  • Fast build-and-see loops like AI tinkering can feel productive while draining energy.

Daily Stoic reading · Returning to composure quickly

IX. Whensoever by some present hard occurrences thou art constrained to be in some sort troubled and vexed, return unto thyself as soon as may be, and be not out of tune longer than thou must needs. For so shalt thou be the better able to keep thy part another time, and to maintain the harmony, if thou dost use thyself to this continually; once out, presently to have recourse unto it, and to begin again.

— Marcus Aurelius, Meditations (tr. George Long)

When a hard moment knocks you off balance, the aim is not to never be shaken but to notice it, come back to your centre as soon as you can, and not stay rattled longer than the situation forces you to. The more often you practise this return, the easier it becomes to recover your footing next time. For a poker player, this means after a bad beat or a tilt-inducing hand, the discipline is to reset quickly, check your process, and get back to playing your standard strategy rather than chasing losses.

Mental model · Math/Statistics

Bayesian Updating

New evidence should shift your beliefs proportional to how surprising it is. Most people either anchor too hard on priors or overreact to recent news. The discipline: state your prior, see the evidence, update — but by how much the evidence would change things, not how much it feels like it should.
