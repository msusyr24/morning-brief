Ben Felix

"Save as much as possible when you're young" is wrong, or at least seriously incomplete — this is the core argument of this video. The life cycle hypothesis says you should smooth consumption across your lifespan, meaning sacrificing heavily in your low-income 20s to give money to your wealthier future self is economically irrational; the marginal utility of $5K at 25 (safer housing, better food, formative experiences) vastly exceeds its compounded value at 45. He also methodically dismantles: dividend investing (dividends don't add returns, they reclassify capital as income while reducing stock price one-for-one); the idea that economic growth predicts stock returns (historically countries with the highest growth produce slightly *lower* returns); and the Buffett-as-stock-picker narrative (Buffett didn't beat a Vanguard US index ETF for 20+ years before retiring, and said himself maybe 10-so people in his lifetime could legitimately outperform). Index funds, he shows, don't deliver "average" returns — they deliver top-quartile returns, and 0% of top-quartile active funds remain top-quartile five years later.

🧬 Health & Science

Saladino on Synthetic Fragrances & Thyroid / Endocrine Disruption

The specific claim: phthalates and other endocrine-disrupting chemicals in synthetic perfumes absorb readily through thin neck skin — which overlies the thyroid directly — and enter systemic circulation affecting thyroid function, testosterone, and estrogen pathways. The broader argument is that the aggregate burden of synthetic fragrances, PFAS in personal care products, and similar chemical exposures is a meaningful contributor to declining fertility rates and rising IVF use. This is consistent with existing literature on phthalates as endocrine disruptors (they're measurably associated with lower testosterone and sperm quality in epidemiological studies), though Saladino's causal confidence exceeds what the data strictly support.

Dr. Sara Gottfried: Hormone Testing, Magnesium, and the PCOS Lifecycle

Specific and actionable: Gottfried recommends dried urine testing over blood panels for women's hormones because it captures metabolomics (how estrogen is being cleared, not just its level) — blood is a 30-second snapshot. She flags that some estrogen metabolites are protective while others she calls "Homer Simpson metabolites" — disruptive and problematic — and that magnesium is the key micronutrient for clearing estrogen, with ~70-80% of Americans deficient. On PCOS: she argues it's routinely under-diagnosed because it "flies below the radar" until pregnancy is attempted, but it's a major cardiometabolic risk factor post-menopause that deserves management across the entire female lifespan. Testosterone in women can begin declining as early as 28 under chronic stress, roughly 1% per year; she targets the top half of the normal reference range as optimal.

Going Dark, and the Era of Law Enforcement Hacking

Cryptographer Matthew Green on the collision between end-to-end encryption becoming standard and law enforcement losing traditional wiretap access — and how governments are responding by building or buying hacking capabilities instead. The argument: we are entering a regime where the alternative to backdoored encryption is nation-state-grade malware deployed against ordinary criminal suspects, which has its own serious rule-of-law implications. Worth sitting with if you think about privacy, surveillance, and the actual tradeoffs at stake.

Donald Robertson on the Stoic technique of cognitive defusion — treating anxious thoughts as objects to observe rather than truths to inhabit. Firefox Is Now the Last Major Browser That Still Supports uBlock Origin

Chrome, Edge, and Brave have all moved to Manifest V3, which effectively breaks uBlock Origin's ability to block ads at the network request level — the thing that makes it actually effective. Firefox remains on the older API. This has real practical implications: if you're not on Firefox, your ad/tracker blocking just got significantly weaker, with downstream effects on page load speed, privacy, and the economics of the web.

The Ancient Contrast Between Otium and Negotium

Pigliucci on Seneca's distinction between otium (genuine leisure — contemplation, study, philosophy) and negotium (busy-ness, obligation, the grind). The Stoic argument isn't to flee the world but to reclaim interior space within it — and to recognize that most of what we call "rest" is actually just passive negotium. Worth 10 minutes.

🤖 AI Capability Watch

Google: Private AI with Homomorphic Encryption

Homomorphic encryption (HE) allows computation on encrypted data without ever decrypting it — meaning an AI model could run inference on your data without seeing it in plaintext. Google is claiming they've made this practical enough for real AI workloads, which has been the longstanding hard problem (HE is typically 1000x+ slower than standard computation). If the performance claims hold, this is a genuine capability unlock: AI services that cannot leak your data even in principle, enabling use cases in healthcare, finance, and legal that are currently blocked by privacy requirements.

GLM-5.3: Frontier Coding with Emergent Cyber Capabilities

GLM-5.3 is a new frontier coding model from Zhipu AI (China) that the lab claims has developed emergent offensive cybersecurity capabilities — meaning it can find and exploit vulnerabilities without being explicitly trained to do so. The "emergent cyber capabilities" framing is significant: if accurate, this is one of the first public model releases where a lab is proactively disclosing dual-use capability at launch rather than discovering it post-deployment. Warrants watching for what this signals about capability thresholds and disclosure norms.
