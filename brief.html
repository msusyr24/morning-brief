🧬 Health & Science

Uric Acid as an Upstream Driver of Insulin Resistance

Thomas DeLauer's video synthesizes a growing body of research on a mechanism most metabolic panels miss entirely: elevated uric acid drives insulin resistance through two distinct pathways — (1) suppression of nitric oxide, which is required for insulin-mediated vasodilation and glucose delivery to skeletal muscle, and (2) mitochondrial oxidative stress that gums up the GLUT4 signaling cascade. The fructose link is mechanistic: every time the liver processes fructose (including from HFCS, juice, and excess fruit in caloric surplus), it generates uric acid as a byproduct — meaning fructose causes insulin resistance partly independent of its caloric content. Practical interventions with actual human trial support: tart cherry concentrate (1 tbsp, 60-120 minutes before bed — quercetin inhibits xanthine oxidase, the enzyme that produces uric acid; also provides dietary melatonin); capers as the most concentrated whole-food quercetin source; whole-food vitamin C competing at the renal uric acid transporter to increase clearance; berberine activating AMPK while also suppressing hepatic fructose/fat processing that generates uric acid upstream.

Xenoestrogens, Testosterone Resistance, and the Measurement Problem

Lipid scientist Anthony Jay (via Thomas DeLauer) distinguishes between microplastic particles and the chemicals within plastics that act as promiscuous estrogen receptor agonists — BPA, phthalates, atrazine (the second most-used herbicide in North America, banned in Europe). The mechanism for men is a three-part problem: lower total testosterone, lower free testosterone, and direct blockade of the androgen receptor. The critical methodological point: most US studies showing "soy doesn't lower testosterone" fail because there's no clean control group — everyone is already saturated with synthetic estrogens, so the delta between groups disappears. European studies do find effects because tighter regulation of synthetic estrogens creates a larger baseline delta to measure against. Jay also notes testosterone resistance as a parallel to insulin resistance — the signal is present but the receptor is blocked — and that women's testosterone is chronically under-tested despite being important for energy, bone density, and cognitive function.

Mouthwash, Oral Microbiome, and Blood Pressure

Ken Berry's short on antibacterial mouthwash cites the oral nitrate→nitrite→nitric oxide pathway: oral bacteria convert dietary nitrates to nitrites, which the body converts to NO for vasodilation. Killing those bacteria with antibacterial mouthwash has been shown in studies to raise blood pressure by 5-20 points. The mechanism is the same as the one DeLauer flags for uric acid — NO suppression stiffening the vascular system. The practical implication is that unless a dentist has prescribed antibacterial mouthwash for a specific condition, using it routinely may be actively harming cardiovascular health.

Untethered AI Agents in Delaware?

Delaware is considering creating a new legal entity class — an "AI Company" (AIC) — where an AI agent, rather than a human, manages day-to-day affairs and can sue, hold property, and incur obligations in the entity's name. This is the first serious legislative proposal to give AI agents legal personhood in a consequential jurisdiction, and Delaware is where most US corporations are incorporated. The downstream implications for liability, contracts, and corporate governance are genuinely novel.
