GTO Wizard

Tom Boss 21 presents a rigorous deep-dive into late registration backed by real tournament data. The core ICM argument: because your probability of finishing 1st doesn't change but your probability of cashing increases when players have already busted, a starting stack is worth 10-20% more when you late-reg with 30-50% of the field remaining. Using WSOP and Triton data (8,000+ bullets), he shows that late entrants cashed ~19% vs ~15% for day-one players — exactly as ICM predicts — and that players consistently made more money on their later bullets. Crucially, variance does not increase with late reg despite common wisdom, meaning your risk-adjusted return actually improves. The "five-bullet rule" emerges from data: ROI craters at bullet five and beyond, likely due to tilt.

Jared Alderman

A tight framework for multi-way spots: before looking at your hand, categorize the board as one that "punishes betting" vs. one that "punishes checking." On an A72 board where you're last to act on the button with a condensed range, mistakes from over-checking are smaller than mistakes from over-betting — so be aggressive. On a connected board like 876 where players should be leading more than they do, checking will be exploited more than betting will — so require strong incentives to bet. The practical upshot: board texture should determine your margin for error before hand selection does.

🧬 Health & Science

Semaglutide linked to lower predicted dementia risk

A new study in Alzheimer's & Dementia found that semaglutide (the active ingredient in Ozempic/Wegovy) is associated with meaningfully lower predicted dementia risk. The mechanistic plausibility is high: GLP-1 receptors are expressed in the brain, and neuroinflammation and insulin resistance are both implicated in Alzheimer's pathology. This extends the GLP-1 benefit profile beyond obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease into neurodegenerative disease — consistent with what Peter Attia has described as a potentially broad mechanism drug.

Abdominal fat predicts heart disease risk better than BMI

New data from the American College of Cardiology confirms what metabolic researchers have argued for years: waist circumference and visceral fat are significantly better predictors of cardiovascular events than BMI. BMI conflates lean mass with fat mass and says nothing about fat distribution; visceral adiposity drives the inflammatory and metabolic pathways that actually cause atherosclerosis. Practically, this means a "normal BMI" person with a large waist is at higher risk than metrics suggest, and tracking waist-to-height ratio or DEXA body composition is more actionable than scale weight.

American College of Cardiology

Multifactorial trials: taking apart the kitchen sink

Attia dissects what multifactorial trials (studies testing multiple interventions simultaneously) can and cannot prove. The core epistemological problem: when five interventions are bundled, a positive result tells you the bundle works but not which component is responsible; a null result may mask a single powerful intervention being diluted by neutral or harmful co-interventions. This is methodologically important for interpreting landmark trials like PREDIMED or polypill studies, where the bundled nature of the intervention makes causation attribution genuinely impossible.

Peter Attia MD

At-home test for infected ticks could improve Lyme Disease diagnosis

The first at-home test that lets you test a tick itself for Borrelia infection has been developed, rather than waiting for symptoms and then testing the patient. This matters because current Lyme diagnosis relies on antibody tests that don't become positive for weeks after infection — by which time early-treatment windows have closed. A tick-level test that returns a result in hours could enable immediate prophylactic antibiotic use and dramatically reduce late-stage Lyme cases.

💡 Worth Reading

Forget the good life, try the good day

Massimo Pigliucci (Figs in Winter) argues that Stoic philosophy offers a corrective to the modern obsession with life-as-narrative: the only temporal unit we actually control is the day, and eudaimonia is better understood as a practice enacted daily than a destination reached eventually. This has real practical implications — it reframes failure not as a derailment of a life plan but as a single day's data, and it removes the deferral trap of "I'll be happy when..."

Figs in Winter (Pigliucci)

The Stoic Taxonomy of Anger

Donald Robertson maps how the Stoics classified anger — distinguishing between the initial involuntary "proto-passion" (propatheia) and the full emotion that requires assent to a judgment. The practical upshot is that the Stoics weren't advising suppression of the initial flinch, which is automatic and unavoidable, but rather the conscious refusal to amplify it into a full-blown passion through false beliefs about harm or injustice. This distinction is clinically useful for anyone working on emotional regulation.
