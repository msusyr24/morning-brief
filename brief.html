🧬 Health & Science

Myostatin Inhibitors: Larger but Weaker Muscles

Peter Attia's piece covers an important distinction for anyone following muscle preservation research: drugs targeting myostatin (a protein that limits muscle growth) can increase lean mass measurably, but multiple trials show this mass does not translate to proportional strength gains, and in some cases strength is actually impaired. The mechanism appears to involve fast-twitch fiber hypertrophy without corresponding neuromuscular adaptation — you get bigger fibers that the nervous system hasn't learned to recruit efficiently. This is a meaningful caution for anyone excited about myostatin inhibition as a longevity or performance tool: the biomarker (lean mass) improves without the functional outcome (force production) following.

Peter Attia MD

Fasting Insulin: The Reference Range Problem

Ken Berry and his guest (a UK physician) make a concrete, falsifiable claim: the LabCorp reference range for fasting insulin (0–24.9 μIU/mL) is clinically indefensible — zero would indicate Type 1 diabetes, and a value of 24 would indicate severe hyperinsulinemia by NIH's own published definition. The NIH threshold for hyperinsulinemia is >10; the guest argues optimal is ≤6, with under 10 being acceptable. This matters practically: a patient with fasting insulin of 18 would be told they're "normal" by standard labs while already metabolically compromised. Worth asking for this test specifically if it's not routinely ordered.
