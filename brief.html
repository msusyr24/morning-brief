♠Poker

GTO Wizard Blog

Solver says raise limpers bigger from the blinds, about 10bb from the BB

GTO Wizard's multiway preflop solver, in a 3-handed 100bb game with rake only after the flop, shows the BB raising to the larger 10bb size against a BTN limp, never the 6.5bb size. The SB mixes sizes and raises more often, because the BB still has to act behind. The reasons are that raising turns weak hands that check poorly into good raises, that the BB needs a wide range to play out of position, and that raising folds out dominated hands. If the limper cannot raise and so is capped, even 14bb or a jam is used. Position matters: the usual 'in position, raise 4bb plus 1bb per limper' rule undersizes from the blinds.

  • Heuristic: raise larger when raising limpers from the blinds. 10bb is the BB baseline, and it can go larger against weak, capped limpers.
  • SB should not use the giant sizes, because BB is still behind.
  • Against a capped limper, you don't need to polarize: raise strong hands, plus some suited connectors.

✚Health & Sleep

Peter Attia

Peter Attia on cold therapy: strongest evidence is for soreness, with a possible cost to hypertrophy

Attia reviews cold water immersion (typically 40-60F; 2-3 minutes at the colder end, about 30 minutes at the warmer end) and whole-body cryotherapy (-110 to -160C for about 3 minutes). He says the strongest data is for delayed-onset muscle soreness, and that cold water is subjectively much colder than cryo. The episode also covers mood and depression, brown fat, and possible hypertrophy blunting. He warns that the literature relies on meta-analyses of very heterogeneous small studies. He also explains standardized mean difference, where 0.2 is a small effect, 0.5 moderate and 0.8 large. Excerpts show he starts with a 2022 meta-analysis of 52 studies in regular exercisers. The video is a rebroadcast, and the excerpt cuts off before his conclusions.

Eckhart Tolle: present-moment practice starts with sense perception and reduces thinking

Tolle's exercise has two steps. First, attend to what you see and hear without labeling it, which he says cuts the amount of thinking. ▶ Michael Saylor · Strategy

Saylor: Strategy aims to be the central bank of crypto, funded by Bitcoin-backed preferred stock

Saylor says Strategy's flagship preferred, STRC, pays about 12% daily and is backed by Bitcoin plus cash. He argues the payout is treated as return of capital, which defers tax for 8 to 10 years. He says the company has no margin debt and no liquidation point. Its break-even is Bitcoin appreciating about 2.4% a year, and he says it would need to refinance in about a decade only if Bitcoin fell 16% a year forever. He says STRC credit risk is about 67 basis points, which he calls investment grade, and that it trades about $200M a day. He puts digital credit at about $15B outstanding, with $22B raised this year. He also says Strategy holds about 4% of all Bitcoin, and states his goal of buying all remaining supply and driving the price to $10M.

  • He tracks the 200-week moving average as Bitcoin's 'book value'; he says price is now below it, which he says it has been only about 3-4% of the time historically.
  • He says STRC buckled to the mid-70s in June, then Strategy raised about $8B, bought back billions of it and raised the dividend.
  • Bank lending against Bitcoin ETFs is about $50B financeable so far, which he sees as the driver for price.
  • He calls the AI/IPO capital rotation a near-term headwind but a long-term tailwind.

Daily Stoic reading · Seeking approval from the ignorant

LI. He that knoweth not what the world is, knoweth not where he himself is. And he that knoweth not what the world was made for, cannot possibly know either what are the qualities, or what is the nature of the world. Now he that in either of these is to seek, for what he himself was made is ignorant also. What then dost thou think of that man, who proposeth unto himself, as a matter of great moment, the noise and applause of men, who both where they are, and what they are themselves, are altogether ignorant? Dost thou desire to be commended of that man, who thrice in one hour perchance, doth himself curse himself? Dost thou desire to please him, who pleaseth not himself? or dost thou think that he pleaseth himself, who doth use to repent himself almost of everything that he doth?

— Marcus Aurelius, Meditations (tr. George Long)

In plain terms, you cannot understand your own place or purpose without understanding the world, so someone who does not know either is a poor judge of your worth, and a person who keeps cursing and regretting themselves is no reliable source of praise. For poker, this means you should weigh feedback from people who cannot evaluate your decisions, such as a rail that cheers a lucky river or a friend who only sees the results, and anchor your sense of a good session to whether the decisions matched your process, not to applause or to the reactions of players who are themselves confused about variance.

Mental model · Psychology

Authority-Misinfluence Tendency

We defer to perceived authority, often past the point of sense. Milgram's experiments: subjects delivered 'lethal' shocks because a lab coat said to. Real-world: pilots crashing planes because captains made errors first-officers wouldn't challenge. Defense: evaluate the claim, not the credential.
