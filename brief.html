🧬 Health & Science

Pulse Pressure: The Blood Pressure Metric Your Doctor Isn't Tracking

Both Ekberg and Attia's content this cycle touch on blood pressure, but Ekberg's specific point on pulse pressure is the most actionable and underreported: the difference between systolic and diastolic (not just the absolute numbers) reflects arterial elasticity. A pulse pressure over 50 (e.g., 120/70 — which most people would call excellent) signals arterial stiffness. The mechanism: elastin, formed mostly in utero and the first years of life, is never meaningfully replaced. Inflammation, glycation from high blood glucose, and calcification all degrade it over time. The clinical consequence is left ventricular hypertrophy (the heart enlarges to compensate for resistance), reduced coronary artery blood flow during diastole, and sharper pressure waves that damage the brain and kidneys. Crucially, the 2025 blood pressure guidelines don't mention pulse pressure at all, and insulin resistance — which drives both BP elevation and atherosclerotic plaque — is rarely measured alongside BP in standard care.

Keto vs. Mediterranean: RCT Results (Nick Norwitz)

A 6-month randomized controlled trial (not epidemiology) with equal weight loss across arms found the ketogenic diet group outperformed Mediterranean and low-fat plant-based diets on liver fat reduction, inflammatory markers, blood triglycerides, and insulin resistance scores. The researchers' stated conclusion was that keto has "greater cardiometabolic benefits" in people with obesity. The equal-weight-loss design is the key methodological strength — it isolates macronutrient composition from total caloric deficit.

Fasting Insulin as a 10-Year Predictive Marker (Ken Berry)

Berry's clinical claim from 25,000 patients: a fasting insulin above 15 can predict type 2 diabetes 5-15 years before diagnosis — far earlier than fasting glucose or HbA1c, which typically only flag pre-diabetes 2-3 years out. He extends this to fatty liver, hypertension, and metabolic syndrome — all visible through fasting insulin before clinical diagnosis. For fat loss specifically, he argues insulin needs to drop to roughly 10 or below before stored fat can be meaningfully mobilized, which is why scale weight often doesn't move even when body composition is improving.

Sleep Timing and Growth Hormone (Huberman / Dr. Gina Poe)

The large growth hormone bolus that supports protein synthesis and memory consolidation occurs during the first 90-minute sleep cycle's slow-wave phase — and it is time-locked to your circadian clock, not just to sleep onset. Going to bed 90 minutes later than usual means missing that GH pulse even if you sleep the same total duration, because cells are synchronized to respond to GH at a specific circadian time relative to melatonin onset. Alcohol before bed suppresses REM sleep and stage 2 sleep spindles — the spindles being the specific mechanism by which memories transfer from hippocampus (RAM) to cortex (hard disk). Late-night REM cycles (second half of sleep) are disproportionately important for creativity and schema integration.

How to Decatastrophize the Worst-Case Scenario — Donald Robertson

Robertson unpacks the Stoic technique of premeditatio malorum — deliberately imagining the worst-case scenario not to induce anxiety but to drain it of its catastrophic charge. The core move: once you've genuinely confronted the worst outcome and found you could survive or accept it, the fear of that outcome loses its grip. Practical companion to Marcus Aurelius's repeated exercise of imagining loss before it arrives.
