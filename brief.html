GTO Wizard

A deep-dive into GTO Wizard's preflop ICM solver using a real final-table hand as the case study. The core finding: a player who min-raised QJs from the cutoff then 4-bet jammed over a chip leader's 3-bet to 7.5bb was almost certainly making a significant error — even when the chip leader is modeled as a "bully" with a +5% raise frequency. The solver shows cutoff's best response to an aggressive 3-bettor is actually to tighten opening range and shift toward open-jamming (avoiding the raise-fold-or-jam problem entirely) rather than min-raising with the intent to snap off. Node-locking experiments showed that even giving the cutoff a 25% calling range against the 3-bet doesn't prompt the small blind to prefer a 7.5bb size — it shifts them toward outright shoving instead. A clear example of why ICM dramatically narrows the range of correct actions vs. chip-EV play.

GTO Lab

A crisp conceptual distinction between equity advantage and nuts advantage as the two components of range advantage. The key takeaway: equity advantage tells you who should be doing the betting, but nuts advantage is what licenses large bets. If your range is ahead in equity but lacks nut hands, you can bet — but you generally cannot size up. Both players betting occurs when equity is close to even and nuts advantage is also split. Simple but foundational for sizing decisions.

🧬 Health & Science

The Lab Reference Range Problem (Chaffee)

Worth flagging as a standalone point: Chaffee documented that four major labs in a single city (Perth) give four completely different reference ranges for B12 — one sets deficiency below 140 pmol/L, another below 330. A patient with B12 of 250 is "fine" at three labs and "deficient" at the fourth. His argument, backed by some literature, is that optimal B12 is 800–1,200 pmol/L, which sits above the upper limit of most lab ranges entirely. The practical implication: treating "within normal range" as a health target is a category error when the ranges themselves are averages of a population that may be systematically deficient.

Avocado's Non-Fat Mechanisms (DeLauer)

Three specific mechanisms beyond "healthy fats": (1) Carotenoid absorption — oleic acid is particularly effective at packaging fat-soluble carotenoids into micelles; the same salad with avocado vs. fat-free dressing produced 15.3x more beta-carotene absorption in a Journal of Nutrition crossover trial. (2) Manoheptulose — a rare sugar in avocado that inhibits hepatic glucokinase, slowing liver glucose processing independently of fat and fiber content; a Journal of Nutrients dose-response study found both half and whole avocado produced significantly lower 6-hour glucose and insulin vs. a calorie-matched carb meal. (3) Gut microbiome — the 163-person, 12-week RCT found increases in Faecalibacterium, Lachnospira, and Alistipes, bacteria associated with anti-inflammatory effects and butyrate production, with downstream improvement in insulin sensitivity markers.

Thomas DeLauer

The 3am Wake Mechanism (Ekberg)

A mechanistically clean explanation worth having: the natural cortisol nadir is 1–3am, after which cortisol begins rising as part of the dawn phenomenon. Deep sleep also shifts to lighter stages around 3am. If your baseline cortisol is already elevated (chronic stress, racing mind) or you've created a blood glucose instability (late high-carb dinner, alcohol), the cortisol uptick at 3am crosses a threshold and tips you into wakefulness. Alcohol specifically impairs gluconeogenesis by occupying the liver, removing the buffer against blood glucose drops, AND its breakdown product acetaldehyde directly stimulates the sympathetic nervous system. The fix for blood sugar-driven waking is earlier/lighter dinners with more fat and protein; the fix for stress-driven waking involves training the parasympathetic system through daily slow-breath practice (4-5s in, 5-6s out), which he frames as literal neuroplastic strengthening of parasympathetic nuclei.
