Nick Norwitz

Two tightly connected videos on sleep quality over quantity. The core argument: the goal isn't 8 hours, it's maximizing deep sleep, when the brain's glymphatic system activates — blood vessels constrict, opening channels through which cerebrospinal fluid flushes metabolic waste including Alzheimer's-linked proteins. Specific tactics he recommends: magnesium threonate (1–2g, 30–60 min before bed), developed at MIT specifically to cross the blood-brain barrier more efficiently than other magnesium forms; side sleeping, supported by animal MRI data showing better glymphatic clearance; consistent circadian rhythm and morning sunlight. He also mentions a fascinating new paper showing optogenetic techniques can mimic deep sleep in mice at the synaptic level without actual sleep — raising the long-term question of whether sleep is even necessary. He's transparent that he's in the top 2% genetic risk for Alzheimer's, which drives his obsession, and deliberately avoids overusing his Oura ring because quantifying imperfect sleep triggered anxiety loops.

GTO Wizard

Tom Boss 21 presents a rigorous deep-dive on late registration in MTTs backed by real Triton data (8,000+ bullets). The theoretical case: when you late-reg, your win-equity for 1st place stays the same as your chip proportion, but your probability of cashing increases because players have already busted — ICM shows this as roughly a 10–15% boost in stack value at the 40–50% field-remaining mark. The empirical case is even stronger: Triton data shows day-one entrants lost ~9.7% of buy-in on average, while late entrants cashed at 19% vs. the expected 15%, exactly matching ICM prediction. Controlling for player skill (comparing each player's own early bullets vs. late bullets), late entries outperformed by 10–13%. Counterintuitive finding: variance doesn't increase with late reg — it stays roughly flat — meaning risk-adjusted return actually improves, so you can shot-take bigger fields on a smaller bankroll. He also introduces a "5-bullet rule": after busting four times, don't fire a fifth bullet — tilt-driven fifth bullets show ~40% ROI collapse in the data.

Video: The Truth About Late Registration

Jared Alderman

Two sharp poker theory clips. In the multiway spot video, he proposes a pre-hand categorization heuristic: before looking at your cards, classify the board as one that punishes betting vs. one that punishes checking. On A72 in position, your range is tight and condensed toward Ax, so the hands that shouldn't bet won't cost you much if they do — checking is punishable, so be liberal betting. On 876, out-of-position players likely underfold leads, so your betting range gets worse when they don't lead enough — punishing a bet, meaning you need strong incentives to fire. Separately, he makes a sharp meta-point: give your opponents far less credit than you instinctively do. Humans are bad at poker, and "respecting" a line that an opponent has never actually executed correctly (e.g., the nut flush draw checked twice then river check-raised) is a massive leak. Disrespect earns win rate.

Renaissance Periodization (Dr. Mike)

Several substantive clips. On testosterone: the popular "T levels have fallen 30–50%" narrative is real but overstated. The Massachusetts Male Aging Study found a meaningful population-level decline beyond what aging alone explained; the Israeli dataset (100,000+ men, 2006–2019) confirmed it. But a 2026 NHANES analysis (10,000 men, 2011–2023) found the fraction with testosterone below 300 ng/dL actually fell from 29.3% to 22.4% — a major reversal that survived age standardization. Obesity rose during this period (from 35% to 39%), so obesity alone can't explain the recovery. The honest answer, per Dr. Mike: we don't know why it reversed, and population averages tell you nothing about your individual situation. Key individual risk factors: obesity (2.7x odds of sub-300), insulin resistance (1.55x), diabetes-range glucose (nearly 3x), and 8+ sedentary hours per day. On supplements: top five are protein powder, creatine monohydrate (5g/day, consistency matters more than timing), EPA/DHA omega-3s (1g/day, check the back label for actual EPA+DHA), vitamin D3 (correct a deficiency, more isn't better), and psyllium husk fiber. On sleep debt: you cannot catch up — recovery sleep restores function but doesn't un-impair the days you were deficit. On the "calories don't matter" claim: categorically wrong for fat loss, with the sole narrow exception of true body recomposition in beginners.

Thomas DeLauer

Three videos with specific mechanistic claims worth noting. On rice and glucose: cooking rice with a tablespoon of olive oil causes amylose to wrap around fat chains and form V-type amylose-lipid crystalline complexes — a distinct structure from resistant starch that resists enzymatic digestion; this is the same mechanism behind RD34/RD43 rice varietals bred for lower glycemic response. Adding acid (rice vinegar or lemon) separately inhibits amylase enzymes. Cooling and reheating after that adds retrograded resistant starch on top — roughly 2.5x more resistant starch than freshly cooked rice. A 2-minute wall sit after eating activates AMPK in large leg muscles, driving GLUT4 glucose transporters to the surface without insulin — the same mechanism as walking but with sustained isometric tension that data suggest may outperform walking for acute glucose clearance, with a meta-analysis showing isometric work reduces blood pressure comparably to medication. On stress and fat: a Biological Psychiatry study found one daily intense stress event reduces resting energy expenditure by ~104 kcal/day — projecting to ~11 lbs/year of fat gain — and a Translational Psychiatry epigenetic clock study (GrimAge) showed the same stressor pattern accelerates DNA aging. Crucially, the moderating variable was psychological resilience and emotional regulation, not stress avoidance. On beta-glucan fiber: a European Journal of Clinical Nutrition RCT found 5g/day of beta-glucan for 5 weeks lowered postprandial glucose, improved insulin resistance, and dropped cholesterol 7.4%; a Nutrients RCT at 3g/day showed 15% LDL reduction by trapping bile acids; Frontiers in Microbiology found 3g shifted the Firmicutes:Bacteroidetes ratio toward bacteria associated with lower body fat and waist circumference.

🧬 Health & Science

Avocado Oil Fraud: UC Davis Study (2026)

A University of California Davis study published in Applied Food Research (2026) tested major "avocado oil" consumer brands using sterol and fatty acid profiling — a rigorous method since oil has no DNA to test. Every tested product from Chosen Foods (mayo, vegan mayo, lemon garlic dressing, Italian dressing), Primal Kitchen (mayo, avocado oil vinaigrette, Italian dressing), and Sir Kensington's failed on both test occasions. The likely adulterant based on the signature: safflower/sunflower/vegetable oil blends. This follows a similar 2020 study, meaning brands were already aware this could be tested. The mechanism of fraud is probably supply chain: outsourced manufacturers share equipment with cheaper product lines. Practical implication: the "clean ingredient" premium-priced mayo market is largely fraudulent, and making your own with verified fats (butter, tallow) is the only reliable option.

Via Ken Berry MD · Monday Night Live

Glymphatic System and Deep Sleep Optimization

Nick Norwitz synthesizes the current mechanistic picture: during deep sleep, reduced brain energy demands allow blood vessels to constrict, opening perivascular channels through which CSF flushes metabolic waste — including amyloid-beta and tau proteins linked to Alzheimer's. Impaired glymphatic flow is a known feature of Alzheimer's disease progression. The practical protocol he cites with RCT support: magnesium threonate 1–2g 30–60 min before bed (the only magnesium form shown to reliably cross the blood-brain barrier, developed at MIT specifically for this; a double-blind RCT showed improvements in deep sleep scores on objective trackers plus reduced "grouchiness"). Side sleeping appears to improve glymphatic efficiency in animals (2015 MRI study with rodents tested in multiple positions); the fact that side sleeping is the most common human sleep position may not be coincidental. He also flags a new paper showing optogenetic brain-region-turn-taking can mimic deep sleep effects in mice — early evidence that sleep's functions might eventually be chemically or neurologically substitutable.

Via Nick Norwitz · How Much Sleep Do You Really Need?

Stress, Metabolism, and Epigenetic Aging

Thomas DeLauer cites two studies worth knowing. First, a Biological Psychiatry study: subjects exposed to one prior-day stressor showed 104 kcal/day lower resting energy expenditure, higher insulin, and reduced fat oxidation over 6 hours — translating to roughly 11 lbs/year of additional fat if chronic, independent of diet. The effect held regardless of whether subjects ate a high-saturated-fat or high-oleic-acid meal. Second, a Translational Psychiatry study using the GrimAge epigenetic clock found cumulative stress significantly accelerated biological aging — and the moderating variable was psychological resilience (emotional regulation, not stress avoidance). This connects to the zone-2 cardio data: a Journal of Obesity 12-week RCT found continuous aerobic exercise produced near-significant 3.1% reduction in visceral fat while HIIT actually increased it in the stressed group, likely because high-intensity training adds cortisol load in already-stressed individuals.

BPC-157: No Human Evidence After Decades

Peter Attia makes a crisp methodological argument: BPC-157's claimed indications have expanded continuously (wound healing → tendons → ligaments → muscle → gut → inflammation → MS) while the original wound-healing claim was never rigorously established in humans. Compare to GLP-1 agonists, which earned each indication sequentially through rigorous trials.
