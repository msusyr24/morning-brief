Five Whys

Toyota's technique: when a problem surfaces, ask 'why?' five times. Each answer gets you closer to root cause. The first answer is always a symptom, not a cause. Munger uses this in business analysis: 'Why is this company losing money?' → five layers deep, you usually find a cultural or strategic issue, not an operational one.

Nick Norwitz

Norwitz had a rich week across several topics. On LDL/cardiovascular risk, he and Ken Berry dissected a new 293,000-person study from a prominent lipidologist showing that when HDL is high, the correlation between apoB and new-onset cardiovascular disease "vanishes" — the paper literally uses the word "immunity" for certain individuals with high LDL who appear not to develop heart disease. Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer covered urolithin A, a postbiotic produced when gut bacteria metabolize ellagitannins from pomegranate. The mechanism: urolithin A activates the PINK1/Parkin pathway (mitophagy — the cellular cleanup of dysfunctional mitochondria), which degrades with age and is the central driver of sarcopenia per a key Int'l Journal of Biochemistry review. A 4-month RCT (66 participants, 1,000mg/day) found significantly more muscle contractions before fatigue in both hand and leg groups, plus drops in acylcarnitines, ceramides, and CRP — all markers of improved mitochondrial function and reduced inflammation. The critical practical finding: 88% of people have zero detectable urolithin A in blood, and even after pomegranate juice, only ~40% reach meaningful levels. Direct supplementation (500mg) got 100% of participants to meaningful levels at 6× higher peak than pomegranate juice. He stacks three independent mitophagy pathways: urolithin A (PINK1/Parkin), exercise (AMPK/ULK1, peaking ~6 hours post-session), and fasting (mTOR suppression + BNIP3 pathway).

🧬 Health & Science

LDL, ApoB, and HDL Context — New 293,000-Person Study

A study in the European Journal of Preventive Cardiology (first author: prominent lipidologist Allan Sniderman) found that when HDL cholesterol is high, the effect of apoB on new-onset cardiovascular disease "vanishes" in a dataset of ~293,000 people. The paper explicitly states there may be "apparent immunity" of some individuals to the clinical consequences of high LDL/apoB — language that has generated significant debate. The practical implication Norwitz draws: a carnivore/keto patient with LDL of 250-300 but high HDL and low waist-to-hip ratio may be in a category where aggressive statin therapy provides no meaningful benefit — and framing apoB as simply "causal" obscures this context entirely. The causality-vs-importance distinction is the key intellectual move: LDL is part of a causal cascade but is neither sufficient to cause disease alone nor necessarily the most important variable to treat in a metabolically healthy person.

Urolithin A and Mitophagy — RCT Data

A 4-month RCT (JAMA Network Open, n=66, 1,000mg/day urolithin A vs. placebo) found significantly greater muscle endurance (more contractions before fatigue) in both hand and leg muscle groups, with biomarker improvements including acylcarnitine reduction, ceramide decrease, and lower CRP. The mitophagy mechanism (PINK1/Parkin pathway activation) is distinct from exercise-induced mitophagy (AMPK/ULK1) and fasting-induced mitophagy (BNIP3/mTOR suppression), meaning these pathways stack additively. Critical practical finding: 88% of people have undetectable urolithin A in blood without supplementation; even pomegranate juice only brings ~40% to meaningful levels, while direct supplementation achieves it 100% of the time at 6× higher peak concentrations.

Fasting Mimicking Diet — Biological Age Reduction in RCTs

Nick Norwitz summarized the fasting mimicking diet protocol (5 days at ~1,000 kcal, low protein, once/month for 3 months/year = 15 days total) citing randomized controlled trials showing ~2.5 years of biological age reduction on blood markers, improvements in liver fat and insulin resistance, and visceral fat reduction — effects that held independent of weight loss. In animal models, the protocol extends lifespan. The mechanism involves enhanced recovery and cellular renewal pathways (autophagy, metabolic reset) triggered by the protein and caloric restriction without full fasting adherence difficulty.

Nitric Oxide: Practical Levers for Cardiovascular Health

Norwitz's full NO video synthesized several evidence-based levers: (1) eliminate mouthwash — multiple studies show 2–3.5 mmHg blood pressure increase from killing oral nitrate-converting bacteria; (2) nose breathing adds ~4 mmHg BP benefit over mouth breathing; (3) humming increases nasal NO production ~15-fold vs. quiet exhalation via mechanical oscillation of paranasal sinuses; (4) beetroot at ~520mg dietary nitrate produces 12–14% endurance improvements in controlled trials; (5) regular exercise induces durable upregulation of eNOS (endothelial nitric oxide synthase), lowering resting BP by ~3.5–8+ mmHg depending on baseline. Extra virgin olive oil's hydroxytyrosol increases NO bioavailability and inhibits tau protein aggregation in Alzheimer's research — quality markers are harvest date (within 18 months), dark bottle, and throat-kick from oleocanthal.

Bones May Release Proteins That Slow Aging and Extend Lifespan

Emerging research suggests bone tissue actively secretes proteins with systemic anti-aging effects — reframing bone as an endocrine organ rather than passive structural tissue. The finding adds to a growing picture (alongside osteocalcin research) that skeletal loading and bone health have systemic longevity implications beyond fracture risk.

💡 Worth Reading

Mathematics Has Changed. Let's Act Like It — Terry Tao

Fields Medal winner Terence Tao argues that AI solving major open problems (Navier-Stokes, quasi-Riemann Hypothesis, Unique Games Conjecture, and hundreds more in a single OpenAI drop) represents a genuine crisis for the mathematical enterprise — specifically for graduate students and postdocs whose life's work is disrupted by having problems "spoiled" before they can explore them. His concern isn't capability (he acknowledges the results are real) but the destruction of the slow, exploratory research culture that produces mathematical understanding, not just theorem-checking. The Lex Fridman emergency episode with three mathematicians provides the best immediate reaction: the "doorman fallacy" framing (don't reduce a mathematician's job to theorem-proving, just as a doorman's job isn't just holding doors) and the "semigroup vs. convex hull" debate about whether AI is genuinely expanding mathematical knowledge or recombining existing ideas very efficiently.
