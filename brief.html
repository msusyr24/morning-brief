Mental Model · Economics/Business

Principle-Agent Problem

When one party (agent) acts on behalf of another (principal), their incentives rarely perfectly align. Lawyers bill by the hour, not by outcome. CEOs optimize for short-term stock price, not long-term company health. The discipline: in any relationship, ask whose incentives actually match yours.

Peter Attia

AMA sneak peek on metabolic liver health. Attia frames the liver as the "canary in the coal mine" for systemic metabolic dysfunction — not a standalone organ problem. He walks through a four-stage model: metabolic stress → steatosis (fat accumulation) → stash/MASH (inflammation and cell death) → fibrosis (scarring, largely irreversible). Key mechanism: selective hepatic insulin resistance means the liver keeps releasing glucose into an already-high blood sugar environment while simultaneously continuing to produce new fat — driven by the first insulin signal failing before the second. He notes that 38% of the world's adult population has some degree of fatty liver disease, and that the leading cause of death in liver disease patients is cardiovascular disease, not liver failure — because a stressed liver overproduces ApoB particles and amplifies systemic insulin resistance. The liver can hold only ~4.5g of glucose total while a single meal might deliver 90g, making the homeostatic precision required extraordinary.

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer covers a PNAS-cited hypothesis linking trans fats specifically to insulin resistance via a mitochondrial mechanism: trans fats resist breakdown by LCAD (long-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase), causing lipid intermediates to accumulate in cell membranes, triggering oxidative stress, and impairing insulin signaling — independent of the glucose pathway. He also covers the Randle cycle ("metabolic gridlock"): cells can't oxidize fat and carbohydrates simultaneously, so combining high fat + high carbs in a single meal causes fuel accumulation and energy toxicity, especially damaging if someone is already insulin resistant. Practical finding from Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism: replacing carbohydrates with protein in one meal increased fatty acid oxidation for the rest of the day. His takeaway: the worst possible meal is high trans fat + refined carbs in a caloric surplus — all three variables compound the dysfunction.

Nick Norwitz

Norwitz highlights clonal hematopoiesis as an emerging and underappreciated cardiovascular risk factor: mutant immune cells born in the bone marrow travel through the bloodstream into coronary arteries, driving inflammation and increasing heart attack risk. With age, these mutant clones can crowd out healthy cells. The mechanism is distinct from — and potentially additive to — LDL cholesterol. Two free interventions with evidence for slowing this process: exercise and adequate sleep.

🧬 Health & Science

Clonal Hematopoiesis — The Heart Disease Risk Factor You Haven't Heard Of

Nick Norwitz's short highlights clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP): mutant stem cells in the bone marrow proliferate, send inflammatory immune cells into coronary arteries, and drive atherosclerosis through an inflammation pathway entirely separate from LDL cholesterol. With age, these clones can become dominant, crowding out healthy hematopoietic cells. It's described as an "emerging risk factor" with growing research attention. The two interventions with current evidence for slowing it: exercise and sleep — both free, both dose-dependent.

Nick Norwitz

Trans Fats → LCAD Inhibition → Insulin Resistance: A Specific Mechanism

DeLauer covers a biological chemistry study showing that trans fats (unlike stearic acid or oleic acid) resist enzymatic breakdown by LCAD — the first step in beta-oxidation. This causes lipid intermediate accumulation in cell membranes, generates oxidative stress, and directly impairs insulin signaling. Separately, the Randle cycle means the body cannot oxidize fat and carbohydrates simultaneously; combining both in high quantities causes "metabolic gridlock" — fuel accumulates in cells because neither pathway can fully clear. A Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism study found that replacing carbs with protein in a single meal increased fatty acid oxidation for the rest of the day. The practical upshot: ultra-processed foods are particularly harmful because they combine refined carbs with trans/industrial fats — hitting both mechanisms at once.

Thomas DeLauer

Liver as Metabolic HQ — Attia's Four-Stage Disease Model

Attia's AMA preview lays out a precise mechanistic sequence from caloric surplus to liver damage: excess calories → hepatic de novo lipogenesis → fat cell overflow → free fatty acids recirculate → liver becomes insulin resistant. The key pathological moment is selective hepatic insulin resistance: the first insulin signal (stop releasing glucose) fails before the second (stop making fat), so the liver simultaneously dumps glucose into already-high blood sugar while continuing lipogenesis — explaining why dyslipidemia always accompanies metabolic liver disease. Steatosis (stage 2) is largely reversible; fibrosis (stage 4) is not, and it's fibrosis that predicts cardiovascular mortality and cancer. At 38% adult prevalence globally, MASLD (formerly NAFLD) is not a niche condition.
