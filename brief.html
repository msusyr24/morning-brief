Margin of Safety

Ben Graham's principle: buy with enough buffer that you're right even if you're somewhat wrong. Applied to bridge engineering: engineers specify 4x the load the bridge will ever see. Applied to life: leave room for error — in financial reserves, in timing, in relationships. The world is less predictable than we think.

England Set to Be One of the First Countries to Eliminate Hepatitis C

England is on track to eliminate hepatitis C as a public health threat — a direct result of direct-acting antiviral drugs (DAAs) that cure over 95% of cases, combined with a systematic national testing and treatment push. This is a genuine public health milestone: hepatitis C kills ~290,000 people per year globally, and elimination at the national level was considered impractical a decade ago.

💡 Worth Reading

Compression Is Prediction

A conceptually rich piece making the case that compression and prediction are mathematically equivalent — if you can predict the next token/bit well, you can compress well, and vice versa. This is the same insight underlying why LLMs trained on next-token prediction turn out to be such capable general reasoners: they're essentially building world models good enough to compress text. Cleanly written and worth sitting with as a mental model for why scaling works.

ngrok blog · HN [380 pts]

Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs

Researchers found that Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google return encrypted chain-of-thought blocks to API clients — and those blocks can be replayed across sessions, users, and models to extract reasoning traces the companies explicitly don't want exposed. The implication: even "hidden" thinking in frontier models may not be truly private from API-level observation. Surprising finding with real implications for AI safety and competitive secrecy.

The Ancient Contrast Between Otium and Negotium

Massimo Pigliucci explores the Roman distinction between otium (leisure for reflection, philosophy, writing) and negotium (business, civic duties) — arguing that the Stoics didn't see these as opposites but as complementary, and that reclaiming deliberate unstructured time is both philosophically grounded and practically urgent. A good antidote to productivity-culture thinking, with real Seneca and Cicero backing.

How Well Does AI Peer Review Work?

A researcher planted 100 known errors across 10 psychology papers and ran them through frontier models and commercial AI review tools. The best single system caught 71/100 errors; pooling all systems caught 93/100. Models are genuinely catching real methodological mistakes — this is a meaningful capability data point, not a vague claim about AI in science.
