GTO Wizard

Solid exploitative PLO lesson: against loose-passive players, the core mistake is sizing down with strong hands. The key insight is that recreational players don't differentiate between a half-pot bet and a 75% bet — their calling range is essentially fixed regardless of sizing — so you're just giving them better odds for free. The solver confirms this: when you node-lock the opponent to a loose-passive calling frequency, it shifts dramatically toward large sizing with value hands. A secondary finding is that you should also cut bluffing frequency against these players, since they're making mistakes by calling, not by folding — so thin bluffs become dead money. Concretely, the example shows a pot that grows from 33bb to 61bb just by using large sizing on flop and turn instead of small.

Jared Alderman

Good discussion of the biggest gap between online crushers and live pros: online players have a calibrated sense of how large a deviation they're making from theory, while live players often make huge folds or calls based on gut reads without understanding the EV cost. The example hand — folding two pair on the turn to a minraise in a live 1/3 game — was theoretically worth about $50 in EV to call, even granting the opponent has a set a large percentage of the time. Alderman's broader point: live players justify not studying by saying "I have live reads," but that's often a rationalization, and understanding pot odds and frequency is not optional for serious play. He also touches on how the tank-then-click line is actually slightly less set-weighted than an instant click, because the tank suggests the player is uncomfortable with their hand.

Renaissance Periodization

Mike Israetel delivers a quick reality check on muscle gain rates: 5 lbs of muscle in a year is a realistic, solid outcome — not 15. Volume guidelines cited: 2-10 sets per muscle group per session, training a muscle 2-4x per week; higher frequency (up to 4x/week at ~10 sets) can accelerate growth for a few months but isn't sustainable long-term.

🧬 Health & Science

Fermented Foods Beat Fiber in Stanford RCT — Consistently

Nick Norwitz summarized a landmark randomized human study from Stanford comparing high-fiber vs. high-fermented-food dietary interventions. Fiber produced heterogeneous outcomes — inflammatory markers went down in some participants and up in others. Fermented foods produced a consistent positive signal: greater microbiome diversity and lower systemic inflammatory markers across participants. This is notable because fiber has long been the default recommendation for gut health, and this study suggests that signal may be far noisier than assumed. Practical implication: start with one daily serving of sauerkraut, kefir, kimchi, or similar and work up to six.

Whey Protein as a Hepatic Glutathione Precursor (Thomas DeLauer)

DeLauer cites a 12-week RCT (Journal of Medicinal Food) showing whey protein isolate significantly raised glutathione levels in chemotherapy patients versus a maltodextrin control — with the mechanism being whey's high cysteine content, the rate-limiting amino acid in glutathione synthesis. A 2023 Frontiers in Medicine paper found that low hepatic glutathione is a defining feature of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and correlates directly with insulin resistance, not just oxidative stress. The implication is that whey isn't just a muscle protein — 30g+ may have a meaningful liver-protective effect via this pathway, especially in metabolically compromised individuals. He also notes that NAC (600mg 3x daily) showed significant reductions in fasting glucose and CRP in an 8-week RCT.

Type 2 Diabetes in Children: Nearly 10x Increase in Two Decades

CDC data cited by Ken Berry MD shows pediatric type 2 diabetes incidence in ages 10-19 rose from 2.37 per 100,000 in 2002 to 22.3 per 100,000 in 2023 — a ~9.4x increase. More alarming: the rate of acceleration is itself increasing, from 2.9% annually (2002-2013) to 4.7% annually (2014-2023). The clinical significance is that a child developing type 2 diabetes at age 10 has far more time to accumulate end-organ damage than someone diagnosed at 50 — making this a compounding public health catastrophe, not a static one.

Pregnancy as Metabolic and Cardiovascular Stress Test (Peter Attia / Dr. Kathryn Gray)

Plasma volume expands by 50% by 28 weeks of pregnancy, creating a relative anemia (the red cell mass doesn't keep pace) and placing extraordinary demand on cardiac output — women with significant cardiopulmonary disease literally cannot tolerate pregnancy. Insulin resistance increases substantially in the second trimester, which is why pregnancy unmasks latent predispositions to type 2 diabetes and metabolic dysfunction. The GDF-15 gene appears to be a key driver of hyperemesis gravidarum (severe pregnancy nausea), not hCG as was long assumed — a finding that's now opening therapeutic targets. The overall framing: pregnancy functions as a full-body physiologic audit that can expose vulnerabilities that would otherwise remain hidden for decades.

Myostatin Inhibitors: Larger Muscles, Less Strength (Peter Attia)

A Peter Attia piece flags a counterintuitive finding about drugs that block myostatin (a protein that limits muscle growth): they produce measurable increases in lean mass but fail to produce corresponding strength gains — and in some cases may reduce functional strength. The paradox suggests that myostatin-driven hypertrophy may not recapitulate the neuromuscular adaptations that produce actual force output, which has implications for anyone considering these compounds for longevity or performance.

💡 Worth Reading

Reading Greek, Missing the Point — Massimo Pigliucci on Mary Beard, Emily Wilson, and Stoicism

Pigliucci argues that classical scholars — even brilliant ones like Mary Beard and Emily Wilson — tend to know Stoic texts deeply as literature but systematically miss what Stoicism actually is: a practical philosophy of how to live. Knowing the Greek is not the same as having internalized the philosophy. This is a two-part piece, and the core provocation is that expertise in a text can become a barrier to understanding the thing the text is about. Worth sitting with if you engage with primary Stoic sources.

Part II · Figs in Winter (Pigliucci)
