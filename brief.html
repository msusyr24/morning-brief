Mental Model · Economics/Business

Principle-Agent Problem

When one party (agent) acts on behalf of another (principal), their incentives rarely perfectly align. Lawyers bill by the hour, not by outcome. CEOs optimize for short-term stock price, not long-term company health. The discipline: in any relationship, ask whose incentives actually match yours.

Jared Alderman

Jared and high-stakes heads-up specialist Uri Peleg dig into a genuinely underappreciated poker concept: the strategic value of hidden information about your own game tree. Uri's core point is that balance only matters if your opponent can detect and exploit imbalance — meaning you can systematically over-bluff certain boards and under-bluff others, and as long as your global frequencies are in the right ballpark, there's no practical way for opponents to discover this. He illustrates this with a real example: he told a shocked opponent "how can you know if I'm balanced?" when questioned about not RNG-ing. The conversation also covers the chess analogy of "invisible chessboards" — your range as a superposition of possible setups your opponent can't see — and how exploitative play is essentially making an informed guess about how your opponent's board is arranged and countering it specifically.

Videos: "Poker Doesn't Work Without Exploits" - Uri Peleg

GTO Lab

Nikki P, Danny D, and coach Axel walk through GTO Lab's new Mystery Bounty library (240+ sims and counting). The key insight Axel hammers: most players dramatically underestimate how different Mystery Bounty strategy is from regular tournament play — it's not "adjust a little," it's a fundamentally different game, especially when bounties first kick in. The "KO to stack ratio" (average bounty value ÷ current stack) is their central heuristic for calibrating how aggressively to bounty-hunt vs. protect tournament equity. Their tooling shows EV broken down into prize-pool EV (green) vs. KO EV (yellow), so you can see hands that are pure-yellow — only profitable because of the bounty, not the tournament — which makes the decision framework concrete. The most common mistake: players are too accustomed to vanilla tournament ICM and don't deviate nearly enough, leaving literal bounty money on the table.

Videos: Poker Is Soft Again: Mystery Bounty Tools, Live

Peter Attia

Attia gives an honest, uncertain take on rapamycin for longevity. The mechanistic case is strong: mTOR inhibition extends life across all four eukaryotic model organisms (yeast, worms, flies, rodents), but there's zero human data proving it works in people. Even if you grant the mechanism transfers, the dose/frequency/timing questions are completely open — continuous dosing in mice may not map to the intermittent weekly protocols humans are using. He says he genuinely doesn't know whether the once-weekly protocol has a favorable enough benefit-to-risk ratio to justify current use, and is watching Matt Kaeberlein's dog study for better signal on intermittent dosing.

Ken Berry MD (with Dr. Shawn Baker)

Berry and Baker make the case that carnivore is essentially "the floor" for low-carb — lowest possible carbohydrate intake — and that's the primary mechanism for type 2 diabetes remission, with A1C improvements from 12+ down to the 4s or 5s possible within 3 months in some patients (though 6–12 months is common). Baker adds that some keto patients who stall switch to carnivore and improve further, possibly due to gut permeability or dysbiosis resolving without plant fiber. Berry makes a specific point about hyperpalatable carnivore foods: cheese and heavy cream override satiety signals even on an otherwise clean diet — he personally carried an extra 10–15 lbs until removing them, despite them being technically "carnivore." On food addiction, both argue the concept should be reframed as sugar/starch addiction specifically — no one presents with clinical food noise around ribeye or eggs, only sugar-containing foods.

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer runs through several specific self-corrections worth noting. On fish oil: he overdid high-DHA megadosing, found it made cell membranes too fluid (fragile rather than functional), and now recommends ~2g/day in TG form rather than ethyl ester. On creatine: he moved from low-dose "nootropic" microdosing to 15g+/day and reports meaningfully better strength and energy, with initial water retention resolving after ~a month. On fasting: he abandoned random one-off fasts in favor of "burst fasting" — 3–4 consecutive days of 18-hour fasts followed by 4–5 days off — finding his body could find homeostasis within the block rather than being chronically disrupted. He also warns that growth hormone releasing peptides like Ipamorelin can meaningfully raise HbA1c, and he'd now run these only alongside strict carb restriction or keto.

Wearables Can Detect Sudden Cardiac Death Days in Advance

A study in JACC: Case Reports analyzed longitudinal wearable physiological data from patients who subsequently died of sudden cardiac death. The finding: detectable changes in wearable-captured physiology (heart rate, HRV, activity) appeared in the days before the event — raising the possibility of predictive early warning using consumer devices. This is a small but methodologically interesting signal for anyone tracking HRV or resting HR longitudinally.

🤖 AI Capability Watch

A Working NES Emulator Baked Into LLM Weights

Drew Breunig encoded a functional Nintendo Entertainment System 6502 CPU emulator into the weights of Qwen3.5-4B — meaning the model itself, not external code, is running the emulator logic, and you can actually play NES ROMs (including Super Mario Bros) inside the model. This is surprising not for practical utility but as a demonstration that LLM weights can encode arbitrary computational systems, not just language patterns — a conceptually novel result about what "weights" can contain.

Google's "Sleep Framework" for LLM Continual Learning

Google Research introduces a method for LLMs to retain new knowledge over time without catastrophic forgetting — inspired by how biological sleep consolidates memory. If this works at scale, it would address one of the fundamental limitations of current models: they can't learn from new information after training without degrading existing knowledge. Still research-stage, but addresses a real capability gap.
