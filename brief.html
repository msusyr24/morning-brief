🧬 Health & Science

Is the Correlation Between Hypertrophy and Strength Gains Stronger Than We Realized?

Stronger by Science digs into a methodological issue in prior research: studies measuring hypertrophy-strength correlations often used short training durations and homogeneous populations, which compressed the apparent relationship. When you look at newer lifters over longer windows, muscle growth appears to contribute more meaningfully to strength gains than the classic literature suggested — though the relationship is still far from deterministic. Practical implication: don't dismiss hypertrophy work as "just aesthetics" if absolute strength is a goal, especially early in a training career.

Stronger by Science

Lithium and Alzheimer's Disease: What to Make of the LATTICE Trial

Peter Attia reviews the LATTICE trial, a negative clinical result for lithium as an Alzheimer's intervention. The piece is notable for what it doesn't conclude: a negative RCT doesn't fully close the door, since mechanistic and observational data linking lithium to neuroprotection remain. Attia uses this to model how to reason carefully about failed trials — when to update hard, and when the prior evidence warrants continued inquiry.

💡 Worth Reading

Why Do We Think We Understand the World More Than We Actually Do?

This covers the "illusion of explanatory depth" — the well-documented phenomenon where people believe they can explain how things work (zippers, toilets, policies) until asked to actually do so, at which point their confidence collapses. The implication for decision-making is significant: confident-sounding explanations are often confabulation, and the gap between "recognizing" and "knowing" is much larger than we feel. A useful mental model to apply to your own reasoning before acting on it.

Astronauts Describe Persistent 'Observer' Sensation After 6-Month Missions

Astronauts returning from long-duration spaceflight report a weeks-long experience of watching themselves from slightly outside their own perspective — a dissociative quality distinct from ordinary re-entry fatigue. This connects to broader questions about how extreme environmental isolation reshapes the sense of self and embodied presence. Worth sitting with as a data point on the plasticity of first-person experience under unusual conditions.

Could Our Preoccupation with Mental Health Be Part of the Problem?

Tyler Cowen flags research suggesting that the cultural push to monitor, discuss, and label mental states may paradoxically increase distress — through over-identification with negative emotions and reduced tolerance for ordinary suffering. Less stigma is clearly good; but the medicalizing of normal adversity may be building fragility rather than resilience. Relevant to anyone thinking about the inner game and whether introspection always helps.

Marginal Revolution

Two Masks, One Stage — Stoic Role Ethics from Panaetius to Epictetus

Massimo Pigliucci traces the Stoic doctrine of roles (personae) — the idea, developed by Panaetius and refined by Epictetus, that we each play multiple roles simultaneously: the universal human role, and the particular roles of our circumstances (parent, citizen, professional). The philosophical tension is how to hold both without collapsing into either pure abstraction or pure situational identity. Useful framing for anyone working through questions of integrity under competing obligations.
