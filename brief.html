Anthony Chaffee MD

Three shorts making the carnivore case via neuroscience and evolutionary biology. On B12: Oxford published data showing that B12 levels in the "normal" range still predicted 2.5–5.5% brain volume loss over 5 years, and maternal B12 in the bottom 10th percentile (still "normal") correlated with worse vocabulary, speech, and math scores in children for at least 14 years. He argues the current normal ranges are population-averaged against a largely plant-eating public, effectively hiding deficiency. On dementia: Chaffee claims the human brain has shrunk 10–17% in both sexes in parallel with the shift to grain-based diets, and that grain-fed pigs show 18% smaller brains than wild boars — arguing neurodegeneration is dietary, not inevitable. On brain fuel: cites PET scan data showing that when ketones are available the brain preferentially uses them (up to 60–70% of energy), with glucose uptake falling measurably on scans even when blood glucose is normal, and that neonatal brains are built for a ketogenic environment.

🧬 Health & Science

Is the Correlation Between Hypertrophy and Strength Gains Stronger Than We Realized?

Stronger by Science digs into new data suggesting hypertrophy contributes more to strength gains in novice lifters than prior studies indicated — but with an important caveat: the apparent correlation is inflated by methodological issues (regression to the mean, measurement error), so the real relationship is meaningfully weaker than the raw numbers suggest. Practically, this means you shouldn't assume that training purely for size will translate proportionally into strength gains, especially in more trained athletes.

Stronger by Science

Two masks, one stage — Stoic role ethics from Panaetius to Epictetus

Massimo Pigliucci traces the Stoic doctrine of "roles" (personae) from Panaetius through Cicero to Epictetus — the idea that each of us plays multiple overlapping roles (human being, citizen, professional, family member) and that living well means understanding which role takes precedence in a given situation. Directly practical for thinking about how to act when duties conflict.
