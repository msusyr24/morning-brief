Ken Berry MD

Berry distills five clinical observations from 25,000 patients. The standout: fasting insulin predicts type 2 diabetes 5–15 years before blood sugar becomes diagnostic, far earlier than standard fasting glucose or A1C testing — but most doctors never order it. His second major point is mechanistic rather than motivational: hunger is a biological drive that always defeats willpower, which is why low-fat and plant-based diets fail structurally (they don't activate satiety hormones). He also flags that the bathroom scale is often the last metric to move — fasting insulin and body measurements are better early indicators of metabolic progress. 🧬 Health & Science

Pulse Pressure: The Blood Pressure Number Your Doctor Isn't Tracking

Dr. Sten Ekberg makes a mechanistically sound case that pulse pressure (systolic minus diastolic, ideally 30–40 mmHg) is a more meaningful cardiovascular marker than either number alone. A reading of 115/55 (PP=60) indicates stiffer arteries than 128/88 (PP=40), despite looking "healthier" at a glance. The mechanism: arterial elastin degrades over time via glycation, inflammation, and mechanical wear (~3 billion heartbeat cycles), and the body's repair attempts using collagen and calcium actually increase stiffness. Stiff arteries force the heart to pump harder (left ventricular hypertrophy), reduce coronary artery perfusion during diastole, and deliver blood in sharp waves rather than smooth flow — elevating risk of kidney damage, stroke, and heart failure. Crucially, this metric appears nowhere in the 2025 blood pressure guidelines, which focus only on absolute values and push earlier medication intervention.

Fasting Insulin as a Predictive Biomarker (Ken Berry)

Berry's clinical observation — backed by the mechanism of hyperinsulinemia preceding blood glucose dysregulation by years — is that a fasting insulin above 15 µU/mL can signal future type 2 diabetes 5–15 years before standard fasting glucose or A1C becomes diagnostic. He also notes that standard metabolic panels miss this entirely, since a fasting glucose of 95 and A1C of 5.6% both fall within "normal" range while fasting insulin of 12–14 would already indicate 2x insulin resistance (HOMA-IR ~2.0), subclinical atherosclerosis, and early dyslipidemia. The implication: tracking insulin, not just glucose, gives meaningful lead time for dietary intervention.

Keto vs. Mediterranean: 6-Month RCT Results (Nick Norwitz)

A 6-month randomized controlled trial — calorie-matched across three arms for equal weight loss — found the ketogenic diet (including bacon, cheesy beef, pulled pork) produced significantly greater improvements in liver fat, systemic inflammation, blood triglycerides, and insulin resistance scores compared to both a Mediterranean diet and a low-fat plant-based diet in people with obesity. The calorie-matching design is methodologically important: it rules out weight loss as the sole driver and isolates carbohydrate restriction as the active variable. The researchers' own conclusion: "A very low-carb diet has greater cardiometabolic benefits than a matched Mediterranean or low-fat plant-based diet."

Sleep Architecture: First-Cycle Growth Hormone and Memory Consolidation (Dr. Gina Poe)

The first 90-minute sleep cycle contains the largest bolus of growth hormone release in the 24-hour period — and missing it isn't compensated for by shifting sleep later, because the release is circadian-timed, not simply "first sleep" triggered. Alcohol suppresses both REM sleep and stage-2 sleep spindles (10–15 Hz thalamocortical oscillations), which are the specific mechanism by which the hippocampus transfers newly encoded memories to distributed cortical storage. Later sleep cycles (second half of the night) have progressively more REM, which is when schema-building and creative association occurs — making both early and late sleep functionally distinct and non-substitutable. Consistent bedtimes, not just consistent wake times, are flagged as one of the strongest markers of long-term neurological health.
