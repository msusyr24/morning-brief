GTO Wizard

Espen reviews six hands from the Triton 200K final table using GTO Wizard's new real-time ICM preflop solver — a feature that lets you input any final table payout structure and stack configuration and get solutions instantly without pre-running sims. The core lesson across the hands: ICM dramatically tightens ranges, making hands like pocket jacks a clear fold in multiway final table spots, while suited aces become preferred bluff-jams because they have better blocker effects (blocking ace-x calls while having equity when called). He walks through Seth Davies's choice to use a raise-fold strategy instead of open-jamming with 46 BBs, and the solver largely disagrees — showing that open-jamming is nearly equivalent EV and removes the awkward spot of getting re-jammed into. Figs in Winter (Massimo Pigliucci)

Pigliucci published a substantive piece on Stoicism and wealth, tackling the real tension: the Stoics held that wealth is a "preferred indifferent" — not good or bad in itself, but something a rational person will pursue as long as it doesn't compromise virtue. Food Processing Influences Metabolism and Brain Activity

Virginia Tech research published this week found that ultra-processed foods (UPFs) influence metabolism and brain activity in ways that go beyond simple caloric content — specifically looking at how UPFs alter the gut-brain signaling hormone hutelin. This adds mechanistic support to the "food noise" framing: the brain's appetite regulation is being actively disrupted by UPF compounds, not just by caloric density.

Virginia Tech / Fralin Biomedical Research Institute · HN discussion

Fructose Mechanism Correction: Small Intestine Clears First, Liver is Overflow

The 2018 discovery (now confirmed in follow-up human-consistent research) that the small intestine — not the liver — handles fructose first at low doses significantly changes the dose-dependent framing of sugar harm. The practical threshold: roughly 10–25g fructose per sitting before overflow hits the liver. A standard soda (21g fructose) sits at the high end of that range in liquid form on an empty stomach, meaning near-certain hepatic overflow. Whole fruit at moderate amounts (blackberries: 2.4g/100g; apple: 6.9g/100g) mostly clears in the gut. Medjool dates (32g/100g fructose) are the outlier that blows the limit immediately. The hunger mechanism distinction still stands independently: fructose minimally suppresses ghrelin compared to protein or fat, and brain imaging shows stronger reward activation and increased junk food craving after fructose vs. glucose consumption.

Covered by Dr. Sten Ekberg

Mouthwash and Blood Pressure: Nitric Oxide Pathway

Human studies show that antibacterial mouthwash raises blood pressure by roughly the same magnitude that exercise lowers it, by killing oral bacteria that reduce dietary nitrate to nitric oxide — a key vasodilator. The mechanism is well-established enough that it's worth reconsidering routine twice-daily antibacterial mouthwash use, particularly if you're monitoring cardiovascular markers.

Cited by Nick Norwitz

Peter Attia: Alzheimer's Symptomatic Treatments — What Cholinesterase Inhibitors Actually Do

Attia's piece examines the real-world efficacy of the drugs (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) that have been the standard of care for Alzheimer's for decades. The honest framing: these are symptomatic treatments that slow cognitive decline modestly — think weeks to months of preserved function — not disease-modifying agents. Worth reading if you have aging parents navigating diagnosis and treatment decisions.

Pessimism Always Sounds Smarter Than Optimism

Morgan Housel's short piece on why pessimism gets treated as intellectual sophistication while optimism gets treated as naivety — even when the base rates favor optimism over long time horizons. The cognitive bias is real: a pessimist who's wrong just got unlucky, but an optimist who's wrong was foolish. That asymmetry shapes how we signal intelligence, not necessarily what's true.

AI and Call Centers: Global Headcount Down 5.3% from Peak

Global call center employment is 5.3% below its December 2023 peak after eight consecutive quarters of year-over-year decline — the first sustained drop after more than a decade of growth. The decline started in high-income countries and coincides directly with the scaling of AI chatbots. This is one of the cleaner early signals of AI actually displacing knowledge-work jobs at scale in the real economy, not just in demos.
