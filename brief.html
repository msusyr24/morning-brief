Jared Alderman

The Brock Wilson WSOP Main Event debrief is excellent. Brock's core approach: use solver knowledge not to play robotically, but to make reads-based deviations — if you notice a player isn't barreling Ace-high on blank turns, you can now over-fold when they do barrel, and over-call when they don't. The specific hand they dissect: HJ opens, BB calls, Q-10-7-3 board. Ace-5 is a high-EV turn barrel because (a) the 3 doesn't help BB's range much, (b) HJ retains all strong hands post-flop-no-check-raise, and (c) if you skip the turn barrel, you have no bluffs on K or J rivers — your hand choice now determines your whole river range. Jared's separate "Can You Trust Your Results" clip is worth pausing on: the imposter syndrome framing is real — you only live your own cards, so variance is invisible to you, and coaching others is one of the fastest ways to confirm your own understanding. The Uri Peleg short cuts to the core: obsessively reviewing big-pot spots where the answer is clear and the EV delta is small is comfort-seeking, not improvement — the high-value work is in the murky spots people avoid.

Peter Attia

Two crisp shorts. On women and fitness post-50: the social media claim that postmenopausal women shouldn't do Zone 2 is wrong — the argument (higher proportion of Type 1 fibers, better use resistance training) is plausible but falsely assumes zero-sum tradeoffs between modalities; both are necessary, and the real answer is "make more than 1 hour available." Hypertrophy–Strength Correlation Stronger in Novices Than Previously Thought

Stronger by Science published an analysis finding that prior studies underestimated how much muscle growth contributes to strength gains in new lifters, likely due to methodological limitations. However, the relationship remains far from tight — muscle gain is not a reliable proxy for strength gain even in novices. Worth reading if you program for beginners or think about hypertrophy and 1RM as interchangeable proxies.

Stronger by Science

Velocity-Based Training & the "Jerk Metric" for Auto-Regulation

Chris Duffin (world-record powerlifter) on the Thomas DeLauer channel argued that VBT (velocity-based training) using your phone camera — which can now replicate lab-grade bar velocity measurement — allows true auto-regulation: you know your effective 1RM that day without maxing out, and can add volume on good days instead of only pulling back on bad ones. The next frontier he describes is the "jerk metric" — the change in rate of acceleration within a rep — which detects compensatory movement patterns before you feel them, indicating a muscle is beginning to fatigue and you're approaching injury territory. He claims this leads-indicator (not lagging like HRV) could allow real-time workout cutoffs that prevent overuse injuries while maximizing total volume over a training year.

Lithium Orotate for Alzheimer's Prevention — 2025 Harvard/Nature Data

Nick Norwitz cited a 2025 Nature paper out of Harvard suggesting lithium orotate at 5mg/day may protect against Alzheimer's by inhibiting an enzyme in the tau hyperphosphorylation pathway — one of the disease's core mechanisms. This is a micro-dose (pharmaceutical lithium carbonate is 150–600mg), available OTC, and distinct from psychiatric lithium use. The data is early but mechanistically grounded and worth watching.
