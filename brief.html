Peter Attia

Attia walks through thyroid physiology with unusual clarity: the gland secretes mostly inactive T4, which gets converted to active T3 locally tissue-by-tissue, meaning TSH — the standard test — is one step removed from what's actually happening in your liver, brain, and heart. He identifies two failure modes he sees constantly: (1) dismissing real hypothyroidism because TSH looks "normal" even when antibodies are positive and symptoms are clear, and (2) functional medicine practitioners who treat borderline reverse-T3 as proof of tissue hypothyroidism regardless of TSH, building elaborate "root cause" narratives around normal variation. His position: overt thyroid hormone treatment is not benign — it can cause cognitive symptoms, AFib, and bone loss — and a large share of mildly elevated TSH will normalize spontaneously on retest. Symptom clusters alone (fatigue, weight gain, feeling off) are essentially useless diagnostically; formal studies show clinicians can't distinguish hypothyroid from euthyroid patients on symptoms alone.

Video: Thyroid health: interpreting symptoms, diagnosing & treating dysfunction

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer covers two topics with actual mechanism behind them. On red light therapy: a controlled trial of 130+ people found measurably denser collagen confirmed by ultrasound, and the mechanism runs through cytochrome c oxidase — the enzyme at the end of the mitochondrial electron transport chain that absorbs red/near-infrared light almost perfectly. As cells age, nitric oxide gets stuck on this enzyme and clogs energy production; red light dislodges it, restoring ATP output so cells can afford expensive repair jobs like collagen synthesis. Dosing: 10–15 minutes, most days, 630–660nm — more is not better and can increase oxidative stress. On calcium and insulin resistance: a human study in Hypertension found that 1,500mg/day of calcium supplementation restored insulin sensitivity and lowered fasting insulin. The mechanism: low dietary calcium paradoxically raises intracellular calcium, which disrupts insulin receptor substrate signaling so insulin can't dock. A secondary study showed 500mg with breakfast reduced lunch calorie intake by 116 calories. He recommends dietary calcium first (sardines, hard aged cheeses, cottage cheese) over aggressive supplementation given coronary calcium score concerns.

🧬 Health & Science

Thyroid Diagnosis: The Two Failure Modes (Peter Attia)

The key clinical framework from Attia's AMA: TSH measures how hard the pituitary is asking the thyroid to work, not what's actually happening in peripheral tissues where T4→T3 conversion occurs locally via different deiodinase enzymes. This means a "normal" TSH doesn't rule out tissue-level hypothyroidism, and an abnormal TSH doesn't always require treatment. Attia flags that subclinical hypothyroidism resolves spontaneously in a large share of cases on repeat testing — and that overtreating with exogenous thyroid hormone carries real risks including AFib and bone loss. His practical rule: integrate TSH, free T4, free T3, antibody status, family history, and symptoms together rather than treating any single lab value in isolation.

Calcium, Insulin Resistance, and Appetite Regulation (Thomas DeLauer)

The counterintuitive mechanism: low dietary calcium raises intracellular calcium, which disrupts insulin receptor substrate-1 (IRS-1) signaling — so the insulin can't bind effectively, circulating insulin stays elevated, and cells become progressively numb to it. The human RCT in Hypertension used 1,500mg/day for 8 weeks and showed restored insulin sensitivity and reduced fasting insulin. A separate study (Proceedings of the Nutrition Society) found 500mg of calcium at breakfast reduced ad libitum lunch intake by 116 calories — likely via the body's satiety signaling once calcium sufficiency is sensed. Practical implication: morning calcium from food (sardines, aged cheese, cottage cheese) or a modest supplement (500–1,000mg) is preferable to high-dose supplementation given the CAC score / atherosclerosis concerns flagged by the AHA.

Red Light Therapy: Mechanism and Dosing (Thomas DeLauer)

The Duke University review of 59 studies / ~1,900 patients, plus a controlled trial measuring collagen density by ultrasound, provide the evidentiary base. The mechanism: cytochrome c oxidase in Complex IV of the mitochondrial ETC absorbs red/NIR light (630–660nm), which dislodges nitric oxide that chronically blocks electron flow as cells age. Restored electron transport → more ATP → cells can afford collagen and elastin synthesis. Dosing principle is important: this is a signal, not a dose-response drug — 10–15 minutes is the research cluster, more creates oxidative stress without additional benefit. Pair with adequate dietary protein; stimulating the repair signal without building blocks is sending an order with no supply chain.

GLP-1 Cycling Degrades Body Composition (Andrew Huberman)

With semaglutide and similar agents, roughly one-third of weight lost is lean mass. When patients cycle off and regain weight, they regain fat — not muscle. Each on/off cycle therefore ratchets the muscle-to-fat ratio in the wrong direction. Huberman's recommendation: instead of stopping, titrate down to a microdose that maintains weight without the full drug burden, or have a explicit bridging plan before discontinuation. This has practical implications for anyone treating GLP-1s as a short-term intervention.
