Mental Model · Risk/Mathematics

Convexity vs. Concavity

Convex payoffs benefit from volatility (options, bets with capped losses). Concave payoffs are hurt by volatility (leverage, short-term trading). Life structures that are convex compound in your favor; concave structures blow up eventually. Structure your life to be convex where possible.

GTO Wizard

Tom Boss delivers a deep, data-backed breakdown of late registration in MTTs. The core ICM argument: when you enter with 50% of the field eliminated, your starting stack is worth ~10-15% more than face value because your bust-out probability distribution shifts favorably while your first-place equity stays identical. He validates this with WSOP data showing 10-15% ROI boosts for late entrants, and Triton data (8,000 bullets, 2025-2026) showing players actually outperform their own early-entry results when they re-enter later — controlling for skill by comparing each player against themselves. Most counterintuitive finding: late reg does NOT increase variance according to ICM math, meaning you get a better risk-adjusted return and can shot-take bigger tournaments on a smaller bankroll. His five-bullet rule, drawn from Triton data showing catastrophic ROI collapse at bullet five-plus: after four bust-outs in one tournament, do not fire again — pure monkey tilt explains the cliff.

🧬 Health & Science

Myostatin Inhibition Clinical Results (Nick Norwitz)

Human trials of myostatin inhibitors — based on a naturally occurring genetic mutation that removes the brake on muscle growth in certain cattle — are showing approximately 9 lbs of fat loss and 7 lbs of muscle gain from a single injection. Myostatin is a protein that suppresses muscle growth; blocking it appears to simultaneously drive body recomposition. This is early-stage but represents one of the more striking single-intervention body composition results reported in clinical trials. Worth tracking as this moves through development.

YouTube: Could One Injection Increase Muscle and Reduce Fat?

Electrolyte vs. Water Hydration Mechanics (Dr. Sten Ekberg)

The specific mechanism worth knowing: potassium is 98% intracellular and magnesium 99% intracellular, meaning standard blood panels are nearly useless for assessing deficiency — serum levels only drop after total body stores are severely depleted, with the bone acting as a long-term reservoir that gets silently leeched for years. Modern diets deliver roughly 2x the sodium and half the potassium/magnesium actually needed. Low-carb and fasting diets compound this: as insulin drops, the kidneys lose their insulin-driven tendency to retain minerals, causing an acute flush of electrolytes that can produce fatigue, muscle cramps, palpitations, and brain fog — symptoms often misattributed to other causes. The fix is consistent co-ingestion of water and electrolytes, not just water volume.

YouTube: Why Drinking Water Is NOT The Best Way To Stay Hydrated

VO2 Max as a Mortality Predictor (Ben Felix / JAMA 2018)

A 2018 JAMA study cited by Ben Felix found that being below-average in cardiorespiratory fitness for your age and sex doubles all-cause mortality risk compared to the top quartile — and that this effect is comparable to or greater than the mortality risk of smoking. Separately, a 2018 study found that maintaining five healthy lifestyle factors (never smoking, healthy BMI, 30+ min/day moderate-vigorous activity, moderate alcohol, high diet quality) extends life by 12-14 years compared to those who don't. The compounding frame is useful: muscle mass starts declining in your 30s, making resistance training function like early retirement contributions — the more you accumulate early, the longer you can draw it down during the inevitable age-related decline.

💡 Worth Reading

How Complex Systems Fail (1998)

A short, dense 1998 paper by Richard Cook that has aged remarkably well. Its core argument: complex systems (medical, infrastructure, financial) are always running in a degraded state, failure is never caused by a single root cause but by the coincidence of multiple latent failures, and practitioners who operate near the edge of failure are paradoxically the ones keeping the system alive. The paper reframes "accidents" not as aberrations but as the normal output of complex systems under pressure. Highly relevant as a mental model for any domain involving high-stakes decisions with incomplete information.

The Sloppification of Peptides

An essay on how peptide therapies (BPC-157, TB-500, etc.) have moved from research-grade compounds with real clinical potential into a chaotic gray market where purity, dosing, and sourcing are essentially unverifiable. The argument is that popularization without regulatory structure has degraded the signal — it's increasingly hard to know whether reported effects are real or artifacts of inconsistent product quality. Relevant if you're tracking longevity and biohacking developments, as it's a useful epistemic caution about the peptide space.
