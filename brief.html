🎥 From People I Follow

Worth your time:

Peter Attia

Jim Otvos — the inventor of NMR-based lipoprotein testing — explains how a single NMR blood scan can simultaneously measure LDL particle number, LDL particle size (small dense vs. large), HDL subfractions, insulin resistance, and inflammatory markers, giving far more cardiovascular and metabolic signal than a standard lipid panel. Key point: the standard calculated LDL-C number (using the Friedewald equation, which divides triglycerides by 5 to estimate VLDL) is a rough estimate that systematically misleads — NMR gives direct particle counts instead. Otvos explains that small dense LDL confers roughly 3x the cardiovascular risk of large LDL at the same LDL-C level, which is why particle number matters more than cholesterol mass. The conversation also touches on how the same NMR readout independently predicts diabetes risk, inflammation levels via GlycA, and all-cause mortality — "a single scan tells you a heck of a lot more than just your cardiovascular risk."

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer walks through a Cell Metabolism RCT (131 overweight adults, 7 weeks) that found combining time-restricted eating (10-hour window) with 3x/week HIIT produced roughly twice the total and visceral fat loss as either intervention alone — with significantly greater HbA1c improvement. The mechanism: fasting depletes glycogen and elevates catecholamines, so HIIT performed during a fast drives deeper fat oxidation and ketone production, while simultaneously suppressing hepatic fat-storage gene expression; crucially, the resistance component of HIIT preserves lean mass during the fast. He emphasizes the dose is short — 10–15 minutes of high-intensity work is sufficient as a metabolic stimulus; the fat burning happens throughout the day, not during the session itself. He also cites a New England Journal study (139 people, 12 months) where TRE vs. calorie restriction alone didn't reach statistical significance but TRE showed consistently better raw numbers across every metric: 8 kg lost vs. 6.3 kg, visceral fat area down 26 vs. 21 cm², and double the improvement in HOMA-IR (insulin resistance).

🧬 Health & Science

NMR Lipid Panel: What It Measures That Standard Labs Miss

From the Peter Attia / Jim Otvos conversation: the standard calculated LDL-C number is a proxy built on the Friedewald equation (total cholesterol − HDL − triglycerides/5), which systematically underestimates risk in people with high triglycerides or metabolic dysfunction. NMR measures LDL particle number (LDL-P) directly, and Otvos's research shows small dense LDL (pattern B) carries ~3x the cardiovascular risk at identical LDL-C levels. Beyond lipids, the same NMR scan measures GlycA (a composite inflammatory biomarker), insulin resistance via LPIR score, and has been shown to independently predict all-cause mortality — making it a rare single-test window into multiple disease pathways simultaneously.

Peter Attia – Episode 402

Zero Coronary Calcium Score ≠ Zero Cardiovascular Risk

Attia's team flags an important clinical nuance: a zero CAC score, while reassuring, should not be used to dismiss sustained LDL-C elevation — particularly in younger adults where soft plaques that don't yet calcify can still drive risk. The argument is that CAC reflects historical plaque that has already calcified, not current arterial inflammatory burden, so a zero score in someone with chronically elevated LDL-P is not a green light.

Peter Attia MD

Is the Correlation Between Hypertrophy and Strength Gains Stronger Than We Realized?

Stronger by Science reviews new analysis suggesting hypertrophy contributes more to strength gains in novice lifters than prior studies indicated — but with an important caveat: the apparent correlation is partially a statistical artifact, and the relationship remains far from deterministic. Practical implication: for new lifters, building muscle and building strength are more intertwined than previously modeled, but programming for one doesn't automatically optimize the other.

Vitamin H: The Science of Happiness Is Weaker Than You Think

Massimo Pigliucci (Stoic philosopher and scientist) argues that the empirical happiness research literature — positive psychology, hedonic adaptation studies, "happiness set points" — is methodologically fragile and overhyped. Worth reading as a corrective to the pop-psychology confidence around what reliably produces wellbeing, especially paired with Stoic frameworks that don't depend on empirical happiness science to justify their prescriptions.

On Contentment — Epictetus, Discourses 1.12

Pigliucci unpacks Epictetus's argument that contentment is not about getting what you want but about correctly understanding what is and isn't in your control — and then genuinely wanting only what is. The Stoic move here isn't resignation; it's reorienting desire toward the will itself, which is the one thing that can't be taken from you. Short but worth sitting with.
