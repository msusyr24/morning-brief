🧬 Health & Science

NMR Blood Analysis: Heart Disease Risk, Insulin Resistance, Inflammation, and Mortality from a Single Test

Jim Otvos, the scientist who developed NMR lipoprotein analysis, walks through what a single NMR scan can reveal beyond standard lipid panels: particle number and size (LDL-P vs. LDL-C), insulin resistance via GlycA and other markers, systemic inflammation, and all-cause mortality risk. The key insight is that LDL cholesterol concentration is a poor proxy — LDL particle number is what predicts atherosclerosis, and two people with identical LDL-C can have radically different cardiovascular risk profiles. The episode makes a compelling case for why NMR should be part of routine preventive medicine and explains what to actually ask your doctor for.

Peter Attia MD — Episode #402

Is the Correlation Between Hypertrophy and Strength Gains Stronger Than We Realized?

Stronger by Science digs into a new analysis suggesting muscle growth contributes more to strength gains in novice lifters than prior research indicated — prior studies may have been methodologically underpowered to detect the relationship. The caveat: the correlation is still not tight, meaning substantial hypertrophy doesn't reliably predict substantial strength gains and vice versa, especially in trained individuals. Practical implication: for beginners, muscle growth and strength are more coupled than we thought; for advanced lifters, they remain meaningfully distinct goals requiring different programming emphases.

Vitamin H: The Science of Happiness Is Weaker Than You Think

Massimo Pigliucci (Stoic philosopher, biologist) argues that positive psychology's evidence base is much shakier than its popular reputation suggests — effect sizes are small, replication rates are poor, and the field conflates hedonic and eudaimonic wellbeing in ways that muddy the findings. The implicit argument: ancient philosophical frameworks may offer more durable guidance on flourishing than the modern "happiness science" industry. A useful corrective to the Huberman-ification of the good life.
