Mental Model · Psychology

Deprival Super-Reaction

Losing $100 feels worse than finding $100 feels good — by about 2x, per Kahneman's research. Munger calls this 'deprival super-reaction.' It explains stock-market panics, breakup grief, and why people fight harder to keep what they have than to acquire what they don't. Knowing this, you can price losses more rationally.

Andrew Huberman / Andy Galpin

This is the Galpin framework for assessing all nine physiological adaptations — worth having as a reference. The nine categories: movement skill, speed, power, strength, hypertrophy, muscular endurance, anaerobic capacity, max aerobic capacity (VO2 max), and long-duration endurance. Key benchmarks: broad jump ≥ your own height (−15% for women) for power; grip strength ≥ 40kg men / 35kg women, dead hang ≥ 30–60 seconds for strength; non-dominant hand shouldn't vary >10% from dominant. For movement, the self-assessment is filming four movements (push, pull, squat, hinge) from front and side and checking four things: symmetry, stability, awareness, range of motion. The framework is useful precisely because it separates these adaptations — most people train only a few and have blind spots.

Selective Insulin Resistance — the mechanism that makes carb restriction make sense

Nick Norwitz's short is worth flagging for the specific mechanistic clarity: insulin resistance is not global. Muscle glucose uptake (pathway 1) and hepatic glucose suppression (pathway 2) become resistant, while hepatic lipogenesis (pathway 3) remains insulin-sensitive. So when the pancreas compensates by secreting more insulin, it overstimuates fat production while failing to clear blood glucose — you get hyperglycemia AND excess liver fat simultaneously. This has been demonstrated in calorie-controlled human RCTs. The implication: lowering insulin via carbohydrate reduction addresses the root driver in a way that caloric restriction alone does not.

Empathy Gaps — Marginal Revolution

A simple but useful observation about hot-cold empathy gaps in decision-making: when well-rested and in your home time zone, you systematically underestimate the cost of future discomfort (a long trip, a difficult commitment). The framing has direct applications to pre-committing to things when in a "cold" state that you'll regret agreeing to in a "hot" state — and vice versa. Classic Kahneman-adjacent territory but with a personal, concrete illustration.

If Math Is More Than Proof, We Need to Better Celebrate the Rest of It — Terry Tao

Fields Medalist Terry Tao argues that mathematical culture over-indexes on formal proof as the only legitimate output, while undervaluing conjecture, heuristics, numerical exploration, and the process of building intuition.
