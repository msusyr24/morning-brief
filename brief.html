Peter Attia

This AMA frames the liver as the "canary in the coal mine" for systemic metabolic dysfunction — not a standalone organ problem. Attia walks through a four-stage progression: metabolic stress → steatosis (fat accumulation) → stash/MASH (inflammation, cell death) → fibrosis (scarring), with only fibrosis being hard to reverse. Key mechanism: "selective hepatic insulin resistance" means the liver keeps releasing glucose even when blood sugar is high, while simultaneously overproducing triglycerides — the worst of both worlds. He notes the condition affects an estimated 38% of the world's adult population, and the leading cause of death in people with liver disease is cardiovascular disease, not liver failure, because a metabolically stressed liver amplifies APOB production and systemic insulin resistance. Early detection is the crux: blood work is nearly useless for catching intracellular electrolyte depletion and early liver fat, since serum levels stay normal until the problem is severe.

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer argues the best longevity diet is a high-protein Mediterranean hybrid — not the conventional low-protein Mediterranean. The case: Mediterranean eating touches nearly every hallmark of aging (DNA oxidative damage, telomere length, epigenetic methylation, autophagy, mitochondrial health) but fails on the muscle-preservation front. A Spanish ketogenic Mediterranean diet trial (lower carb, built on olive oil + fish + quality meat) reversed fatty liver in 93% of participants in 12 weeks, normalized triglycerides from 232→111, fasting glucose from 118→90, and resolved metabolic syndrome in every participant — while still technically overweight, meaning metabolic healing outran weight loss. The omega-3 mechanism that's underreported: EPA/DHA get incorporated into muscle cell membranes and amplify anabolic signaling from protein, with controlled studies showing omega-3 supplementation raises muscle protein synthesis — especially valuable because older muscle develops anabolic resistance requiring more protein to get the same growth signal.

Andrew Huberman

Dr. Chris Palmer's clinical work makes a strong case that metabolic dysfunction and mental illness are deeply intertwined — not merely correlated. His most striking case: a patient with schizoaffective disorder who had failed 17 medications over 8 years had his auditory hallucinations and paranoid delusions largely resolve within 6-8 weeks of a ketogenic diet, without any medication change. Palmer's threshold for clinical effect: blood ketones >0.8 mmol for depression, >1.5 mmol for psychotic/bipolar disorders. His key observation from his own practice is that patients who achieved urinary ketosis showed benefits while those who tried the diet without reaching ketosis typically did not — suggesting ketone production itself, not just carb reduction, drives the effect. The metabolic-syndrome-to-mental-clarity arc from his own experience (metabolic syndrome resolved in 3 months on Atkins, with dramatic secondary improvement in mood, energy, and sleep) parallels his patients' outcomes and grounds the argument in personal validity-checking.

₿ Bitcoin & Macro

Coldcard Bug Exposed Single-Sig Risk — Multi-Vendor Multisig Is the New Bitcoin Custody Baseline

A hardware wallet bug in Coinkite's Coldcard led to real thefts in July–August 2026, and Coinkite has responded by requiring dice rolls and additional entropy on new seed generation. The lasting lesson from Bitcoin Magazine's analysis isn't to abandon self-custody but to treat single-vendor single-sig as unacceptable custody risk — multi-vendor multisig (using devices from different manufacturers) is now argued to be the minimum baseline for meaningful holdings. This is a concrete security posture shift worth internalizing if you hold self-custodied BTC.

🧬 Health & Science

GLP-1 Medications Beyond Weight Loss — Peter Attia

Peter Attia's review covers the expanding evidence base for GLP-1 receptor agonists beyond obesity treatment. The drug class now has established data across cardiovascular outcomes, kidney disease progression, and sleep apnea, with emerging signals in addiction (alcohol, opioids), neurodegeneration (Alzheimer's), and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. The key mechanistic question is whether these benefits are mostly downstream of weight loss and metabolic improvement, or whether GLP-1 receptors in the brain, heart, and kidneys confer direct organ-level effects — and the current data increasingly suggest both pathways are operating simultaneously.

FDA Approves First Targeted Therapy for Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer

The FDA has approved the first-in-class targeted therapy for metastatic pancreatic cancer — a disease historically resistant to treatment with a five-year survival rate under 12%. Pancreatic cancer is notoriously detected late and has had essentially no new meaningful treatment options for years, making this a genuine clinical advance rather than an incremental approval.

Carnivore + Heavy Lifting for Testosterone — Ken Berry MD (informal data)

From Berry's informal community tracking of hundreds of men: eating high-fat red meat and regularly lifting heavy things produced average testosterone increases of ~125–150 points (ng/dL), with a range of 50–450 points. This is informal, self-reported data from a motivated community rather than an RCT, so effect sizes are probably inflated — but the directional finding (dietary fat + resistance training → testosterone) is consistent with the published literature on testosterone's precursor relationship to dietary cholesterol and the well-established anabolic response to compound lifting.

Amazon Mechanical Turk Shutting Down September 30

MTurk — Amazon's crowdsourced human-task marketplace that ran for 20 years — is closing at the end of September. It was the foundational infrastructure for AI training data labeling, behavioral economics research, and social science studies; its closure is a marker of how AI-generated synthetic data and specialized labeling platforms have displaced the model. The real question is what happens to the research methodology pipeline that depended on it.

How to Mentally Rehearse Coping — Donald Robertson

Robertson's lesson in his Stoic Mind series covers the Stoic practice of negative visualization (premeditatio malorum) as a structured mental rehearsal — not catastrophizing, but deliberately walking through adverse scenarios in advance so the actual event arrives with less psychological shock. The technique is grounded in Epictetus's framework: rehearse both the event and your intended response so that when difficulty arrives, your reaction is already practiced rather than improvised.

Donald Robertson / Substack

What Philosophy Promises — Massimo Pigliucci on Epictetus 1.15

Pigliucci unpacks Epictetus's argument that philosophy doesn't promise to get you external things — wealth, power, health — but rather teaches you that whether you get them or not, you will be okay. The promise of philosophy is the cultivation of equanimity toward outcomes you don't control, not optimization of outcomes themselves. A short but pointed piece for anyone who confuses Stoicism with a performance framework.
