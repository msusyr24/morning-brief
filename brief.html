Mental Model · Epistemics

Survivorship Bias

We see the winners and forget the losers who didn't make it. Successful entrepreneurs' advice is biased toward the strategies of survivors, ignoring the identical strategies of people who failed. WWII example: engineers wanted to armor the parts of returning planes with bullet holes — until Abraham Wald pointed out the planes that didn't return were hit elsewhere.

🧬 Health & Science

Compounding Health — Key Data Points (via Ben Felix)

A few specific numbers worth filing away: VO2 max in the bottom quartile for age/sex is associated with 2x all-cause mortality vs. the top quartile, with the JAMA authors noting the mortality risk of low fitness is "comparable to, if not greater than" cigarette smoking. Muscle mass decline (sarcopenia) begins as early as your 30s and compounds — the framing Ben uses is that you want to "retire" with enough muscle mass to sustain the drawdown of aging, same logic as portfolio size. On sleep: under 6 hours/night is associated with elevated cardiac risk; sleeping pills correlate with shorter lifespan. On happiness: people who self-report as "not happy" have a 14% higher all-cause mortality rate than very happy people, controlling for socioeconomic variables.

Why Aren't Smart People Happier? (2022)

Resurfaces on HN with 109 points — the core argument is that high cognitive ability doesn't translate to better life outcomes in the hedonic sense, and may actually work against happiness through mechanisms like heightened expectation, comparison, and counterfactual thinking. Worth reading alongside the Ben Felix video above, which makes the empirical case that relationships and subjective well-being are among the strongest longevity predictors.

The Stoic Taxonomy of Anger

Donald Robertson breaks down how the Stoics — primarily Seneca — categorized anger into distinct stages and types, which is more practically useful than the generic "don't be angry" advice. The classification matters because different stages of anger require different interventions; catching it early (the "first movement") is far easier than trying to reason your way out of full passion.

How to Mentally Rehearse Coping

Part of Robertson's Stoic Mind miniseries — this one covers premeditatio malorum and related visualization practices as trainable mental skills, not just philosophical attitudes. The practical framing: mentally rehearsing adverse scenarios reduces their emotional impact when they occur and strengthens the response pattern, similar to how athletes use mental imagery before competition.

Massimo Pigliucci reads the new Pope's AI encyclical through a Stoic-philosophical lens rather than a theological one. I Should Have Loved Biology (2020)

Resurfaces on HN with 208 points. A good piece to sit with if you've ever felt the gap between how science is presented and how interesting it actually is.

Anthropic has deployed an invisible watermark in Claude's text output — a steganographic signal embedded in the writing itself, detectable without the user knowing it's there.
