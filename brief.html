Also posted:

  • Peter Attia: A framework for evaluating peptides — not as a category but one-by-one. His five-question scaffold: Is there a viable mechanism? Human evidence of benefit? Understood safety/dosing/PK? Does benefit justify risk for this person? Is there a better-characterized alternative? He runs BPC-157 through the framework and it fails every test: no characterized receptor, no human RCTs after 30 years, all positive data from one lab with IP interests, unknown bioavailability, and the proposed pro-angiogenic mechanisms (VEGF, nitric oxide) are arguably reasons for concern rather than confidence. BIP-110 Fork Stalls at Two Blocks as Bitcoin Miners Refuse to Follow

A controversial protocol change (BIP-110, backed by the Knots community, aimed at filtering "spam" transactions) attempted to fork Bitcoin on Saturday and failed decisively — miners refused to follow, leaving the fork stranded at two blocks. This is a meaningful signal about the difficulty of imposing policy changes on Bitcoin's base layer without miner consensus, and a win for those who argue the network's conservatism is a feature.

🧬 Health & Science

Peptides: The Five-Question Framework

Covered above in YouTube section, but the RSS version includes access to the full AMA show notes now made free. The clinical crux: Attia's position is that being a "peptide" tells you nothing about safety or efficacy — it's a chemical description, not a quality signal. The GLP-1 agonists and insulin are peptides; so is BPC-157, which has no published human RCTs, no known receptor, and mechanisms (VEGF upregulation, pro-angiogenic signaling) that should increase caution in anyone with cancer risk, not decrease it. His rule: if a better-characterized drug exists that does the same thing, the burden of proof on the less-characterized version is high.

Peter Attia MD

Does Protein Restriction Still Make Sense in Modern Kidney Care?

Attia's team revisits the decades-old clinical assumption that high protein intake damages kidneys. The piece argues the evidence for protein restriction in kidney protection was built in an era before modern nephrology — particularly before ACE inhibitors and careful blood pressure management — and may not apply to otherwise healthy people eating adequate protein for muscle maintenance. Worth reading if you're on a high-protein or carnivore-adjacent diet and have had a doctor flag creatinine levels.

💡 Worth Reading

Be Mindful Not to Injure Your Own Character — Commentary on Epictetus #38

Donald Robertson's ongoing close reading of the Enchiridion. Handbook passage 38 is one of the more practically demanding: Epictetus argues that entertaining yourself with morally coarse people or content isn't neutral — it corrodes your character gradually, without the drama of a single bad choice. Robertson draws the modern parallel to ambient media consumption. Worth sitting with alongside Marcus's similar warning in Meditations about the company you keep internally.

The River and the Labyrinth (Parts I & II) — Pigliucci on Process Philosophy vs. Heraclitus

Massimo Pigliucci works through what process philosophy (Whitehead's metaphysics of becoming over being) gets right — that reality is fundamentally dynamic — and where it loses the thread by overcorrecting into abstraction. Part II brings it back to Heraclitus, arguing the pre-Socratic insight ("you can't step in the same river twice") is more useful and less cumbersome than Whitehead's full apparatus. Good two-part pair for anyone thinking about identity, change, and the Stoic concept of the ever-flowing present.

The Mechanical Miracle That Ruined Mark Twain's Life

A well-reported piece on Twain's catastrophic investment in the Paige Compositor — a typesetting machine he believed would make him fabulously wealthy. He sank roughly $300,000 (millions in today's terms) over 14 years into a machine that was ingenious, nearly functional, and ultimately obsolete the moment it was ready. It's a sharp case study in sunk cost fallacy, concentrated risk, and the psychology of believing you've found an edge — Twain's letters during the period are remarkable for showing how hope and prior investment distorted his read of the evidence.

🤖 AI Capability Watch

Meta Releases Muse Glimmer: 30B Open-Weight Agentic Model

Meta has returned to open-weight models with Muse Glimmer, a 30B parameter model under a clean Apache 2.0 license — a meaningful upgrade from the restrictive Llama licenses. It is designed for always-on, persistent agentic workflows (tool use, multi-step task completion) and is optimized to run locally rather than requiring cloud infrastructure, which opens the door to private, offline AI agents on consumer hardware for the first time at this capability level.

Needle2: 14MB LLM Designed for Phones, Wearables, and Robots

Needle2 is a 14-megabyte language model small enough to run on a microcontroller or smartwatch — the entire model fits in RAM on devices with no GPU. The practical implication is genuinely on-device AI with no network dependency on hardware that was previously considered far below the threshold for LLM inference, which matters for privacy, latency, and offline use cases in embedded systems.
