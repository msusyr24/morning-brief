Mental Model · Economics

Supply and Demand

Prices are signals, not values. When something is scarce relative to demand, its price rises until demand drops or supply increases. Most people ignore this when buying bubbles (tulips, crypto) or selling panics (2008 real estate). Munger's defense: always ask, 'relative to what supply, and what demand level?'

Worth your time:

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer's carb-management video is genuinely stacked with mechanisms worth understanding. The core argument: it's not carbs but glucose absorption speed that drives insulin burden, and you can attack that chain at multiple points simultaneously. Key tactics with actual data behind them: (1) Resistant starch — cook and refrigerate rice/pasta/potatoes 12–24hrs; retrogradation creates RS3 starch that resists amylase digestion; a 31-RCT meta-analysis in Frontiers in Nutrition found RS2 lowered fasting glucose and insulin in diabetics over weeks to months. (2) Meal sequencing — a Diabetes Care RCT found eating protein and vegetables first, carbs last, cut 30-min glucose by 29% and 60-min glucose by 37% vs. eating carbs first; the mechanism is GLP/GIP hormonal priming that slows gastric emptying before glucose even arrives. (3) Post-meal movement — a systematic review found light walking (not just standing) activates GLUT4 translocation, clearing glucose via a completely insulin-independent pathway; isometric holds (wall sits, bottom of push-up) acutely and chronically increase GLUT4 protein levels. (4) Psyllium husk — 5–10g in water 5–10 minutes before a carb meal forms a physical gel barrier that slows enzyme access to starch; a 2024 BMC meta-analysis of 19 RCTs (900+ subjects) found psyllium decreased blood sugar, HOMA-IR, and HbA1c vs. placebo, with dose-response kicking in above 10g. His fiber supplement video is a useful companion: concentrated single-fiber supplements (especially fructooligosaccharides) can actually narrow microbiome diversity over time by selectively feeding certain species while starving others — a Cell study found a high-fermented-food diet increased bacterial species count while high-fiber diet did not, despite both improving gut health. Psyllium gets a pass because it contains multiple soluble fiber types and acts more as a food matrix than a concentrated supplement. His pumpkin seed video covers zinc's role as a structural cofactor for 17β-HSD (the enzyme catalyzing the final step of testosterone synthesis) — a marginal deficiency dropped serum testosterone 73% in one study and doubling occurred when sufficiency was restored; delta-7 sterols in pumpkin seeds also compete at the androgen receptor and inhibit 5-alpha reductase (the DHT pathway linked to hair loss and BPH), with an RCT showing 40% increase in hair count at 24 weeks on 400mg/day pumpkin seed oil vs. 10% in placebo.

Renaissance Periodization

RP analyzed 231,741 mesocycles from their Hypertrophy app — a dataset with no real precedent in exercise science, where most studies run 20–50 subjects. The findings: men emphasize chest, back, biceps, triceps, and delts all roughly equally (~60–65% emphasis rate), but then fall off a cliff — forearms come in at 29%, and no lower body muscle breaks 23%. Women do the mirror image: glutes, quads, and hams cluster at 70–75% emphasis, then everything else (including most of upper body) clusters in the 25–30% range, with chest dead last at 16.6%. The editorial take from Mike Israetel: this reflects evolved primate signaling — men chasing upper-body dominance markers, women chasing lower-body fertility/health signals — and outside published literature (Mealey 1997; Baz-Valatal 2026 showing back led at 15–17 sets/week with hams last at ~8 sets) directionally confirms the pattern, just with far smaller samples. The practical implication they actually raise: occasional emphasis mesocycles on neglected muscle groups are probably worth trying, but training what you actually care about is also fine and is not a mistake. The separate short on Dorian Yates is a clean coaching point: Dorian trained hard AND with good technique — the people who copied his intensity without his form are the ones who needed shoulder replacements, not him; survivorship bias hides the casualties of sloppy high-intensity training.

🧬 Health & Science

NMR Blood Analysis: What One Blood Draw Can Actually Tell You

Peter Attia's conversation with Jim Otvos (creator of the NMR LDL-P test) covers what is genuinely a clinically underused tool. Standard lipid panels measure cholesterol concentration inside lipoprotein particles using chemical assays — they don't tell you how many particles there are or their size. NMR spectroscopy can simultaneously quantify LDL particle number, particle size (small dense LDL confers ~3x cardiovascular risk at a given LDL-C level vs. large LDL), HDL subfractions, VLDL, triglycerides, and — critically — markers of insulin resistance and inflammation, all from the same scan. Otvos's framing: a single NMR panel is more informative than any combination of standard biomarker panels for assessing total metabolic risk. The separate Attia note on coronary calcium scores is also worth internalizing: a CAC of zero reduces but does not eliminate cardiovascular risk, particularly in younger adults with chronically elevated LDL-C — the plaque may simply be non-calcified (soft plaque), which CAC scanning misses entirely.

Hypertrophy and Strength Gains: Is the Correlation Stronger Than We Thought?

A new Stronger by Science analysis revisits the relationship between muscle growth and strength gains. Prior research suggested the two were only weakly correlated (~0.20–0.30), implying neural adaptations dominate early strength development. The updated read: hypertrophy probably contributes more to strength gains in newer trainees than those studies suggested — but measurement error in older studies artificially suppressed the correlation, and the true relationship is still far from deterministic. Practical implication: training for hypertrophy and training for strength are more complementary than some programming debates acknowledge, especially in earlier training stages, but the relationship is nowhere near "size equals strength."

LLMs Reward Expertise

This piece makes a counterintuitive argument: AI tools don't flatten expertise — they amplify it. A novice using an LLM gets plausible-sounding output they can't evaluate; an expert gets a force multiplier because they can direct, critique, and validate what the model produces. The gap between expert and novice LLM users may be wider than the gap without AI, not narrower. Worth sitting with as a mental model for how to think about AI's labor market effects and your own skill development priorities.

On Contentment — Epictetus, Discourses 1.12

Pigliucci's close reading of Epictetus on contentment: the Stoic argument is not resignation but a precise distinction between what is "up to us" (our judgments, desires, values) and what is not (outcomes, reputation, circumstances).
