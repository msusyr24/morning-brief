GTO Wizard / GTO Lab

Two strong poker theory releases this week. GTO Lab's mystery bounty masterclass (with Nick Petrangelo, Daniel Dvoress, and Axel Hallay) makes the case that mystery bounties are not just "regular tournaments where you play a bit looser" — they're a fundamentally different game with their own strategic framework. The key variable is the KO-to-stack ratio: how large the bounty is relative to the target's stack. Early in a field of 1,000, ICM barely matters, chips are worth the least relative to the prize pool, and the dominant mistake is playing too tight and leaving bounty money on the table. They've built ~300 sims covering varying bounty sizes, stack depths, and player counts, with tools that break out EV from the prize pool vs. EV from the KO pool separately — so you can see which hands you're playing purely for the bounty. On the Jared Alderman side, his conversation with Uri Peleg cuts to something underappreciated: GTO poker assumes both players know each other's game trees, but in reality your opponent has no idea what your actual strategy is. Uri's point — that it only matters if you bluff too much or too little if your opponent is actually calling at the right frequency — means you can "fudge" frequencies across different board textures globally (over-bluff aces-high, under-bluff kings-high, etc.) and no live opponent can detect it without a large tracked sample. The practical upshot: unexploited deviation is free EV.

Anthony Chaffee MD

Justin Boyle ("Ultra Carnivore") has completed three 100-mile races and won a 37-mile trail race, all on essentially zero carbohydrates — fueling with salt, water, butter, and what he calls "carnivore ice cream" (heavy cream + protein powder, negligible carbs). He reports never experiencing the glycogen "bonk" that carb-dependent runners fear. Dr. Chaffee cites the McSweeny 2018 study of world-class keto ultramarathon and Ironman athletes, which found the ketogenic group outperformed the carbohydrate group on a 6-second sprint and critical power test at statistical significance — the two categories that supposedly require carbs most. The proposed mechanism: fat-adapted athletes maintain high fat oxidation up to ~86-90% VO2 max (vs. ~55% for carb athletes), producing enough fuel at that crossover point that no sport's sustained caloric demand (~2,000 cal/hr at the extreme) would exceed it. Chaffee also reframes "hitting the wall and getting a runner's high" in carb athletes as an accidental forced transition into fat burning — which keto athletes live in by default.

Lex Fridman

An emergency panel of three mathematicians (fluid dynamics, complexity theory, number theory) responds to a recent OpenAI drop of ~700 AI-generated mathematical results, including progress on Navier-Stokes, the quasi-Riemann hypothesis, the unique games conjecture, and 3SUM. The panel's nuanced consensus: the results appear to be in the "semigroup" of existing mathematics — not a linear combination of known ideas (the derogatory "convex hull"), but a vast and deep recombination that no human could have assembled given breadth and speed constraints. No genuinely new mathematical definitions or concepts appear to have emerged yet. The 3SUM result is field-altering for fine-grained complexity theory because it dissolves a hardness assumption an entire subfield was built around. The "doorman fallacy" gets invoked: reducing mathematics to theorem-proving misses what mathematicians actually do — asking the right questions, building theories, mentoring, finding beauty. The most practically urgent concern isn't AI replacing mathematicians but overwhelming the journal/peer-review system, which was already collapsing under volume before AI. Terence Tao is quoted calling it a disruption of "many person-years of ongoing patient research" by graduate students who can no longer explore spoiled problems freshly.

Nick Norwitz: Saccharin Disrupts Glucose Tolerance via Gut Microbiome

A human study published in Nature found that ~5 days of routine saccharin consumption (the sweetener in Sweet'N Low) worsened glucose tolerance in most of seven subjects — not by directly spiking blood sugar, but by altering the gut microbiome in a way that increased the blood sugar response to subsequent carbohydrate consumption. The proposed mechanism is called "gut glitch": the sweetener doesn't add calories but degrades the microbiome's ability to handle carbs. Stevia and allulose appear metabolically neutral to beneficial by comparison. Small study, but the mechanism is plausible and the nature of the effect (delayed, indirect) is exactly why it gets missed in short-term studies.

Thomas DeLauer: Early Signs of Insulin Resistance Are Mitochondrial Signals

DeLauer frames insulin resistance as downstream of mitochondrial dysfunction, not purely a sugar/carb problem — when mitochondria run poorly, every high-energy-demand tissue (skin, kidneys, eyes, muscles) underperforms at its personal weakest link. Specific early signs: skin tags and velvety dark patches (acanthosis nigricans correlate with elevated HOMA-IR), nocturia (kidneys dumping excess glucose hyperosmotically pull water into urine), persistent thirst despite hydration, fluctuating vision (lens shape changes with glucose swings), and inability to shift into glycolysis during high-intensity effort. Practical interventions with cited evidence: early time-restricted eating (a tightly controlled pre-diabetes trial improved insulin sensitivity and oxidative stress without weight loss), food sequencing (vegetables → protein → carbs reduces post-meal glucose and insulin in clinical study), and compound multi-muscle post-meal movement (muscle contraction pulls glucose without insulin, maintains insulin sensitivity for 24-48 hours).

Donald Robertson: How to Use Rational Emotive Imagery

Robertson explains Rational Emotive Imagery (REI) as a daily mental rehearsal practice derived from REBT — you deliberately imagine a distressing scenario, let yourself feel the negative emotion fully, then practice shifting to a healthier emotional response (not suppressing the feeling, but changing its quality). The goal is to gradually reprogram automatic emotional reactions to adversity. Practical technique with Stoic roots, relevant to emotional regulation under pressure.
