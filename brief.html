🧬 Health & Science

Peter Attia AMA #88: Metabolic Liver Health — Assessment, Early Detection, and Reversal

Attia's central claim: metabolic liver disease (NAFLD/MASLD) is frequently silent and underdiagnosed, but is reversible in its early stages — specifically, steatosis and early fibrosis can regress when metabolic drivers (hyperinsulinemia, excess fructose, visceral fat) are addressed. The key practical implication is early intervention: the earlier fibrosis is caught, the higher the probability of full regression. He also stresses that standard liver panels (ALT, AST) are poor early-warning tools and that more sensitive markers and imaging are needed for accurate risk stratification. Worth your time if you care about metabolic health or longevity — this is the kind of specific mechanistic and clinical content Attia does well.

Peppermint Oil Reduces Blood Pressure by 8.48 mmHg in Small Study

A PLOS ONE study found a mean systolic blood pressure reduction of 8.48 mmHg from peppermint oil — a surprisingly large effect size for a topical/aromatherapy intervention, rivaling some pharmaceutical interventions at low doses. The sample size is small, so treat this as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive, but the magnitude warrants attention given the near-zero risk profile of the intervention.

MS Paint and Photos Invisibly Watermark Even Locally Generated Output with a GUID

A researcher reverse-engineered MS Paint and the Windows Photos app and found they embed invisible, unique GUIDs in image output — including images generated or edited locally, with no network connection required. This is a privacy and forensics issue: your "local" edits are silently fingerprinted, potentially linking image provenance to a specific machine or user. The implications extend to anyone who assumes local processing means no tracking.

The Stoic Taxonomy of Anger — Donald Robertson

Robertson lays out how the Stoics — particularly Seneca — classified anger not as a single monolithic emotion but as a sequence of stages: the initial involuntary physical impression, the assent to that impression, and then the full emotional response. The therapeutic implication is precise: Stoic practice targets the moment of assent, which is the only stage under voluntary control. This framework maps well onto modern cognitive behavioral models and is directly applicable to emotional regulation under pressure.

How to Mentally Rehearse Coping — Donald Robertson

Part of Robertson's Stoic Mind miniseries, this piece covers the Stoic practice of negative visualization (premeditatio malorum) as a structured mental rehearsal technique — not for dwelling on the negative, but for pre-building emotional responses to adversity so they don't arrive as surprises. He gives concrete strategies: rehearsing specific scenarios, imagining your response, and habituating the feeling of coping before the event occurs. Directly relevant to anyone working on decision-making under pressure.

Octopus Intelligence May Be Related to a Never-Before-Seen Mutation

Researchers identified a novel genetic mutation in octopuses that appears to improve translational fidelity — the accuracy with which ribosomes build proteins from mRNA. The hypothesis is that this mutation, unique to cephalopods, may underpin their unusually complex nervous systems and cognitive abilities. It's an interesting counterpoint to the assumption that intelligence evolved primarily through genomic complexity; here the mechanism is quality control at the protein-synthesis level.
