Jared Alderman

Alderman interviews Nick, a 25-year-old high-stakes cash player who built and lost his bankroll multiple times before stabilizing at $20K–$40K games. The core thesis: what separates successful poker pros isn't intelligence or pattern recognition — it's the emotional capacity to absorb six months of losses and keep working. Nick Norwitz

Norwitz breaks down the "entourage effect" for caffeine: the same 100mg dose hits completely differently depending on the delivery vehicle because of co-traveling molecules. Coffee delivers free caffeine with rapid absorption (peak at 30–45 min), producing the classic jittery spike. Green tea and matcha add L-theanine, which promotes alpha-wave "relaxed alertness" and blunts the edge. Yerba mate uniquely contains all three major methylxanthines (caffeine, theobromine, theophylline) producing a slower, more layered stimulant curve — like kindling vs. a campfire. Dark chocolate is dominated by theobromine (3:1 ratio over caffeine), a milder cardiovascular stimulant that explains why you can eat a lot of it without feeling wired. Practical note: French press raises LDL by 10–20 mg/dL via diterpenes that paper filters trap. Celsius specifically inhibits COMT and MAO enzymes via EGCG and ginger, prolonging the adrenaline surge — useful for some, anxiety-inducing for stimulant-sensitive people.

Andrew Huberman on BPC-157 and Pinealon (via Modern Wisdom)

Huberman describes injecting 100–200mcg BPC-157 directly into a shoulder with several months of persistent pain — "gone, history, can't find the pain" — while acknowledging the absence of a saline control. More interesting is his pinealon (a brain-derived peptide, not pineal-derived despite the name) protocol: taken every 3–4 nights at ~0.1 IU, he reports 2.5–3 hours of REM sleep inside 6–7 hours, with the REM elevation persisting into subsequent nights even after cutting to once every 10 days. He explicitly flags that growth hormone secretagogues (tesamorelin, ipamorelin, CJC) consistently nuked his REM and spiked his PSA — a useful counterpoint to the common peptide-stack enthusiasm. All of this is anecdotal with no controls, but the REM data point is specific enough to be worth watching.

The Past Is Not a Defendant

Pigliucci argues that judging historical cultures by modern standards is both intellectually lazy and practically useless — the more valuable move is to understand the constraints and reasoning of earlier thinkers and extract what remains applicable. A direct application of the Stoic principle of focusing on what's in your control (learning from the past) vs. what isn't (changing it).
