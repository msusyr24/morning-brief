Worth your time:

Peter Attia

Attia makes a compelling case that healthspan pursuit is essentially a free twofer: everything you do to improve physical capacity, cognitive function, and emotional health also extends lifespan — and likely captures ~75% of the longevity benefit even if you never think about disease prevention directly. His core claim: low cardiorespiratory fitness carries mortality risk comparable to or greater than smoking, per a 2018 JAMA study. He uses the Tithonus myth to frame the goal clearly — you don't want more years in a declining body, you want your final decade to function like you're 20 years younger. Practical implication: optimizing VO2 max, strength, and healthspan markers IS the longevity strategy.

Videos: Why healthspan is a crucial component of longevity

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer argues walking is underrated as a fat-loss tool, citing a meta-analysis of ~50,000 people showing 5,800 steps/day reduces mortality risk by 40%, and that each additional 1,000 steps links to a 12% drop in all-cause mortality. The key mechanism he emphasizes: muscle contraction during walking pulls glucose from the bloodstream via GLUT4 translocation — bypassing insulin entirely — which is especially valuable for insulin-resistant people. At low intensity, a higher *percentage* of fuel comes from fat oxidation (vs. glycogen) compared to HIIT, and this advantage compounds over time as mitochondria adapt. He also cites a Lancet study of 1 million people showing 60–75 minutes of moderate daily activity *eliminates* the increased mortality risk from sitting 8+ hours. His practical prescription: under 60, aim for 8–10k steps; fasted morning walks with coffee maximize fat oxidation window.

Renaissance Periodization (Dr. Mike)

Dr. Mike critiques Chris Hemsworth's minimal-equipment travel workout with genuine exercise science specificity. Key points: high-rep sets (30–50 reps) taken close to failure do produce meaningful hypertrophy, just not quite as efficiently as sub-30-rep sets — relevant when you're stuck with light equipment. On the side-bend exercise, he flags a common mistake: holding equal weights in both hands cancels the oblique stimulus via counterbalancing; you need a unilateral load. He also clarifies that loaded carries (like Hemsworth's kettlebell carry) are excellent general fitness tools but rank poorly for hypertrophy specifically — strongmen use traditional bodybuilding movements for the physique work. The overarching message: when traveling, you don't need ideal, just *good enough* — high volume, controlled reps, close to failure.

Felix (who disclosed a testicular cancer diagnosis at under 40 despite healthy lifestyle) argues that health investment compounds exactly like financial investment — and the failures are similarly irreversible by the time they show up. His "index fund level agreement" health framework: VO2 max is the single most powerful longevity marker (low fitness = 2x all-cause mortality risk, comparable to smoking); strength training should be treated like retirement saving — build the largest possible base before the inevitable age-related drawdown (sarcopenia starts in your 30s); sleep under 6 hours is associated with elevated cardiac risk; and relationship quality is one of the strongest longevity predictors per the Harvard Study of Adult Development. 🧬 Health & Science

Fructose's Missing Stop Switch — and What It Means for Liver Health

Dr. Ekberg's liver video is worth flagging for one specific mechanistic point that's genuinely useful: glucose metabolism triggers a negative feedback loop via hexokinase → glucose-6-phosphate that tells the cell to slow processing. Fructose metabolism via fructokinase has no such feedback loop — the enzyme just keeps going. Additionally, fructose produces zero ghrelin suppression, meaning hunger is completely unaffected no matter how much fructose you consume. The practical implication: orange juice and apple juice contain roughly the same total sugar and fructose content as Coca-Cola (OJ ~90g sugar/L vs. Coke ~110g/L), making the "it's natural" framing misleading. Background data point: 88–93% of metabolically unhealthy Americans likely have some degree of fatty liver infiltration, given the dose-dependent relationship between insulin resistance and hepatic fat.

AlphaGenome Maps 9 Billion DNA Variants

Google DeepMind's AlphaGenome model maps the functional effects of 9 billion DNA variants — essentially predicting what each genetic variant does in the body. This is the kind of scale that makes genome-wide association studies and personalized medicine meaningfully more tractable. The practical downstream value: understanding which variants actually alter gene expression or protein function, versus those that are neutral, which has been the bottleneck in translating genetic data into clinical insight.

Navier-Stokes Announcement

The Clay Mathematics Institute has made an announcement regarding the Navier-Stokes equations — one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems ($1M prize each), which asks whether smooth solutions to the equations governing fluid flow always exist or can break down in finite time. This problem has been unsolved for over 150 years and is foundational to fluid dynamics, weather modeling, and turbulence theory. The announcement is worth watching if it constitutes a claimed solution or significant progress.

Stoicism versus Daoism

Donald Robertson compares the two philosophies — both traditions emphasize living in accordance with nature and reducing attachment to externals, but differ on agency: Stoicism is fundamentally about active rational will and cultivating virtue through effort, while Daoism leans toward effortless non-striving (wu wei). Robertson likely argues these are more complementary than opposed. A useful conceptual map if you're interested in how Stoic discipline relates to acceptance-based frameworks.
