🧬 Health & Science

Sinclair Lab: OSK Gene Therapy in Human Clinical Trial

The first human age-reversal trial is actively dosing patients with an OSK gene cocktail (three Yamanaka factors delivered via AAV-like capsules) targeting glaucoma and NAION (sudden blindness). The mechanism: OSK removes aberrant DNA methylation marks that accumulate with aging, resetting the epigenetic "clock" without dedifferentiating cells — in mice, this restored vision, regrew optic nerve fibers, improved cognition and memory, and reversed fatty liver disease. Sinclair separately reports that AI-enabled computational docking screened a trillion drug candidates in two months to find 200 molecules that may replicate OSK's effects orally, now entering cell-based testing.

LDL Particle Size: "Fluffy LDL" Is Not Protective

Jim Otvos (inventor of NMR LipoProfile) states clearly that large LDL particle size does not confer cardiovascular protection. Key evidence: FH patients — who suffer fatal heart attacks in their 30s-40s — have predominantly large LDL particles. The actual risk variable is LDL particle number (ApoB), not size. The "pattern A vs. pattern B" framing, often used to dismiss high LDL-P, is described as "completely specious."

Protein Restriction in Kidney Disease: Outdated Evidence?

Peter Attia's piece argues the standard advice to restrict protein for CKD patients was established before modern nephrology management (dialysis quality, ACE inhibitors, GFR staging) and that current evidence does not support blanket restriction for most patients — and may actively harm them by accelerating sarcopenia. The post reviews the mechanistic and clinical literature distinguishing true progression risk from the historical assumptions.

Resistant Starch and Visceral Fat

Nick Norwitz cites double-blind RCT data showing that resistant starch (RS) — found in green bananas, available as supplement powder — reduces visceral fat even in calorie-controlled conditions. The mechanism: RS bypasses small intestine digestion, feeds colonic microbiota, which produce short-chain fatty acids that signal systemic metabolic changes including visceral adiposity reduction. The effect appears independent of total caloric intake.
