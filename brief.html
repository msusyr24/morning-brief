Mental Model · Economics/Productivity

Pareto Principle (80/20)

Roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes, across almost every domain. 80% of sales from 20% of customers, 80% of crime from 20% of criminals, 80% of your happiness from 20% of your activities. The skill: identifying the 20% before wasting effort on the 80%.

🧬 Health & Science

Clonal Hematopoiesis: The Cardiovascular Risk Factor You've Never Heard Of

Nick Norwitz highlights emerging research on clonal hematopoiesis — where mutant bone marrow cells accumulate with age, migrate to arterial tissue, and drive chronic inflammation linked to heart attack risk. The mechanism is distinct from cholesterol: it's an immune dysfunction originating in the bone marrow. Two evidence-based interventions shown to slow it: regular exercise and adequate sleep. Worth tracking as this enters clinical cardiology risk assessment.

Nick Norwitz — The #1 Heart Factor They Don't Teach in Med School

Dr. Chris Palmer: Ketogenic Diet Remitting Treatment-Resistant Psychosis

Palmer describes a patient with schizoaffective disorder — 17 failed medications, daily hallucinations, paranoid delusions for years — who experienced near-complete remission of psychotic symptoms on a ketogenic diet within 6–8 weeks, with no medication changes. His mechanistic hypothesis: many psychiatric disorders involve mitochondrial dysfunction, and ketosis corrects the same energy-sensing pathways (AMPK, PGC-1α) that are impaired. He targets blood ketones >1.5 mmol for psychotic/bipolar disorders and >0.8 mmol for depression, and stresses this should be done with medical supervision alongside (not instead of) existing treatment.

Huberman Lab — Diet & Nutrition for Mental Health ft. Dr. Chris Palmer

Peter Attia: Liver as the Canary for Metabolic Dysfunction

Attia's AMA makes a strong case that MASLD (formerly NAFLD) affects an estimated 38% of the world's adult population and that the leading cause of death in liver disease patients isn't liver failure — it's cardiovascular disease, because a metabolically stressed liver overproduces ApoB-containing particles and amplifies systemic insulin resistance. The four-stage progression (metabolic stress → steatosis → MASH → fibrosis) is largely reversible until the fibrosis stage, which is the actual mortality predictor. His key mechanistic point: selective hepatic insulin resistance means the liver simultaneously keeps releasing glucose into a high-blood-sugar environment AND keeps producing fat — a particularly destructive combination that conventional "insulin resistance" framing undersells.
