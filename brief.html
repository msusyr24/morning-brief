Jared Alderman — Online crushers vs. live pros

Alderman's central thesis: live players make exploitative deviations all the time (folding what is a solver jam, for instance) but have no calibrated sense of how large the deviation is. He walks through a hand — HJ opens A8s in a $1/$3 game, flops top two on A-K-3-8 rainbow, bets, gets min-raised on the turn, and folds — where the solver says calling was worth roughly +$50 in EV. The live player's justification ("I'm pretty sure he has a set") isn't wrong as a read, but even if villain has a set 75% of the time, the pot odds and remaining equity (boat outs) still make folding a significant mistake. Jared's broader point: live players use "I have a read" as a blanket excuse to skip math entirely, which quietly bleeds money in spots where the read is correct but the price still demands a call.

Fasting insulin as an early warning biomarker (Ken Berry)

A fasting insulin above 15 µIU/mL can identify future type 2 diabetics 10-15 years before fasting glucose or A1C becomes abnormal — because insulin resistance raises insulin first, while glucose is still compensated. The same marker is an early signal for fatty liver, hypertension, and metabolic syndrome. This is a clinically actionable point most people aren't getting from routine labs, since fasting insulin is not part of standard panels and many doctors don't order it.

Your blood sugar can look "normal" while trouble is already brewing — Ken Berry MD

Baking soda removes up to 71% of dental plaque in a single brushing (Thomas DeLauer)

A five-trial pooled analysis published in the Journal of the American Dental Association (270+ patients) found baking soda toothpastes outperformed regular toothpaste significantly on whole-mouth plaque removal, with a dose-response relationship — more baking soda, more effect. Three simultaneous mechanisms: mild abrasion lifts biofilm, alkalinity neutralizes bacterial acid output, and at high enough concentration it has direct antibacterial action. Xylitol acts as a metabolic Trojan horse — streptococcus mutans uptakes it as if it were sugar, then gets trapped in an energy-wasting dead-end loop that halts acid production; however, resistance can build, so intermittent use (a few times per week) is better than daily. Conventional antiseptic mouthwash (chlorhexidine) kills the nitrate-reducing tongue bacteria responsible for oral and systemic nitric oxide production; 7 days of use measurably raises salivary lactate and systemic blood pressure.

This Removes 71% of Plaque in a Single Brushing — Thomas DeLauer

Thyroid supplement fraud: actual hormones found in OTC products (Peter Attia)

Chromatography analysis of 10 commercial "thyroid support" supplements found 9 of 10 contained T3 and 5 of 10 contained T4 — several at doses exceeding standard prescription starting doses — with none of the products disclosing hormone content on the label. Documented cases of thyrotoxicity and a fatal outcome linked to hormone-adulterated weight-loss supplements exist in the literature. Kelp-based supplements independently trigger hyperthyroidism in people with autonomous nodules by flooding the system with iodine.

Risks associated with thyroid supplements — Peter Attia MD

Carnivore/lion diet and MS: a notable case and the lectin hypothesis (Anthony Chaffee)

Chaffee describes a patient with MS who followed a strict lion diet (beef, lamb, water only) for 8 months while optimizing B12, D3, vitamin A, omega-3s, and ketosis — resulting in near-complete symptom resolution and a 40% reduction in lesion size on MRI, which her neurologist described as unprecedented. The mechanistic hypothesis he invokes (argued since the 1980s) is that plant lectins bind to nerve tissue and trigger immune crossfire, and that removing dietary lectins halts the autoimmune attack. He also notes that optimal B12 reference ranges in the literature (800–1,200 pmol/L) often exceed the upper end of standard lab ranges, making "normal" results meaningless for this population.
