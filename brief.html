🎥 From People I Follow

Worth your time:

Andrew Huberman

Huberman's conversation with Arthur Brooks on happiness is one of the more substantive frameworks I've seen on this topic: Brooks argues happiness has three macronutrients — enjoyment, satisfaction, and meaning — and that most people fail because they conflate pleasure with enjoyment. The key distinction: pleasure is limbic (dopamine hit, solo, addictive), while enjoyment requires people, memory, and conscious management. Critically, Brooks argues unhappiness is inside each macronutrient — suffering is not the opposite of happiness but a component of it, and trying to eliminate suffering paradoxically kills happiness. Separately, Huberman's conversation with Rogan covers a framework worth noting: the dynamic tension between bottom-up impulses (hypothalamus, appetite, scroll, react) and top-down suppression (prefrontal cortex) is the central variable in being an effective human — and it can be trained through "micro-sucks" all day, not just big hard workouts. Huberman also shares a striking personal thread: multiple mentors, colleagues, and friends have died young (cancer, suicide, overdose), leading him to conclude that knowledge alone does not protect us — the ability to regulate the bottom-up/top-down tension does.

Videos: The Art of True Happiness | Dr. Arthur Brooks · Why You're Tired, Stressed & Unfocussed · Joe Rogan Experience #2553

Nick Norwitz

Norwitz did a rigorous 30-day lion diet (meat, salt, water only) and tracked biomarkers throughout. Key findings: no constipation — bowel frequency was actually better, consistent with a 2012 RCT showing fiber elimination improved IBS-C from one BM per 3.75 days to daily, with a dose-dependent worsening effect from more fiber. His omega-3 index dropped substantially (15.68% → 10.51%) in just 30 days with no seafood — a legitimate concern he'd address with supplementation on any repeat. The vitamin C paradox: glucose and vitamin C compete for the same cellular transporters, so eliminating sugar increases vitamin C absorption efficiency; ketone production may also support NADPH-mediated vitamin C regeneration. For gut conditions specifically, fiber-free diets achieve 60-85% response rates in treatment-resistant IBD — a real clinical tool almost nobody knows about because it's administered as liquid formula rather than meat. His overall verdict: carnivore is not a panacea but has genuine mechanistic and clinical support for specific gut and metabolic conditions, and the "fading trend" narrative misses a more nuanced picture.

Video: The Strange Science of Eating Only Meat

Peter Attia (Podcast + YouTube)

Attia's conversation with lipidologist Michael Davidson makes the case for primordial prevention with notable specificity: the "8-gram rule" — 200 mg/dL LDL × 40 years = 8 grams cumulative cholesterol exposure, which is the threshold associated with plaque formation. Keep LDL below 80 mg/dL lifelong and data suggest you simply don't get heart disease. Davidson's own story is a compelling n=1: two brothers with identical bad lipids at age 14, one started statins in med school, the other relied on strict vegetarian diet and started statins much later — the second needed bypass at 44. Their shared frustration: LDL is uniquely treated as a "lifestyle problem" despite being as causally established as hypertension or smoking, neither of which we wait on. Discussion also covers CETP inhibition and a new class of drugs (Davidson is CEO of New Amsterdam Pharmaceuticals in this space).

🧬 Health & Science

LDL Causality and the 8-Gram Rule — Peter Attia / Michael Davidson

The most actionable framing from Davidson's interview: cumulative LDL exposure is what drives atherosclerosis, and the threshold that matters is roughly 8 grams lifetime (200 mg/dL × 40 years = 8g; 80 mg/dL × 100 years = 8g). Data suggests keeping LDL below 80 mg/dL lifelong essentially eliminates ASCVD risk. The clinical frustration: we don't wait to treat hypertension or diabetes until organ damage appears, but we apply exactly that logic to LDL despite equally strong causal evidence from Mendelian randomization studies. Davidson's personal comparison — brother on vegetarian diet, no statins until 40s, needed bypass at 44 vs. Davidson on statins since med school — is a clean n=1 illustration of the decade-of-compounding argument.

Carnivore Diet: Gut Health Mechanisms and Nutrient Deficiency Data — Nick Norwitz

Beyond the observational claims, Norwitz covers several specific mechanisms worth tracking: (1) Ketone bodies in the gut reprogram immune cells toward anti-inflammatory states and may support stem cell renewal of gut lining. (2) A Cell Host & Microbe study found fiber-free diets starve off Mucispirillum, a pathological gut bacterium associated with dysbiosis — the "starve the bad guys" mechanism has top-tier journal support. (3) Fiber-free elemental diets already achieve 60-85% response rates in treatment-resistant IBD in clinical settings — a fact largely unknown because the modality is administered as formula, not food. (4) Microbiome diversity: a study comparing 10 long-term carnivore dieters (avg. 3 years) against 874 matched controls found no significant difference in diversity metrics — challenging the assumed fiber-diversity link. Specific deficiency concern: 30 days of lion diet dropped his omega-3 index from 15.68% to 10.51% with no seafood; calcium is also a real gap on dairy-free carnivore (beef provides ~10-20mg/100g vs. RDA of 1,000-1,200mg).

Happiness Macronutrients — Arthur Brooks via Huberman Lab

Brooks's framework draws on factor analysis of high life-satisfaction populations: enjoyment (pleasure + people + memory + conscious management), satisfaction (joy from accomplishment after struggle), and meaning (the why of existence). The critical non-obvious finding: unhappiness is inside each macronutrient — struggling to accomplish something is part of satisfaction, not its enemy. Brooks draws a neuroscience-backed distinction between pleasure (limbic, dopamine, animal) and enjoyment (prefrontal, social, executive) — if you're doing something pleasurable alone and it's becoming compulsive, it's pleasure not enjoyment. The practical implication for high-achievers: strivers often optimize for "points on the board" at the expense of enjoyment, and the evidence suggests this is a structural error, not a personality quirk.

Astral Codex Ten

People who can't picture anything are rewriting the science of imagination

Aphantasia — the inability to form mental images — is forcing researchers to revise how they model imagination and memory, since people with the condition can still recall facts, navigate, and dream without any visual mental content. The finding challenges assumptions that visual imagery is necessary for higher-order cognition, with implications for understanding creativity, memory, and how widely brain function can vary while still being "normal."
