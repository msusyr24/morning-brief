Incentive-Caused Bias

Munger on FedEx: the company couldn't get packages sorted on time until they stopped paying workers by the hour and started paying by the shift. Workers had been incentivized to drag out the job. Once the incentive flipped, the bottleneck vanished overnight. Whenever a system produces strange behavior, ask: what is it paying for?

Renaissance Periodization

Mike Israetel highlights a term coined by Jay Cutler: the "feel set." Distinct from a warm-up set (you're already warm), a feel set is a lighter preparatory set when switching to a mechanically similar but technically distinct movement — e.g., going from incline barbell to incline dumbbell press. The purpose is purely neurological: locking in the specific motor pattern before going heavy. Short clip but a genuinely useful training concept that most people do intuitively without naming it.

🧬 Health & Science

Protein Dosing: The Case Against the 1g/lb Dogma

Thomas DeLauer's conversation surfaces an important mechanistic argument: Don Layman's own muscle protein synthesis research shows that roughly 50-60% of MPS is driven by training stimulus — protein is secondary. The more interesting claim is the "anabolic resistance" reframe: rather than simply accepting that older people need more protein because of age-related resistance, the hypothesis is that chronically high protein intake may itself be driving resistance by never allowing the receptor signaling system to resensitize — analogous to insulin resistance from chronic hyperinsulinemia. The 73-year-old guest (Mark) trains fasted, eats in a compressed window, averages ~110-120g protein/day, and reports maintained muscle mass — a useful anecdotal data point against the "you must hit 150-170g at your age" prescription.

Fructose Metabolism and Liver Damage: The Key Mechanisms

Ekberg provides a clean mechanistic explanation for why fructose is uniquely damaging: glucose metabolism uses hexokinase with end-product negative feedback (a natural shutoff); fructose uses fructokinase with no shutoff and no ghrelin suppression, meaning hunger is unaffected regardless of intake. The result is a one-way ratchet toward overconsumption and hepatic fat deposition. His comparison of orange juice, apple juice, Coca-Cola, and Mountain Dew on a sugar-per-liter basis is striking — apple juice (~110g sugar, ~60g fructose/L) is essentially equivalent to Coke, and the framing of "fruit juice as health food" is biochemically incoherent at those quantities.

Peter Attia: Healthspan and Lifespan Are Nearly Identical Targets

Attia's core claim deserves attention: optimizing healthspan metrics (VO2 max, strength, balance, cognitive processing speed, emotional regulation) is likely to capture ~75% of the maximum achievable lifespan benefit even if you never think about longevity directly. The implication is that the framing of "longevity medicine" as something separate from "performing and feeling well in your 50s and 60s" is mostly a distinction without a difference — the interventions are the same, and the lifespan benefit comes along for free.

Peter Attia: Nearsightedness and Screens

Attia's newsletter piece covers the myopia epidemic — the question is whether screen time itself, near-work generally, or lack of outdoor light exposure (which triggers dopamine release in the retina that regulates eye growth) is the primary driver. The outdoor light hypothesis has the strongest mechanistic support: even modest daily outdoor time appears to slow axial elongation of the eye in children, independent of screen time. Worth reading if you have kids.

Death, Love, Stoicism — Donald Robertson

Robertson writes on how the Stoics — particularly Marcus Aurelius — used the contemplation of death not morbidly but as a tool for clarifying love and attachment. The Stoic view is that acknowledging impermanence sharpens rather than diminishes love; "memento mori" and "amor fati" are two sides of the same coin. Brief and worth sitting with.
