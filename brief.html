Mental Model · Economics

Marginal Utility

Each additional unit of something is usually worth less than the previous. The first slice of pizza beats the tenth. Applied: diminishing returns govern almost every effort — study, exercise, relationships. At some point, more input produces less output. Know where that point is for your main endeavors.

GTO Wizard / GTO Lab / Jared Alderman

Jared Alderman makes a point that's easy to miss: the real value of studying solvers isn't memorizing the theoretical strategy, it's understanding the EV gap between decisions. When two actions are separated by a massive EV difference, your live-game reads almost never justify deviating — he illustrated this with a client who folded two pair facing a near-certain set, then node-locked the sim at 98% sets and showed a call was still correct because the price made it +EV regardless. GTO Lab demonstrates a complementary concept: blockers can flip a thin hand into a bet under ICM pressure, specifically showing how holding the king of spades (blocking the nut flush) makes a marginal king-high hand "extremely betterable" when combined with a heart blocker. The GTO Wizard short on Bryn Kenney vs. Adrian Mateos explores how elite players deviate from solver optima and whether those deviations are justified.

Videos: Jared Alderman: What to learn from solvers · GTO Lab: Blockers under ICM · GTO Wizard: When Pros Deviate

Peter Attia

Attia does a framework episode on peptides, and it's actually useful as a general drug-evaluation template. His five questions: Is there a viable mechanism? Is there human evidence of meaningful benefit? Do we understand safety, dosing, and PK? Does benefit justify risk for this person? Is there a better-characterized alternative? He then runs BPC-157 through this framework and finds it fails on nearly every count: the alleged parent protein has never been fully characterized, the discoverer deliberately withholds the screening method, 80%+ of positive studies come from one academic group with IP interests, there are zero published human RCTs after three decades, and the pro-angiogenic mechanisms proposed (VEGF, nitric oxide) should actually increase concern rather than decrease it for anyone worried about tumor growth. He also revisits protein restriction and kidney function, noting the case for limiting protein was built on older nephrology assumptions that don't hold up well under modern scrutiny.

Videos: Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing hype · RSS: Peptides AMA · Protein and renal function

Stronger by Science (RSS)

A new analysis revisits the correlation between hypertrophy and strength gains. The finding: hypertrophy probably contributes more to strength in newer lifters than prior studies suggested, but the relationship is nowhere near as clean as it looks at first glance — the correlation is inflated by confounders and methodological artifacts. Practical implication: you can't assume that because muscle is growing, strength will follow proportionally, especially in trained individuals.

🧬 Health & Science

Taxi Drivers Rarely Die of Alzheimer's — Spatial Reasoning as Neuroprotection

A piece in The Conversation (226 HN points) covers research showing taxi drivers — who build and continuously use complex mental maps — have unusually low rates of Alzheimer's. The mechanism proposed is that sustained spatial reasoning and hippocampal activation creates structural changes that provide meaningful cognitive reserve. This dovetails with the egg/Alzheimer's data from Saladino's short (39,000-person study, 27%+ risk reduction from 1+ egg/day) — both pointing toward modifiable inputs with outsized effects on dementia risk.

Peter Attia: BPC-157 Fails Every Criterion for a Legitimate Peptide

Attia's framework-based takedown of BPC-157 is the clearest synthesis I've seen on why this peptide specifically warrants skepticism despite wide use: no characterized parent protein, deliberately withheld discovery methodology, 80%+ of studies from one conflicted lab, zero human RCTs in 30 years, unknown human PK and bioavailability, and pro-angiogenic mechanisms that should increase cancer-risk concern rather than dismiss it. His bucket system (unsupported / plausible but unproven / scientifically legitimate) is a reusable scaffold for evaluating any compound someone recommends.

Nick Norwitz: Microbiome Cocktail Replicates 600-Calorie/Day Metabolic Boost

Three separate controlled human trials found low-protein diets increased total energy expenditure by ~20% (~600 cal/day). Researchers traced the mechanism to gut microbiome shifts that cause fat cells to "leak" energy as heat rather than store it (increased thermogenesis). They then identified the specific microbiome signature and synthesized a four-microbe probiotic cocktail that replicates the effect in animal models — a potential "calorie-burning probiotic" now in early development. Replication in humans at scale is the outstanding question.
