🎥 From People I Follow

Worth your time:

Nick Norwitz

Norwitz breaks down the fasting mimicking diet (FMD) — a protocol of roughly 700–1,100 calories/day, low protein, fat-primary, for 5 consecutive days, cycled 3x per year (15 total days). A 2024 Nature Communications study found participants became biologically younger by a median 2.5 years on a validated blood-based aging score (albumin, creatinine, CRP, HbA1c, blood pressure) — and critically, the reduction in biological age was NOT associated with how much weight people lost, suggesting metabolic reprogramming beyond simple caloric deficit. Additional findings: liver fat dropped from 14% to 8% in participants with fatty liver (>40% relative reduction), immune aging markers improved, and a monkey study showed lower bodyweight set points persisting 1,000+ days after the protocol ended. The two key mechanistic levers are (1) caloric restriction with fat as primary fuel, forcing ketosis, and (2) protein restriction, which lowers IGF-1 and suppresses mTOR — the cell's growth switch. Norwitz is careful to note this is about periodicity, not chronic restriction, and that it differs fundamentally from just "eating less."

Video: This Diet Reverses Aging in 15 Days

GTO Lab (Nick Petrangelo, Daniel Dvoress & Axel Hallay)

The core argument: mystery bounty tournaments are not "regular tournaments with slightly wider ranges" — they're a structurally different game, and most players massively underadjust. Axel Hallay introduces the KO-to-stack ratio as the key governing variable: bounty value divided by villain's current stack, which drives preflop and postflop strategy throughout. GTO Lab built a library of ~300 sims specifically for mystery bounty spots, and the EV breakdown tool separates prize-pool EV (green) from bounty EV (yellow) — many early-stage hands show pure yellow EV, meaning you're calling/jamming purely for the knockout with zero expected prize pool gain, which almost no player accounts for. The most common leak identified: players VPIP too tightly at the bounty-on stage because they're pattern-matching to vanilla tournament play, leaving significant money on the table.

Video: Mystery Bounty Is a Completely Different Game

Jared Alderman (with Uri Peleg)

Uri Peleg makes a genuinely interesting point about hidden information in poker: a player doesn't need to be perfectly balanced — they only need to be balanced enough that the opponent can't detect the imbalance. If you over-bluff ace-high boards and under-bluff king-high boards but are roughly correct globally, there's no practical way for opponents to discover this. He frames GTO play using a chess analogy: each player has an "invisible chessboard" (range), and GTO is setting up your pieces optimally given your opponent can see your board but not your plan — while exploitative play means making an informed guess about how they've set up theirs and countering. Alderman's underlying thesis: people dramatically overestimate how precisely opponents track your frequencies in real-time, which means well-disguised population-level exploits can be highly +EV.

Video: "Poker Doesn't Work Without Exploits" - Uri Peleg

Lex Fridman (with mathematicians Steven Brunton, Ankur Moitra & Alex)

This is an emergency episode convened after OpenAI released proofs of ~700 mathematical results including progress on Navier-Stokes, a quasi-Riemann Hypothesis result, and the Unique Games Conjecture — problems that would constitute instant Fields Medal territory if done by humans. The panel's sharpest distinction: the AI results appear to be in the "semigroup" of known mathematical moves (combining known techniques in long, surprising chains) rather than generating genuinely new definitions or concepts — but the semigroup reach is far larger than previously thought, which is itself shocking. Ankur Moitra raises the "doorman fallacy": reducing mathematics to theorem-proving is like reducing a doorman's job to door-opening — the actual value of mathematicians lies in asking the right questions, building conceptual frameworks, and mentoring, none of which has been displaced. The deepest concern isn't about senior researchers but about PhD students, whose traditional blueprint (pick a hard problem, improve it, make a name) has been destabilized overnight; Terence Tao is quoted calling it a "casual disruption" of years of patient graduate research.

Video: AI Crisis in Math: Navier-Stokes, Riemann Hypothesis & Complexity Theory | Lex Fridman #503

🧬 Health & Science

Peter Attia: What Labs Actually Tell You (and What They Don't)

Key framing from AMA #91: even a comprehensive blood panel gives you roughly 25–30% of the information needed to build a complete risk picture. Attia's specific concern with direct-to-consumer lab testing is that it amplifies a false belief — "if nothing is flagged outside reference ranges, I'm fine" — when reference ranges are population-based norms, not individualized risk thresholds. His clinical approach structures health through the lens of the major longevity threats: cardiovascular disease, cancer, neurodegeneration, metabolic disease, and accidental death on the lifespan side; physical capacity, cognitive function, and emotional health on the healthspan side. He emphasizes longitudinal data over snapshots — a single CRP of 3 could be post-vaccine noise or chronic systemic inflammation, and without history you cannot distinguish them.

Source: Peter Attia AMA #91 · YouTube preview

Peter Attia: Less Protein, Longer Life? The Protein Paradox

Attia's team investigated the tension between high-protein recommendations for muscle/longevity and research suggesting protein restriction extends lifespan (via IGF-1/mTOR suppression — the same mechanism as the fasting mimicking diet above). Worth reading alongside the Norwitz FMD piece: both touch on the same mechanistic question of when mTOR suppression is beneficial versus when adequate protein is essential. The resolution likely involves periodicity — cycling between anabolic and catabolic states rather than chronic restriction or chronic surplus.

Your TV Can See Through Walls

A technical project demonstrating that modern smart TVs emit enough RF signal that they can be used — inadvertently or deliberately — to detect movement through walls via signal reflection analysis, essentially turning a consumer device into a passive radar. The implications for home privacy are real: a device most people consider passive and display-only has enough radio presence to function as a motion sensor for an external observer.

Semi-Transparent Magenta Solar Panels in Agrivoltaics

Cadmium telluride solar panels tuned to absorb green light (which crops use less efficiently) while transmitting the red and blue wavelengths plants need most — allowing crops to grow underneath active solar panels with minimal yield loss. The magenta tint is a byproduct of the specific wavelengths absorbed, and the dual land use (power generation + agriculture on the same footprint) addresses one of the core land-competition critiques of large-scale solar deployment.
