Mental Model · Math/Statistics

Law of Large Numbers

Over many trials, averages converge to expected values — but in the short run, anything can happen. Casinos know this; gamblers forget it. Applied to life: don't judge strategies by single outcomes. A good decision can have a bad result, and vice versa.

GTO Wizard

A sharp walkthrough of GTO Wizard's new preflop ICM solver, illustrated with a real final-table hand: a player with ~30bb opens QJss in the cutoff, the chip leader (84bb) 3-bets to 7.5bb, and the player jams — costing themselves roughly $60K in equity. The solver confirms it was a punt. The more interesting finding: even if you model the chip leader as a "bully" profile (3-betting 5% more than GTO), QJs still doesn't become a profitable jam — the better adjustment is to simply not open in the first place and avoid the high-variance confrontation entirely. The video also covers node-locking: when the cutoff is forced to have a calling range, the chip leader's preferred response shifts toward shoving rather than raising to 7.5bb, since the larger 3-bet size loses more when folded to a shove than the smaller one does.

Jared Alderman

Interview with a 25-year-old high-stakes online reg (playing up to $40K on Coin/ACR) who built his roll twice after going broke. His core thesis on what separates pros from washouts: it's not pattern recognition or game theory knowledge — it's emotional tolerance for prolonged losing. He specifically calls out that losing for 6 months while continuing to show up is simply not a skill the general population has, and that high-volume grinders who can't handle deep thinking fatigue will flame out even with strong fundamentals. He also notes a common misconception about high-stakes pools: it's not all elite regs; there are often bum-hunters and weak spots, meaning a solid but not exceptional player can still have a strong win rate if they're disciplined about game selection.

GTO Lab

Concise conceptual breakdown of range advantage vs. nuts advantage: equity advantage tells you who should be betting, but nuts advantage is what allows large bet sizes. If you lack nuts advantage, large bets are generally unexploitable — you simply cannot support them even if your overall equity is ahead. Worth bookmarking as a clean mental model for bet sizing decisions.

Thomas DeLauer

Structured breakdown of sweetener data starting from a 103,000-person French cohort (BMJ, NutriNet-Santé) followed for 9 years: overall artificial sweetener intake linked to 9% higher CVD risk and 18% higher cerebrovascular risk; aspartame specifically to 17% higher cerebrovascular disease; ace-K to 40% higher coronary artery disease; sucralose to 31% higher CHD risk. He covers three mechanisms — gut microbiome disruption (2014 Nature study showing microbiota transfer induced glucose intolerance), sweet taste receptor mismatch causing cephalic insulin response (sucralose group showed 20% higher peak glucose and 22% greater peak insulin vs. water in a Diabetes Care study), and a 2025 Cell Metabolism animal study linking aspartame to vascular inflammation via fractalkine/endothelial signaling. The one sweetener he argues actually improves metabolic outcomes dose-dependently: allulose — a rare monosaccharide structurally similar to fructose but handled differently, which in a randomized crossover study blunted both postprandial glucose and insulin in a dose-dependent fashion via partial sucrase inhibition and competitive transport at GLUT5/GLUT2. His hierarchy: artificial sweeteners as occasional tools, stevia and monk fruit as solid alternatives with real mechanistic data, erythritol probably fine but uncertain, allulose as the metabolically active option to actually use with carbs.

Two distinct pieces. The drug development episode with Lloyd Klickstein lays out the full economic reality of pharma: a new drug costs and requires as many people as building the Burj Khalifa, and patent law gives only 10–15 years of effective commercial exclusivity from launch (not the full 20-year patent term) — a fact that DeLorean underlies the pricing pressure that dominates the industry.
