Nick Norwitz

Norwitz made a nuanced point about diet soda: it's not that a single can spikes blood sugar or insulin (it doesn't), but that chronic exposure to artificial sweeteners reshapes the gut microbiome in a way that induces insulin resistance in a majority of people. He cited "landmark data including in humans" showing that a week of artificial sweetener consumption caused more than half of subjects to develop insulin resistance — and critically, when gut microbiome samples from those people were transferred to animals, those animals also developed insulin resistance, implicating the microbiome as the mechanism. The practical takeaway: occasional is fine, habitual is not.

Videos: What Everyone Gets Wrong About Diet Soda

Peter Attia

Attia's thyroid AMA is substantive and worth the time if you've ever been told your TSH is "normal." His core thesis: thyroid management is a Goldilocks system where both over- and under-treatment are genuinely harmful, and there are two failure modes in medicine — missing real hypothyroidism because TSH falls within a wide "normal" range despite symptoms, positive antibodies, and family history; and overtreating borderline cases under "functional medicine" frameworks that treat subclinical findings as definitive. He specifically warned that over-treating with thyroid hormone can cause cognitive symptoms, atrial fibrillation, and bone loss. His recommended approach: multiple data sources (labs + symptoms + clinical context), not lab values in isolation. He also noted that symptoms like fatigue are so non-specific that blinded studies couldn't distinguish hypothyroid from euthyroid patients by symptoms alone.

Videos: Thyroid health: interpreting symptoms, diagnosing & treating dysfunction, & navigating the gray zone

Renaissance Periodization

Quick but useful clarification on chest training: pausing at peak contraction in fly movements probably doesn't meaningfully grow the inner chest. The stretch stimulus drives more hypertrophy (at the outer/distal part of the muscle), and more stretch also produces better overall hypertrophy than peak contraction holds. The practical implication: prioritize stretch-focused chest work, and use peak-contraction holds sparingly if at all.

🧬 Health & Science

Semaglutide and Aging — Peter Attia

A new study found that semaglutide extended median lifespan and improved several aging biomarkers in female mice. Attia's framing is cautious: the headline requires careful interpretation (female mice only, median not maximum lifespan, mechanism unclear — could be weight loss itself rather than a drug-specific effect). Worth watching as a data point in the GLP-1 / longevity space, but not yet evidence that these drugs "slow aging" in any meaningful mechanistic sense.

Peter Attia MD

Thyroid Gray Zone — How Practitioners Miss in Both Directions

From Attia's AMA (see also YouTube summary above): the specific clinical failure modes are worth noting separately. Under-treatment: dismissing a patient with a TSH of 4.2 (technically "normal") when they have a patchy gland, positive thyroid antibodies, and family history. Over-treatment: functional medicine practitioners treating elevated reverse T3 as proof of tissue hypothyroidism regardless of TSH/T4 levels, then building elaborate "root cause" narratives (gut health, adrenal fatigue) around findings that often normalize spontaneously. Attia's data point: a large share of mildly elevated TSH resolves on repeat testing without intervention, while unnecessary treatment with thyroid hormone carries real risks (cognitive symptoms, A-fib, bone loss).

Pigliucci on Freud: Zombie Psychoanalysis

Massimo Pigliucci (Figs in Winter) ran a two-part series on why psychoanalysis keeps surviving despite being empirically killed repeatedly. The core claim: Freudian theory has been falsified on its own terms — the empirical record consistently fails to support the theoretical framework — but it persists because it functions more like a religion or interpretive humanities tradition than a scientific theory, making falsification socially inoperative. Relevant if you're interested in philosophy of science and how bad ideas maintain institutional momentum.
