AI Hyperscalers Are Pricing Bitcoin Miners Off the Grid — Here's Why It's a Massive Win-Win

AI data centers are outbidding Bitcoin miners for grid power, forcing miners off primary grid connections. The counterintuitive argument here: this is actually a structural positive — it pushes miners toward stranded, curtailed, and off-grid energy sources, making the network more resilient and energy-diverse, while freeing premium grid capacity for AI buildout. Worth reading if you hold the view that Bitcoin's energy story is a liability — this reframes it as an adaptive advantage.

💡 Worth Reading

The Ancient Contrast Between Otium and Negotium

Massimo Pigliucci (Figs in Winter) digs into the Roman distinction between otium (leisure for reflection, reading, philosophy) and negotium (the busyness of public and commercial life). The Stoics and Epicureans debated how much of each life required — Seneca in particular argued you could pursue otium without retreating from the world entirely. Practically relevant if you're thinking about how to protect cognitive space in a high-output life.

Figs in Winter (Pigliucci)

How to Detach from Anxious Thoughts

Donald Robertson (the leading modern interpreter of Stoic cognitive therapy) opens a new miniseries on "The Stoic Mind." This first lesson covers the Stoic/CBT technique of cognitive distancing — recognizing that you are not your thoughts, and that automatic impressions don't require assent. The overlap between Epictetus's doctrine of the phantasia and modern defusion techniques in ACT is the substantive thread here.

Donald Robertson

Be Mindful Not to Injure Your Own Character — Epictetus Handbook #38

Robertson's commentary on Handbook #38, where Epictetus warns that spending too much time at entertainments (games, shows, social spectacle) gradually corrupts character — not through a single dramatic failure, but through slow erosion of your standards and attention. The mechanism is subtle and the application to modern doom-scrolling is obvious without Robertson having to say it.

Donald Robertson

What Sort of Maths Are LLMs Good At?

Written by Timothy Gowers, a Fields Medal–winning mathematician — so this is not a layperson's take. He probes where LLMs actually succeed and fail in mathematics: strong on pattern-matching and known result retrieval, weak on genuine novelty and multi-step deductive chains that require holding a proof structure in working memory. Useful calibration on the "AI solves math" narrative — the capability is real but specific.

The Quest for Caffeine You Can Have at Night

Scott Alexander digs into the science and practicality of caffeine analogs and metabolic variants that might give the alertness benefit without the sleep-disrupting half-life of standard caffeine. He surveys the actual compounds being explored, their mechanisms, and the evidence (or lack thereof). Worth reading if you've thought about adenosine, sleep pressure, or why your 2pm coffee still hits at midnight.
