Nick Norwitz

Norwitz covers a genuinely compelling new Nature study: "clonal hematopoiesis" — the accumulation of mutant, inflammatory immune cells in bone marrow as you age — is a major, underappreciated driver of atherosclerosis. The mechanism is striking: these mutant clones migrate from bone marrow into artery walls and fire up inflammation. A 90,000-person dataset showed more physical activity tracks with less clonal hematopoiesis, and mouse experiments showed exercise stopped clone expansion entirely for certain mutants while sleep disruption accelerated it. Crucially, these weren't redundant effects — exercise and sleep act through separate, complementary pathways. The human payoff: a randomized controlled trial (130 adults, published in Nature Metabolism) showed one year of exercise caused a 7% regression of arterial plaque on imaging, while the GLP-1 drug group showed no statistically significant benefit — leading the authors to conclude exercise is "more sensitive" to vascular benefit than the drug.

GTO Lab

Short but useful clip on limp-node strategy: the core point is that limping in position is only as exploitative as the opponent's response allows. If villain is folding too much to raises AND not putting enough money in when limping themselves, the correct adjustment is simply to raise more — get that extra big blind in pre-flop so you can bet larger on the flop and build the pot more reliably. The takeaway is a clean anchor: if you have a good hand and your opponent isn't putting money in, you should put it in yourself rather than trying to get clever with limps.

Peter Attia

Attia interviews headache specialist Brian Grosberg for a thorough orientation to the primary headache landscape. Key distinctions worth knowing: tension-type headache (bilateral, mild-moderate, no nausea, may have light OR sound but not both) is the most common but rarely brings people to a specialist; migraine requires ≥5 lifetime attacks lasting 4–72 hours, and up to 40% of migraineurs have bilateral (not unilateral) pain — most don't know this. A clinically important but underappreciated phenomenon: allodynia, present in ~65-70% of migraineurs, where normal stimuli (hair brushing, glasses on the nose) become uncomfortable during an attack. This matters because if patients develop allodynia and then use triptans, the window for treatment efficacy may have closed. Grosberg also notes that headache gets shockingly little curriculum time in medical school, which partly explains why it's undertreated.

Thomas DeLauer

DeLauer covers BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) as a muscle-to-pancreas signaling molecule — a mechanism most people haven't heard framed this way. The key finding: skeletal muscle cells produce and secrete BDNF when they contract, and pancreatic beta cells have dedicated BDNF receptors. When BDNF docks, it amplifies insulin secretion specifically when glucose is elevated — not at baseline, so it's not causing hypoglycemia. A 2020 Nature Communications study showed knocking out BDNF specifically in skeletal muscle caused significant glucose intolerance. A meta-analysis of 16 studies found circulating BDNF averages 1.12 ng/mL lower in people with insulin resistance. Practical levers: vigorous exercise requiring lactate (the "burn") spikes BDNF acutely; fasting elevated BDNF 47% in a 29-day Ramadan study even without exercise; food sources include fatty fish, dark berries, 85%+ dark chocolate, green tea (EGCG takes 2–3 weeks of consistency), and extra-virgin olive oil. DeLauer also covers the walking vs. running fat-burning question with actual mechanism detail — the cortisol spike, glucagon surge, and ghrelin rebound from running all push you toward post-exercise carb cravings and compensatory eating, while walking keeps all three hormones more stable.

🧬 Health & Science

Clonal Hematopoiesis: The Bone Marrow Driver of Heart Disease You Haven't Heard Of

Nick Norwitz's breakdown of a new Nature study introduces a mechanism that deserves wider attention: with age, certain stem cells in bone marrow acquire mutations that give them a survival edge, causing them to expand and crowd out normal cell lineages. These mutant clones are disproportionately inflammatory, and they migrate from bone marrow → blood → artery walls, where they fire up atherosclerosis. The study's key finding across 90,000+ people: more moderate-to-vigorous physical activity correlates with less clonal hematopoiesis, even after adjusting for BMI. In mouse experiments, 12 weeks of exercise halted expansion of certain mutant clones entirely; sleep disruption accelerated expansion and produced significantly more inflammatory mutants than sedentary controls. The mechanism is specific: exercise activates brainstem neurons that release hormones (including adrenaline) that bind immune cell receptors and suppress inflammatory signaling — a second-hit effect beyond just slowing clone production. The human RCT payoff: 7% plaque regression in one year from exercise alone, vs. no significant benefit in the GLP-1 drug group, published in Nature Metabolism 2024.

BDNF as a Muscle-to-Pancreas Insulin Signal

Thomas DeLauer covers a mechanism connecting muscle contraction to pancreatic function: skeletal muscle cells produce and secrete BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) during exercise, and pancreatic beta cells express dedicated TrkB receptors for it. A 2020 Nature Communications study showed BDNF amplifies insulin secretion specifically when blood glucose is elevated — not at baseline — meaning it's a glucose-sensitive signal, not a hypoglycemia risk. When researchers knocked out BDNF specifically in skeletal muscle in mice, the animals developed significant glucose intolerance. A 16-study meta-analysis found people with insulin resistance have circulating BDNF averaging 1.12 ng/mL lower than healthy controls. Practical implication: the BDNF pathway could be a key mechanism by which exercise improves insulin sensitivity beyond simple glucose uptake, and it's activated by lactate-generating vigorous activity (not gentle walking). Separately, a 29-day Ramadan fasting study showed BDNF rose 47% from fasting alone, suggesting fasting and vigorous exercise hit the pathway through complementary mechanisms.

Walking vs. Running: The Hormonal Case for Lower Intensity Fat Loss

Ekberg's analysis goes beyond "fat burning zone" percentages to explain why walking often outperforms running for fat loss in practice via three hormonal mechanisms: (1) Cortisol spikes sharply with running, upregulating carbohydrate use and increasing post-exercise cravings; walking keeps cortisol near baseline. (2) Glucagon surges 4–6x higher during running vs. walking, depleting glycogen and creating a strong "refuel" signal post-workout. (3) Ghrelin (hunger hormone) rebounds hard after running suppression but stays stable during and after walking. The practical consequence: runners often compensate calorically in ways walkers don't. An important nuance: these ratios shift dramatically on a low-carb or keto-adapted diet — fat oxidation can extend to 80–90% of max intensity in adapted individuals, compared to ~50% at the "fat max" for carb-adapted people.

Peter Attia on Migraine: Clinical Distinctions Worth Knowing

From the Grosberg interview: the diagnostic criteria for migraine require ≥5 attacks lasting 4–72 hours plus two of four features (unilateral pain, throbbing, moderate-to-severe intensity, aggravated by activity) AND either nausea/vomiting or photo+phonophobia. Up to 40% of migraineurs have bilateral pain — widely misunderstood as ruling out migraine. Allodynia (uncomfortable sensation to normally neutral stimuli like hairbrushing or glasses) is present in ~65–70% of migraineurs and is clinically important: once allodynia sets in during an attack, triptans lose efficacy, making early intervention critical. Medication overuse headache is a secondary cause that can masquerade as worsening primary headache — caffeine withdrawal is another secondary cause, neither requiring imaging to diagnose.
