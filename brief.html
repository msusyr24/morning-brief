GTO Wizard

A deep and genuinely instructive PLO preflop breakdown. The core insight: PLO is soft because hand evaluation is hard — there are 270,000 starting hand combos vs. 1,300 in Hold'em, so even modest study creates large edges. The video introduces the concept of "harmony" (how well your four cards work together across multiple nut pathways) and demonstrates counterintuitive results: JT9s single-suited outperforms AK72 double-suited as an EP open because AK72 blocks the flush that pays you off, while the 7 and 2 rarely contribute to made straights. On suitedness, the "crappy" double-suited type — your two highest cards sharing one suit, two lowest sharing another — is worth roughly half the EV of the good types because you can only make low flushes and block your own payoffs. Connectivity analysis shows that middling, gapped cards (J975) actually make more straights than a top-of-deck run (AKQJ) because extremity cards have fewer wrap combinations.

GTO Lab

Nick Petrangelo, Daniel Dvoress, and Axel Hallay debut a mystery bounty library and make a strong case that almost no one has a real strategy for these tournaments. The key framework introduced is the "KO stack ratio" — bounty value divided by villain's stack — as a shortcut for adjusting call-off ranges in real time. At a KO:stack ratio above 2.0, you call any two cards regardless of ICM. Below that, the library shows how to separate hands into pure-yellow (KO EV only), pure-green (prize pool EV only), and blended EV — and the blended tab is the one that matters. The sims reveal that pushing ranges in mystery bounties are strange (sometimes tighter, sometimes high-card heavy) compared to vanilla MTTs, and that the same stack distribution at different bounty sizes produces dramatically different correct strategies — something impossible to derive intuitively.

Jared Alderman

Jared makes a nuanced argument that aggression is cognitively overvalued in poker because players conflate "agency" with "betting." The real point: checking is not passive, it's a strategic choice — and versus recreational players who have a structured (if exploitable) game, the correct counter is often to keep pots small rather than bet and win small amounts immediately. The mistake is paying a "premium" (losing extra EV in the pot) as insurance to take down a pot now, rather than trusting your ability to navigate streets. He also cautions against two-dimensional player modeling — labeling someone "aggro" or "station" misses the contours of their strategy, e.g., a player who check-raises top pair always but never bluffs is aggressive in one dimension and passive in another, and those two dimensions require different exploits.

🧬 Health & Science

The Fructose Mechanism Update — What Lustig's Famous Lecture Got Wrong

A 2018 study (confirmed in follow-up research) found that the small intestine — not the liver — is the primary processor of fructose, clearing up to 90% of low doses. The liver is a backup overflow system. This matters for dose-dependent decisions: liquid fructose on an empty stomach likely saturates the gut at ~10g per sitting, while fructose consumed slowly with fiber may allow up to ~25g before overflow reaches the liver. Practical upshot: a half-can of soda likely already overloads the system; whole fruit with fiber at moderate portions does not. The Lustig "alcohol without the buzz" comparison is mechanistically wrong even if the clinical warning still stands. Separately, fructose suppresses ghrelin (satiety hormone) less than glucose does, and unlike glucose produces almost no insulin response — meaning it provides no satiety signal while still delivering calories and driving cravings, a double problem independent of the liver question.

Cortisol, Chronic Undereating, and the Sodium-Potassium Pump

Thomas DeLauer's most substantive claim: chronically restricting calories depletes the minerals needed to run the sodium-potassium pump, which neurons rely on for stable electrical firing. When this pump is impaired, the brain stays hyperexcitable, cortisol rises to compensate, and early-morning waking (2–3am) follows. This creates a loop where the person thinks they need better sleep hygiene, when the root cause is metabolic stress from under-fueling. His recommended fix: target rough caloric maintenance over a 7-day rolling window rather than daily restriction, fasting 2–3 days/week but eating adequately on other days. The fermented-food-GABA mechanism he cites is from a Molecular Psychiatry study showing a 32% drop in perceived stress in a psychobiotic group vs. 17% control, with measurable shifts in tryptophan metabolism away from the neurotoxic kynurenine pathway toward serotonin.

Thomas DeLauer — 1/2 Cup of This Drops Cortisol and Shrinks Visceral Fat

Cancer Prevention: Polygenic Risk, Inflammatory States, and the "Bad Luck" Revision

Oncologist Siddhartha Mukherjee on Peter Attia's podcast updates several priors. On prevention: we lack a "high cholesterol equivalent" biomarker for cancer risk from inflammatory states, but we now know inflammatory states are pro-carcinogenic — the biomarker will come. On hereditary risk: Vogelstein's original 5% hereditary figure is outdated; we've since found that much of "unexplained" hereditary cancer is driven by hundreds of polygenic "nudge" mutations (each adding a small percentage of risk) rather than single "shove" mutations like BRCA1. On treatment: immunotherapy now meaningfully extends survival in smokers with lung cancer (something Mukherjee considered impossible 15 years ago), and CAR-T produces genuine long-term remission — not 5-year survival but 15-20 year survival — in responsive leukemias and lymphomas. The colorectal cancer rise in young people remains a genuine mystery with no established cause.

Astral Codex Ten

Will bystander economies be able to finance their governments?

Economist Jesús Fernández-Villaverde's paper "Saving for the Machines" poses a pointed macro question: AI investment requires enormous capital formation, which crowds out consumption and compresses the tax base. Countries that are not at the AI frontier — "bystander economies" — may face a fiscal crisis as their labor income falls and capital flows toward the handful of countries building AI infrastructure. This is a concrete, structural argument rather than vague AI-disruption hand-waving.

Anthropic research finds that LLMs can now assist with formal verification — mathematically proving that code behaves correctly — at a scale previously impractical. Formal verification was historically too labor-intensive for most real software; if LLMs can automate the proof-writing step, this changes the security and reliability calculus for critical systems.

Gaius Blossius, the Revolutionary Stoic
