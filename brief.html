Mental Model · Math/Statistics

Law of Large Numbers

Over many trials, averages converge to expected values — but in the short run, anything can happen. Casinos know this; gamblers forget it. Applied to life: don't judge strategies by single outcomes. A good decision can have a bad result, and vice versa.

🧬 Health & Science

Day-Three Fasting Threshold — Protein Mechanisms in Prolonged Fasting

A study tracking 3,000 proteins across a 7-day water-only fast in 12 healthy subjects found that the largest cluster of changes — involving longevity, cardiovascular, and brain health pathways — didn't emerge until day three. The implication is that most intermittent fasting protocols (16:8, 24-hour fasts) may primarily deliver calorie restriction effects rather than the distinct molecular benefits of extended fasting. This challenges the common assumption that any fast length produces the same qualitative biological effects at lower magnitude. Nick Norwitz covered this and noted that some targeted lifestyle strategies (and specific molecules) may be able to accelerate similar pathways without full multi-day fasting.

Nick Norwitz — Most People End Their Fast Too Soon

Sleep Deprivation and Visceral Fat: Mayo Clinic RCT

A randomized controlled trial published in JACC compared healthy young adults sleeping 4 hours vs. 9 hours in a controlled lab setting over 2 weeks. The 4-hour group accumulated 11% more visceral fat (measured by CT scan) despite gaining almost no scale weight. This is a mechanistically important finding: visceral fat accumulation — the metabolically dangerous depot linked to cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and inflammation — can increase substantially without any signal in body weight, making it invisible without imaging. The gene expression data from a separate chronobiology study showed that late eating (same food, same calories, just timed 4 hours later) shifted fat tissue gene expression toward storage over release.

Thomas DeLauer — Every Night You Do This, Belly Fat TRIPLES Around Your Organs

Protein Leverage Hypothesis: Appetite Targets Protein, Not Calories

Research across multiple species (including humans) consistently shows that animals regulate protein intake more tightly than carbohydrate or fat intake — when dietary protein is diluted, total caloric consumption increases until the protein target is met. A human study in AJCN found that simply doubling protein from 15% to 30% of calories caused participants to spontaneously reduce intake by 440 calories/day without any calorie restriction instruction. The practical implication: front-loading protein early in the day (hitting 30–50% of daily target at the first meal) creates a stronger GLP-1/PYY satiety response and reduces total-day food noise more effectively than distributing the same protein across meals.

Thomas DeLauer — I Changed HOW I Eat Protein

Lithium and Alzheimer's — LATTICE Trial

Peter Attia covers a negative clinical trial (LATTICE) examining lithium as an Alzheimer's intervention. The trial failed to show benefit, though Attia notes the result leaves open mechanistic questions rather than closing the door — prior observational and small RCT data had suggested low-dose lithium might have neuroprotective effects through tau phosphorylation and BDNF pathways. The failure of translation from mechanism to clinical outcome in dementia trials is a recurring pattern worth tracking for anyone following longevity research.

Peter Attia — Lithium and Alzheimer's disease: what to weave of this LATTICE?

Hypertrophy-Strength Correlation: Stronger by Science Reanalysis

A new analysis from Stronger by Science argues that prior studies underestimated how much muscle growth contributes to strength gains in novice lifters — but adds an important methodological caveat: the relationship appears much stronger than it actually is due to statistical artifacts (shared variance, measurement issues). The real-world takeaway is nuanced: hypertrophy matters more for novice strength gains than previously measured, but it's still not the dominant driver, and the correlation is inflated by how both variables were assessed.
