Renaissance Periodization

Mike Israetel did a full price-per-gram-of-protein breakdown shopping at Aldi and Costco. The winner: whole eggs at ~2 cents/gram of protein, narrowly beating fat-free milk (~2.3¢) and chunk light tuna (~2.9¢). Whey protein powder tied with cottage cheese and chicken thighs at ~3.9¢ and is genuinely competitive with whole foods — not the luxury item people assume. Canned chicken, despite the "prepper food" reputation, is actually the most expensive on the list (~5.7¢) because you're paying for the prep labor. He also makes the useful distinction between "cheapest per gram of protein" and "cheapest per gram of protein per 100 calories" — which matters depending on whether you're in a caloric surplus or deficit.

Thomas DeLauer

Two substantive mechanistic videos. On sleep: a study of 3,628 adults measuring actual serum antioxidant levels (not food recall) found 10/12 antioxidants significantly lower in people with sleep disorders; after full statistical adjustment, lutein/zeaxanthin showed the strongest association (28% lower sleep disorder risk when sufficient) and vitamin C came in at 17%. A Mendelian randomization analysis using genetic data — which controls for reverse causation — found causal links between vitamin C and nighttime waking, and selenium and sleep apnea. The mechanism: oxidative stress accumulates while awake, and the glymphatic system clears it during deep sleep; if sleep is already impaired, the clearance fails, creating a self-reinforcing loop that antioxidants can interrupt. The practical dose sweet spot for vitamin C is 125–200mg/day (a single bell pepper or two kiwis); for lutein/zeaxanthin, an RCT showed 20mg/4mg respectively for 6 months improved sleep quality in high-screen-exposure adults. On thyroid: T4-to-T3 conversion requires selenium (the deiodinase enzymes are literally selenoproteins and cannot be built without it), iodine for raw hormone synthesis, and iron for the thyroperoxidase enzyme — and these three interact mechanistically, not additively. Cortisol suppresses the same 5'-deiodinase conversion pathway through a completely separate route, and excess reverse T3 then blocks thyroid receptors even if T3 is present. L-theanine at 200mg was cited as producing a ~40% cortisol reduction within an hour in one study.

ModernWisdom (David Sinclair episode)

David Sinclair gives the most current public update on his epigenetic reprogramming work. The core claim: aging is caused by corruption of the epigenome (the pattern of DNA bundling/unbundling that tells cells what type of cell to be) — not DNA damage itself. His lab published in 2023 that they could artificially corrupt this epigenetic software to rapidly age a mouse, and then reverse it. The gene therapy (OSK — three Yamanaka factors delivered by viral vector) has now moved into humans in the eye, targeting glaucoma and macular degeneration; he claims it works on "pretty much any disease or injury" tested in the eye in animals. More newsworthy: his lab has spent five years developing an oral version — a pill given 3 days/week for a month in old mice that produces measurable rejuvenation including memory and skin quality. The mechanism appears to run through TET enzymes that strip chemical marks off DNA (the same reset pathway used by embryos); blocking TET enzymes eliminates the pill's effect, confirming it's genuinely epigenetic, not just a hormesis response. He states the first person to live to 150 is already alive, and believes there is no theoretical upper limit to human lifespan in biology.

Videos: "Age Reversal Is Coming." Everything You Need To Know - Dr David Sinclair

🧬 Health & Science

FDA Authorizes First Wearable That Continuously Monitors Both Ketone and Blood Sugar Levels

This is a genuine first: no wearable has previously been authorized to track both glucose and ketones simultaneously in real time. For anyone doing low-carb, carnivore, or metabolic health monitoring, this closes a significant gap — you previously needed separate CGM and ketone finger-prick tests to understand whether you were actually in ketosis and how your glucose was responding. The combination gives a direct window into metabolic state that has been unavailable outside a lab.

Peter Attia AMA #88: Metabolic Liver Health

Attia's key claim: metabolic liver disease (steatosis and early fibrosis) is reversible if caught early — address the metabolic drivers and the liver can actually regress. The implication is that aggressive early screening matters more than most people realize, and that standard liver panels often miss early dysfunction. Worth listening to if you're tracking metabolic health markers or have any concern about visceral fat accumulation.

💡 Worth Reading

Black Hole Singularity Is a Surface, Not a Point

New preprint argues the singularity at the center of a black hole — long assumed to be a zero-dimensional point of infinite density — is actually a two-dimensional surface. If correct, this resolves certain mathematical infinities in general relativity and has implications for how information is destroyed (or isn't) inside black holes. Early days, but it's the kind of foundational physics rethink worth tracking.

HN Discussion

What Philosophy Promises — Figs in Winter (Pigliucci on Epictetus 1.15)

Massimo Pigliucci unpacks Discourses 1.15, where Epictetus addresses what philosophy actually delivers versus what people show up hoping to get. The Stoic answer is uncomfortable: philosophy doesn't give you control over outcomes, other people, or circumstances — it gives you a method for working on yourself. Worth sitting with if you're in the middle of something hard and tempted to use philosophy as a comfort rather than a discipline.

Figs in Winter

How to Mentally Rehearse Coping — Donald Robertson

Robertson's second lesson in his Stoic Mind miniseries, on premeditatio malorum as a practical skill rather than a philosophical posture. The specific technique: mentally rehearsing not just that bad things might happen, but how you would actually respond — what you'd say to yourself, what you'd do, what Stoic principle you'd apply. This is closer to cognitive behavioral therapy's "behavioral rehearsal" than to generic "expect the worst" advice.
