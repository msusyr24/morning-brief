GTO Lab

Nick Petrangelo, Daniel Dvoress, and Axel Hallay walk through GTO Lab's new mystery bounty library — 240+ sims and counting — built specifically for a format they argue almost no one plays correctly. The core insight: mystery bounties aren't "regular tournaments where you adjust a little." They're a fundamentally different game, and the most common mistake is being too tight when bounties first kick in, since ICM is minimal and bounties are maximally valuable at that stage. The key analytical tool they built is the KO-to-stack ratio: bounty value divided by current stack, which drives the entire pre- and post-flop strategic adjustment. They also show a combined EV view that breaks out prize-pool EV vs. KO EV per hand — revealing that some hands are played purely for bounty value, with zero prize-pool EV, which is nearly impossible to intuit without sims.

Videos: Mystery Bounty Is a Completely Different Game | Nick Petrangelo, Daniel Dvoress & Axel Hallay

Jared Alderman

Jared and Uri Peleg dig into the hidden information layer of poker that gets underappreciated: your opponent cannot know your game tree. Uri's key claim is that bluffing balance only matters insofar as your opponent can detect it — meaning you can over-bluff ace-high boards and under-bluff king-high boards and be globally fine, because there's no practical way for anyone to discover this unless you tell them. The chess analogy Uri uses is sharp: GTO poker is setting up your "invisible chessboard" optimally given your opponent doesn't know your board. Exploitative poker is making an informed guess about how they've set up theirs. Uri also pushes back on RNG-dependency: he stopped RNGing and instead asked "how can my opponent even know if I'm balanced?" — and found that as long as frequencies aren't face-up, the edge from perfect randomization is minimal compared to other exploits available.

Videos: "Poker Doesn't Work Without Exploits" - Uri Peleg

Nick Norwitz

Norwitz covers the fasting mimicking diet (FMD) with unusual depth, anchoring on a 2024 Nature Communications RCT: three 5-day FMD cycles (700–1,100 kcal/day, fat-dominant, protein-restricted) lowered biological age by a median of 2.5 years on a validated blood-based aging score, with a second independent trial replicating the finding. Crucially, the reduction in biological age was not correlated with weight loss — implying this is genuine metabolic reprogramming rather than a caloric restriction artifact. Mechanistically, the two levers are ketosis (from caloric + fat emphasis) and mTOR/IGF-1 suppression via protein restriction. Additional findings: liver fat dropped from 14% to 8% in participants with fatty liver, immune aging markers improved, and a monkey study showed lower bodyweight set points persisting 1,000+ days after the intervention ended. His modeled estimate: three FMD cycles per year could reduce 20-year cardiovascular mortality by 17% and cancer mortality by 6%.

🧬 Health & Science

Fasting Mimicking Diet: 2.5 Years of Biological Age Reduction in 15 Days (Nick Norwitz deep dive)

The key RCT (Nature Communications, 2024): three 5-day cycles of ~700–1,100 kcal/day, fat-dominant, protein-restricted diet reduced biological age by a median 2.5 years on a validated mortality-predictive blood biomarker score (albumin, creatinine, CRP, HbA1c, blood pressure). A second independent trial replicated the finding. The age reduction was statistically independent of weight loss — suggesting metabolic reprogramming rather than caloric restriction is the mechanism. The two mechanistic levers: (1) ketosis forced by low-calorie, fat-dominant intake, mimicking fasting metabolic state; (2) protein restriction suppressing IGF-1 and mTOR (the cell's growth switch), triggering autophagy and cellular renewal pathways. A 2023 mouse study showed FMD cycles protected cardiac tissue from immunotherapy-induced fibrosis while making cancer cells more vulnerable — consistent with "differential stress resistance" where healthy cells hunker down while cancer cells get caught out. The monkey data is the most surprising: post-intervention weight setpoints remained lower 1,000+ days after animals resumed ad libitum eating, suggesting durable metabolic reprogramming rather than temporary caloric deficit. Practical protocol: one 5-day cycle per month for three months = 15 total days per year. Fat-dominant, low protein, ~700–1,100 kcal. This is not indefinite caloric restriction — the benefit comes from periodicity and the signaling environment change, not sustained deprivation.

Nick Norwitz

Blood Work as a Health Proxy: Peter Attia's 25–30% Rule

Attia's core framework for interpreting labs: even the most comprehensive blood panels give you roughly 25–30% of the information needed for a complete health risk picture. The "check engine light" mental model — if nothing's flagged, all is fine — is dangerously wrong; most of the actionable risk information lives outside reference ranges and outside blood markers entirely. Longitudinal data matters more than snapshots: a CRP of 3 means something completely different if it's a chronic baseline versus a post-vaccination spike. He also notes that LP(a) and ApoE only need to be tested once (genetic, doesn't change), while markers like LDL and CRP require trend context to interpret meaningfully.

Insulin Resistance as the Root of Five "Separate" Problems (Thomas DeLauer)

DeLauer argues that morning brain fog, need-to-eat-breakfast, afternoon energy crashes, irritability between meals, and elevated fasting glucose (90+ mg/dL) are five presentations of the same root cause: chronically elevated insulin suppressing lipolysis, which prevents the brain from accessing ketones as backup fuel in the fasted state. The key mechanism: insulin blocks the lipolysis pathway even at sub-diabetic levels, leaving the brain simultaneously starved of glucose (last meal metabolized) and ketones (pathway blocked). He distinguishes this from low-carb-induced "peripheral insulin resistance," which is a protective adaptation. His lowest-friction intervention: shift dinner 2 hours earlier, extending the overnight fast without any other dietary change — estimated 15–20% improvement in insulin sensitivity from that alone.

Are RCTs Underpowered? — Marginal Revolution

A study of 745 principal-hypothesis tests from 190 articles in leading economics journals finds that actual t-statistics peak well below what you'd expect from adequately powered studies — suggesting systematic underpowering, which produces inflated false-positive rates even in published, peer-reviewed RCTs. Directly relevant to evaluating health research claims, including some discussed in this brief.
