Terence Tao: AI is moving mathematics from proof scarcity to proof abundance

In a long conversation with Tyler Cowen, Tao says AI has pushed mathematics into an era of 'proof abundance'. The episode summary says this raises questions about the future of the research paper and suggests video talks may become more valuable than papers. Tao also describes recasting epsilon-delta analysis as a game against an opponent, which makes strategy easier to think about. He says he uses Ricardo's comparative advantage to decide how to spend his time. On Black-Scholes, he calls it an 'algebraic miracle' that works under Gaussian fluctuations and becomes dangerous when they don't hold, especially once it is compounded into derivatives.

  • Framing a hard analysis statement as a game gives you strategies and a sense of who is ahead.
  • Comparative advantage is a practical rule for choosing what to work on.
  • Know when a model breaks down: Black-Scholes is risky without Gaussian fluctuations.
