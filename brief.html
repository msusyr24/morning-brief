🧬 Health & Science

Blue Zones Debunked — The Data Problem in Longevity Research

Dr. Saul Newman (Oxford/UCL) presents a damning statistical case: Japan's Ministry of Justice audit found 82% of verified 110+ year-olds were already dead — 236,000 people still on paper, pensions being collected by families for decades. The mechanism is simple: these individuals were born before reliable birth records, so age is unverifiable. Newman extends this to the two leading Nature and Science papers on lifespan limits and longevity plateaus — both contained elementary errors (wrong spreadsheet column, data rounded to zero at extreme ages) that, when corrected, eliminated the findings entirely. His conclusion: all longevity data beyond roughly age 100 is unreliable, which also invalidates pension and life insurance actuarial assumptions built on it. The Blue Zones are a casualty of this broader data failure, not a deliberate fraud — they simply took unverifiable records at face value and built a $700M+ wellness industry on them.

Thyroid Supplement Fraud: Real Hormones in Unregulated Pills

Peter Attia cites chromatography analysis of 10 commercial "thyroid support" supplements: 9 of 10 contained actual T3, 5 contained T4, and several at doses exceeding standard prescription starting doses — none disclosed on the label. Separate reports link adulterated weight-loss supplements (also containing thyroid hormones) to at least one fatality. Iodine-rich kelp in many products independently triggers hyperthyroidism in people with undiagnosed autonomous thyroid nodules. The practical implication: "thyroid support" is one of the highest-risk supplement categories, not a benign wellness product.
