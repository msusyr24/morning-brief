Mental Model · Economics

Supply and Demand

Prices are signals, not values. When something is scarce relative to demand, its price rises until demand drops or supply increases. Most people ignore this when buying bubbles (tulips, crypto) or selling panics (2008 real estate). Munger's defense: always ask, 'relative to what supply, and what demand level?'

Nick Norwitz

Norwitz makes the case that simultaneous fat loss and muscle gain is biologically real and becoming more accessible through emerging tools. His "natty" baseline: sleep (growth hormone surges in deep sleep stages 3–4 and drives visceral fat loss), 2–3 resistance sessions/week with progressive overload, and 1.6–2.2g protein/kg bodyweight — above which returns diminish. His case study: a hypogonadal patient on TRT (to restore normal levels only) + tirzepatide + low-carb diet + resistance training lost ~50 lbs of pure fat while gaining 26 lbs of muscle over ~18 months. He then covers 5-amino-1MQ (an NNMT inhibitor that preserves NAD+ by plugging a "metabolic leak"), which in mice improved grip strength 40%, exercise capacity 20%, and stacked additively with training for 60% total strength improvement, plus reduced fat infiltration into muscle — though no human RCT data yet. ATX-304, an AMPK activator in Phase 2 trials, caused 100% of weight loss from fat mass with no lean mass loss in animals, even with increased calorie intake.

🧬 Health & Science

Simultaneous Fat Loss + Muscle Gain: What the Evidence Actually Says (Nick Norwitz)

The mechanistic case for body recomposition rests on compartmentalization: the body can simultaneously direct anabolic signals to muscle while mobilizing fatty acids for fuel, even within short timeframes. The practical hierarchy of evidence-based natty tools is sleep (GH surges in stages 3–4, promotes visceral fat loss), progressive resistance training, and 1.6–2.2g/kg protein. The Nicholas N. case study — hypogonadism corrected to normal T levels + tirzepatide + resistance training — produced 50 lbs of pure fat loss and 26 lbs of muscle gain over ~18 months with no illicit compounds. The novel compound 5-amino-1MQ (NNMT inhibitor, preserves NAD+) showed additive effects with exercise in animal models (60% combined strength improvement) and reduced intramuscular fat infiltration — worth tracking as human data develops. ATX-304 (AMPK activator, Phase 2 complete) caused zero lean mass loss with all weight loss from fat in animal models despite no caloric restriction.

Mitochondrial Threats Flying Under the Radar (Thomas DeLauer ft. Dr. Perlmutter)

Three specific, underappreciated mitochondrial threats with mechanistic grounding: (1) Single nights of poor sleep impair glucose uptake and mitochondrial function measurably the next day — not just cumulatively over time; chronically, this creates an obesity-sleep apnea feedback loop. (2) PM2.5 airborne particles (from highways, wildfire smoke, incense burning) drive Alzheimer's risk via neuroinflammation; Toronto research shows Alzheimer's risk scales directly with proximity to major highways. (3) Paraquat herbicide — now displacing glyphosate in the US as weeds develop resistance — is a documented mitochondrial toxin used to experimentally induce Parkinson's disease in primates, banned or severely restricted in 70+ countries.

Fat Loss Cardio and Muscle Retention: The 130 BPM Threshold (Renaissance Periodization)

When muscle retention is the primary constraint during a cut, cardio modality matters: elliptical and incline walking are preferred because they distribute effort across many muscle groups, protect joints, and keep heart rate under ~130 bpm. Above that threshold, fatigue accumulation begins to compete with the recovery capacity needed for hypertrophy stimulus, increasing catabolism risk. This is a simple, actionable heuristic for programming cardio during a fat loss phase.

Why Most Stereotypes Are Negative

A cognitive-ecological theory arguing that negative stereotypes dominate not due to bias or malice but because negative traits are informationally more useful for survival and social navigation — they signal threats that require action, while positive traits are less behaviorally urgent. This is a substantive empirical claim about the structure of social perception worth sitting with, especially against the common assumption that stereotype negativity is primarily a product of prejudice.

On the difference between knowing a Stoic text philologically and understanding it as a living philosophy — using Mary Beard and Emily Wilson as case studies in how classical scholars can translate with precision and miss the practical point entirely. The implicit argument: philosophy as a way of life requires practitioners, not just commentators. Relevant for anyone using Stoic texts as operational tools rather than cultural objects.

Stoic Cost-Benefit Analysis — Donald Robertson

Robertson reconstructs the Stoic practice of weighing actions against their cost to character — distinct from consequentialist calculation because the primary currency isn't outcomes but who you become. The Stoics asked not just "what will this produce?" but "what kind of person does this action make me?" A useful framework for decisions where the right choice is obvious in outcome terms but harder to commit to under pressure.
