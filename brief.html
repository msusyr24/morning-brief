Headache specialist Brian Grosberg walks through the full taxonomy: tension headaches (both-sided, mild-moderate, no nausea, never both photo- and phonophobia) vs. migraines (unilateral in 60%, pulsating, moderate-severe, 4–72 hours, requires at least 2 of 4 features plus photo/phonophobia or nausea). Microglia, Metabolism, and the Feed-Forward Brain Inflammation Cycle (DeLauer / Perlmutter)

Perlmutter's mechanistic argument is worth extracting: microglia (brain immune cells) exist in M2 (supportive, anti-inflammatory) or M1 (destructive, pro-inflammatory) states, and their metabolism directly mirrors systemic metabolism. High fasting insulin and blood sugar lock them into M1, which then degrades the blood-brain barrier, lets in more inflammatory signals, and shifts more microglia to M1 — a feed-forward cycle. Critically, this also weakens the anterior cingulate cortex's connection to the prefrontal cortex, impairing the "adult in the room" decision-making and strengthening amygdala-driven impulse choices — making bad metabolic choices more likely the worse your metabolism already is. The cycle is self-reinforcing in both directions. The practical upstream levers are fasting insulin, fasting glucose (he recommends periodic CGM use), and gut integrity (leaky gut → systemic inflammation → leaky brain).

Ketogenic Diet for Treatment-Resistant Psychiatric Illness (Palmer / Huberman)

Palmer's key clinical data points: in his practice, patients with treatment-resistant depression, bipolar disorder, and schizoaffective disorder who achieve blood ketones >0.8–1.5 mmol show benefits that didn't emerge from medications alone. The mechanism he points to is mitochondrial — ketones provide a more efficient alternative fuel for neurons under metabolic stress, and the same pathway explains the 100-year track record of ketogenic diet for epilepsy. His benchmark patient (17 failed medications, daily hallucinations, now in sustained remission) is an extreme case, but he reports the pattern across multiple patients. He's careful to note this is adjunctive, not a replacement for supervised psychiatric care, and that coming off psychiatric meds is dangerous without supervision.
