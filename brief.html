US set to announce new tariffs on 16 trading partners

The BBC understands the US will announce new tariffs on 16 jurisdictions, including China, the EU, Mexico and Japan. Solver says Kenney's 178% pot river overbet with jacks and Eibinger's ace-three call were both right

At Triton North Cyprus, with about 150+ big blinds, Kenney called preflop with JJ in the big blind. GTO Wizard prefers calling to three-betting by a slim margin (4.43 vs 4.41 EV). Eibinger's check-back with A3s on a 10-5-3 flop was approved but cost EV, since the solver continuation-bets 95.5% of the lojack range, mostly at 39% pot. Checking back left his river range capped, so the solver overbets JJ at 180% pot (EV 17.42 vs 17.30 for pot and 17.01 for half-pot). Facing that bet the solver folds 64.4% of the range, but A3s is among the hands that call. It blocks nothing critical, and Kenney's polarized range includes bluffs like KJ, QJ and missed draws.

Two new statin trials: plaque shrinks, and heart attacks fall in over-70s

A Danish trial of people averaging 64 with at least 50% coronary blockage compared atorvastatin 40 mg with rosuvastatin 40 mg plus ezetimibe. LDL fell from about 120 to about 70 (-38%) versus about 40 (-65%). Total plaque volume shrank 13.6% versus 21% over 18 months, and soft plaque shrank 20% versus 28%. Calcified plaque grew by 42% and 62%, which would be expected to stabilize plaque. A separate independent trial of about 10,000 healthy people averaging 75 compared atorvastatin 40 mg with placebo over almost 6 years. Major events fell from 8.3% to 6%, and heart attacks from 2.75% to 1.6%. Strokes and deaths did not change significantly, and dementia showed no difference.

  • Side effects on statin vs placebo: muscle/joint complaints 32% vs 29%, liver issues 3.3% vs 0.9%, diabetes-related issues 4% vs 2.7%; serious cases were rare.
  • Benefit was significant only in men, and only with systolic BP over 128 or BMI over 25. The authors did not run the statistical tests needed to confirm these differences, and a 26-trial meta-analysis found benefit in both sexes.
  • If a statin causes problems, options include a half dose, every-other-day dosing, switching to rosuvastatin, or ezetimibe.

◎Mind & Philosophy

Donald Robertson

Donald Robertson: don't "awfulize" even when a situation really is bad

Robertson, drawing on Albert Ellis, defines awfulizing as judging something 101% bad, which makes it unbearable and something that must not happen. He links this to the Stoic judgment that something is kakon, meaning it ruins your whole ability to live well. Reframing from "awful" to "highly unfortunate but not a total disaster" swaps neurotic unhappiness for ordinary unhappiness, in Freud's phrase. This helps because panic degrades problem-solving, and the more serious the problem, the more you need reason rather than fear.

  • Watch for globalizing language like "total disaster" and swap in "bad but not awful."
  • Ask whether triggering the fear response helps solve the problem.

Daily Stoic reading · Desire and aversion within control

Remember that desire demands the attainment of that of which you are desirous; and aversion demands the avoidance of that to which you are averse; that he who fails of the object of his desires is disappointed; and he who incurs the object of his aversion is wretched. If, then, you shun only those undesirable things which you can control, you will never incur anything which you shun; but if you shun sickness, or death, or poverty, you will run the risk of wretchedness. Remove [the habit of] aversion, then, from all things that are not within our power, and apply it to things undesirable which are within our power. But for the present, altogether restrain desire; for if you desire any of the things not within our own power, you must necessarily be disappointed; and you are not yet secure of those which are within our power, and so are legitimate objects of desire. Where it is practically necessary for you to pursue or avoid anything, do even this with discretion and gentleness and moderation.

— Epictetus, Enchiridion

In plain terms: wanting an outcome you do not control guarantees disappointment, and dreading one guarantees misery when it arrives, so aim your wanting and avoiding at your own choices and effort rather than at results like sickness, death, or money. For poker, this means you can fully control your decisions, bet sizing, and table discipline, while card distribution and opponents' reactions stay outside your power; you still act with care and moderation when a choice is practically required. Under variance, judge each session by whether you played the hands well, not by the short-run outcome, and drop the craving for a specific result after a bad beat or a hot streak.

Mental model · Psychology

Lollapalooza Effect

Munger's own coinage for when multiple biases converge on the same outcome. Example: auction fever combines commitment bias, social proof, contrast bias, and deprival super-reaction — all pushing the bidder the same way. When you're making a decision and several biases happen to agree, be most suspicious, not least.
