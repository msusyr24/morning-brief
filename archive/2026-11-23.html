BBC World

COP31 ends without a major climate deal after fossil fuels row

Talks in Antalya ran two days over, becoming the longest UN climate conference ever. The final draft never mentioned fossil fuels directly, and the EU, Pacific island nations and others rejected it. Poorer countries objected that it had no concrete adaptation finance figures. It is the first collapse of a COP's main political agreement since Copenhagen in 2009. Some smaller agreements survived: progress on a 'just transition mechanism', and backing from over 80 countries for a target of 35% electricity share of global energy use by 2035. The UN says current policies point to about 2.8C of warming by 2100. The next COP is in November 2027 in Ethiopia, and the UN climate chief will seek advice on reforming the consensus process.

GTO Wizard: simplify solver strategies with node locking and lose almost no EV

A GTO Wizard stream shows how to node-lock simplified strategies and measure the EV cost against the best response. In heads-up 100bb, replacing the small blind's mix of raise, call and limp with a pure 2.5x open or fold left EV unchanged at 0.55 bb/hand. The big blind's defense is harder: a rough hand-built strategy with a 10x 3-bet lost about 3bb/100. Compare-EV on the hands that are unhappy and fewer bluffs (over-bluffing was the problem) brought the loss down to about 2bb/100. Removing locks on some hands lets the solver choose how to distribute the actions among them.

  • Always measure EV at the root node. Measuring deeper makes a simplification look like it cost EV when it didn't.
  • Match the original action frequencies, here raise 26%, call 37% and fold 36%, then iterate with Compare EV.
  • Heads-up rake at NL500 turns the small blind's 5bb/100 and the big blind's -16bb/100 into an 11bb/100 gap that goes to the house.

Thomas DeLauer's 14-day high-carb 'anabolic burst cycling' self-experiment: gained 15 lb

After 7 days at about 1,200 kcal of very low-carb, low-fat food, he ate 500 to 700g of carbs and about 5,000 kcal a day for two weeks. He gained about 15 lb. The theory, from the 1990s and backed here by Joel Green, is that after depletion there is a 10 to 14 day window when surplus calories go to muscle via IGF-1 and mTOR rather than to fat. He used heavy compound lifts and creatine at 10 to 15g. The evidence is anecdotal: no measured body composition was given, and the video is sponsored by a honey brand.

◆AI Capability

OpenAI Blog

OpenAI's chief scientist says falling perplexity predicts new AI capabilities

Jakub Pachocki argues that perplexity, how well a model predicts the next token, is the best measure of progress. He reports log-perplexity of about 0.99 for GPT-3.5, 0.91 for GPT-4, below 0.8 for the current models, and about 0.76 for the model that began resolving open math problems. He expects more transformative capabilities at about 0.68 to 0.7 and says AI could speed up pretraining research as soon as 2027. He proposes that labs agree on shared perplexity-based limits they won't cross until AI monitoring improves.

Daily Stoic reading · Accept small losses calmly

Begin therefore with little things. Is a little oil spilled or a little wine stolen? Say to yourself, “This is the price paid for peace and tranquillity; and nothing is to be had for nothing.” And when you call your servant, consider that it is possible he may not come at your call; or, if he does, that he may not do what you wish. But it is not at all desirable for him, and very undesirable for you, that it should be in his power to cause you any disturbance.

— Epictetus, Enchiridion

Small losses like spilled oil or stolen wine are a fair price for a calm mind, and nothing good comes free. Do not hand other people the power to upset you by expecting every servant or small event to go your way, since that dependence is what actually costs you peace. For a poker player, this means treating small bad outcomes, like a bad beat on a low-stakes hand or a dealer or opponent doing something irritating, as ordinary costs of playing, so they do not leak into the next decision.

Mental model · Psychology

Deprival Super-Reaction

Losing $100 feels worse than finding $100 feels good — by about 2x, per Kahneman's research. Munger calls this 'deprival super-reaction.' It explains stock-market panics, breakup grief, and why people fight harder to keep what they have than to acquire what they don't. Knowing this, you can price losses more rationally.
