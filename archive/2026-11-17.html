✚Health & Sleep

Peter Attia

Mitochondria expert: 'dysfunction' is overused, and most mitochondrial supplements are unproven

Nav Chandel tells Peter Attia that mitochondria do more than make ATP. They also supply building blocks for lipids, nucleotides and amino acids, and they send signals through hydrogen peroxide, metabolites and mitochondrial DNA and RNA. He doubts that normal aging makes the electron transport chain leak toxic amounts of H2O2, since cells have strong antioxidant systems. Selenium, which supports the enzyme GPX4, is the key one, and he says a normal diet usually provides enough. The episode also covers metformin and why most cancer trials of it have failed, and it rates supplements including urolithin A, alpha-ketoglutarate, NAD precursors, methylene blue, hyperbaric oxygen and red light.

  • Vitamin C matters mainly as a cofactor for epigenetic enzymes; he says about two oranges a day is enough
  • Selenium-poor soils (northern New Zealand, Sweden, Finland) are where deficiency is a concern; the US diet is high in selenium

Slow breathing at about six breaths per minute shifts the nervous system toward calm and may lower blood pressure

Nick Norwitz argues that the vagus nerve acts as the heart's brake and has an anti-inflammatory reflex. He cites a review linking anger outbursts to nearly fivefold heart attack risk in the following hours, and a trial of 280 adults in which eight minutes of angry recall impaired blood vessel dilation for 40 minutes. In a 53-person trial, non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation cut AFib burden by 85% and lowered TNF-alpha by 23%. Breathing at six breaths per minute lowered blood pressure by 8 mmHg in 20 hypertensive people. Cyclic sighing for five minutes a day beat mindfulness for mood in a Stanford trial. The evidence is small-sample and he presents it enthusiastically.

  • Target about 6 breaths per minute with longer exhales
  • Cyclic sighing: double inhale through the nose, long slow exhale through the mouth

Oats lower cholesterol only under certain conditions: high LDL, high-molecular-weight beta glucan, enough dose

A pooled analysis finds oats reduce cardiovascular risk, but the beta glucan fiber studies are inconsistent. Studies in people with normal cholesterol showed no effect. Low-molecular-weight beta glucan did not work even at higher doses, probably because it is less viscous. About 3 g or more of high-molecular-weight beta glucan tends to lower cholesterol; a 3 g dose of medium-weight beta glucan showed no effect, while 4 g did. Roughly 75 g of dry oats provides about 3 g of beta glucan. Molecular weight cannot be known from the label, so population and dose are what you can control.

  • Benefit is mainly for people with elevated cholesterol
  • About 75 g dry oats per day gives about 3 g beta glucan

▶ Renaissance Periodization

Renaissance Periodization ranks longevity fads: morning salt water is negative, red light and grounding are unsupported

The RP team rates popular longevity hacks on a 0-10 scale. Morning salt water scores negative one because it likely raises blood pressure in a population already high in sodium. Red light masks and grounding mats get question marks because there is no link to longevity. Greens drinks get one point, and only if your diet is already good. Sauna is the most credible: strong Finnish observational data, but randomized trials show smaller effects, mainly lower blood pressure. Their practical sauna guidance is three to five sessions a week of 10 to 20 minutes, as an addition to exercise.

  • Red light does not reach deep organs; claims of recharging all mitochondria are nonsense
  • Sauna mortality reductions of around 40% are likely confounded by lifestyle

Daily Stoic reading · Keep your attention on the ship

As in a voyage, when the ship is at anchor, if you go on shore to get water, you may amuse yourself with picking up a shellfish or a truffle in your way, but your thoughts ought to be bent toward the ship, and perpetually attentive, lest the captain should call, and then you must leave all these things, that you may not have to be carried on board the vessel, bound like a sheep; thus likewise in life, if, instead of a truffle or shellfish, such a thing as a wife or a child be granted you, there is no objection; but if the captain calls, run to the ship, leave all these things, and never look behind. But if you are old, never go far from the ship, lest you should be missing when called for.

— Epictetus, Enchiridion

Enjoy the small pleasures of the shore, such as a shellfish or a truffle, or in life a spouse or child, but keep part of your mind on the ship so that when the captain calls you can drop everything and go without looking back. For a poker player, this means enjoying the session and the side pleasures while staying ready to exit or change plans the moment the situation or your bankroll signals it, and recognizing that the older or more tied down you are, the less distance you should stray from the exit.

Mental model · Psychology

Reciprocity Tendency

When someone does you a small favor, you feel pressure to return it — often with interest. Hare Krishnas used this by giving flowers at airports before asking for donations. The cost of a flower vs. the guilt of not reciprocating was wildly asymmetric. Aware of this tendency, you can watch for it in negotiations, sales pitches, and favors-exchanged-for-favors politics.
